# Research review — exact conservation laws on a lattice, and how coupled matter↔gauge systems are built to have them

*2026-09-16 - 11:10 · reference summary · companion to `docs/audits/2026-09-16-photon-fermion-momentum-investigation.md` · scope: what the external literature establishes about making charge, energy and momentum conservation **exact at finite lattice spacing**, and which of it applies to the F384–F390 chain*

**Why this review exists.** F389 §4 concluded that casim's photon↔fermion loop cannot conserve momentum exactly because its two channels are separately-derived numerical prescriptions bridged together rather than two variations of one gauge-invariant lattice action, and stated that deriving both from one action "is a strictly bigger undertaking … not attempted here." This review asks what is already known about that undertaking. The short answer is that it is the standard construction in four separate literatures, three of which the repo already partly implements.

## 1. Lattice gauge theory — exact gauge invariance at finite spacing

**The result.** In Wilson's formulation the gauge field is not a vector potential sampled on sites but a set of **compact link variables** `U_{x,μ} = exp(i q a A_μ(x))` living on the bonds. The action is built from closed loops of these (plaquettes), and it is **exactly invariant** under local phase rotations

$$\psi_x \to e^{i\theta_x}\psi_x, \qquad U_{x,\mu} \to e^{i\theta_x} U_{x,\mu} e^{-i\theta_{x+\hat\mu}}$$

at any lattice spacing, not only as `a → 0`. The associated lattice Ward–Takahashi identity **is** the discrete continuity equation, and the current appearing in it is `∂S/∂A_{x,\mu}` — read off from the action, never constructed.

**Why it matters here.** F384 defines the current the other way round: it treats continuity as a linear constraint and solves it for the minimal (purely `Ĉ`-longitudinal) solution, which is why its own caveats say "the transverse part is free, unconstrained gauge freedom." F389 then has to *choose* a transverse field to add, and discloses that choice as non-unique. In the Wilson construction neither step exists: there is one current, it is not free in any sector, and continuity is a theorem rather than an input. F388 §2's "this current cannot radiate" is a consequence of the F384 framing, not of lattice physics.

**Status:** [verified] — standard and textbook. Overviews: [Lattice gauge theories — Scholarpedia](http://www.scholarpedia.org/article/Lattice_gauge_theories); [Informal Lectures on Lattice Gauge Theory, G. D. Moore](https://theorie.ikp.physik.tu-darmstadt.de/qcd/moore/latt_lectures.pdf); [lattice gauge theory — nLab](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/lattice+gauge+theory). Recent work on exact lattice identities vs. continuum-limit ones: [Exact Lattice Identities and Continuum-Limit Dyson–Schwinger Equations for Yang–Mills Theory](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05415) **[unverified]**.

**Hamiltonian variant.** The Kogut–Susskind formulation puts the electric field on links as the conjugate momentum to the link phase, `[θ_l, E_l] = i`, and makes the Gauss law an exact constraint generated by the gauge transformation itself — so it is preserved to machine precision rather than approximately. This is the closer analogue of casim's tick-based engine than the Euclidean path-integral version. See [A Formulation of Lattice Gauge Theories for Quantum Simulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.3085) **[unverified]**.

## 2. Discrete exterior calculus — both `d∘d = 0` identities by construction

**The result.** Fields are assigned to the cells of a complex by degree: scalars to sites (0-forms), vector potentials and electric fields to bonds (1-forms), magnetic flux to plaquettes (2-forms), charge to cells (3-forms). The exterior derivative `d` maps degree `n` to degree `n+1` and is, concretely, the **oriented sum over the boundary**. `d∘d = 0` then follows from the fact that the boundary of a boundary is empty — so **both**

- `curl(grad φ) = d_1 d_0 φ = 0`
- `div(curl A) = d_2 d_1 A = 0`

hold identically, for any complex, at any spacing. The Yee/FDTD scheme is the best-known special case; `B = dA` is literally the oriented sum of `A` around a plaquette, i.e. the **Peierls holonomy**.

**Why it matters here.** The companion audit §3 establishes that `charge_coupling.bcc_curl_symbol` satisfies the second identity vacuously (it holds for *any* vector field under a cross product) and fails the first maximally (`|C×G|/(|C||G|)`: max `1.000000`, mean `0.742`). A DEC curl cannot fail either, because neither is an approximation. Verified in this session on a 2D complex: both identities at `8.9×10⁻¹⁶`.

**The repo already has this, in 2D.** `charge_coupling.discrete_curl_z` and `discrete_div` are exactly a DEC pair — the docstring of the second says it is "chosen as the adjoint partner of `discrete_curl_z` so that `div∘curl ≡ 0`," and `solve_A_coulomb_2d` imposes the Coulomb gauge in that convention. The 3D BCC path did not extend it; it built a spectral symbol from the fermion walk's spin axis instead. Extending the existing 2D construction to a 3D BCC complex is the one genuinely new object the audit's §6.3 identifies.

**Status:** [verified] — standard. [Discrete Differential Forms for Computational Modeling, Desbrun et al.](https://geometry.caltech.edu/pubs/DKT05.pdf); [Geometric Computational Electrodynamics with Variational Integrators, Stern, Tong, Desbrun & Marsden](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4470) **[unverified, but directly on point — this is the paper that combines §2 and §3]**. BCC/FCC-specific: [Discrete exterior calculus approach for discretizing Maxwell's equations on face-centered cubic grids for FDTD](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021999118301773) **[unverified]** — the nearest published precedent for a non-cubic DEC Maxwell complex, and the most directly useful reference for step 4 of the audit's program.

## 3. Variational and multisymplectic integrators — exact discrete momentum maps

**The result.** A numerical scheme obtained as the **discrete Euler–Lagrange equations of a single discrete action** (a "variational integrator") is automatically symplectic and **preserves exactly the discrete momentum map of any symmetry of that discrete action** — exactly, for any timestep, not to the order of the method. The field-theoretic generalisation is multisymplectic. Energy is *not* exactly conserved by such schemes but is bounded without secular drift, which is the characteristic symplectic signature.

**Why it matters here.** This is the precise technical content of F389 §4. Casim's loop is an **operator-splitting** scheme: the field free-rotates by its own propagator, is then sourced, and matter then steps on the published `A`. Each factor may individually be unitary or symplectic, but the composite is not the discrete Euler–Lagrange equation of any functional — so there is no action whose symmetry could produce a conservation law, and none is produced. F389's measured `O(g)` vs `O(g²)` order mismatch is the visible symptom: two prescriptions derived independently have no reason to match orders, while two variations of one functional cannot fail to.

Relevant caveat, and it is important: the theorem delivers exact conservation for the momentum map of a **continuous** symmetry. Discrete lattice translation is not one. See §5.

**Status:** [verified] — standard. [Discrete Mechanics and Variational Integrators, Marsden & West](https://www.cds.caltech.edu/~marsden/bib/2001/09-MaWe2001/MaWe2001.pdf); [An Overview of Variational Integrators, Lew, Marsden, Ortiz & West](https://lagrange.mechse.illinois.edu/pubs/LeMaOrWe2004/LeMaOrWe2004.pdf); [Multisymplectic Geometry, Variational Integrators, and Nonlinear PDEs, Marsden, Patrick & Shkoller](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s002200050505); [A Tutorial on Variational Integrators, Webb](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.5090) — all **[unverified]** individually, but the result is textbook.

**Discrete-spacetime-specific treatments** (closer to a CA than to a PDE solver, and worth a proper read before step 4): [Discrete field theory: symmetries and conservation laws](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.04788) **[unverified]**; [Symmetries and conservation laws in discrete spacetime](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02119) **[unverified]**; [Symmetry-preserving discretization of variational field theories](https://arxiv.org/html/1509.08750) **[unverified]**.

## 4. Geometric particle-in-cell — the same problem, solved, in a neighbouring field

**The result.** Plasma simulation faces casim's exact problem: charged matter coupled to a lattice EM field, where naive schemes conserve neither charge nor energy and drift secularly. The modern answer is to derive particle *and* field updates from one discrete variational principle on a DEC complex, giving exact discrete charge conservation and either exact energy conservation or bounded (non-secular) energy error, by construction rather than by tuning.

**Why it matters here.** This is the closest engineering precedent for what the audit's §6 proposes, with a decade of practical experience about which discretisation choices actually work. Notably, the charge-conservation result in this literature is *geometric*: it follows from the current being the correct cochain-valued object, not from any smoothing or correction step — the same statement as §1 and §2 from a different direction. F389 §4's disclosed, ungated observation that field energy "keeps growing without an obvious saturation" over 300 ticks is exactly the secular-drift signature this literature exists to eliminate.

**Status:** [unverified] — located, not read. [The geometric theory of charge conservation in particle-in-cell simulations](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/336869299_The_geometric_theory_of_charge_conservation_in_particle-in-cell_simulations); [Geometric Integration of the Vlasov–Maxwell System with a Variational Particle-in-Cell Scheme](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254996133_Geometric_Integration_Of_The_Vlasov-Maxwell_System_With_A_Variational_Particle-in-cell_Scheme); [A semi-implicit, energy- and charge-conserving PIC algorithm for the relativistic Vlasov–Maxwell equations](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.01565).

## 5. Crystal momentum — why the roadmap's claim 1 is not achievable as written

**The result.** A lattice breaks continuous translation invariance down to a **discrete** subgroup. Noether's theorem requires a continuous symmetry, so there is no exactly conserved momentum in the continuum sense. What survives is **crystal (quasi-)momentum**, conserved **modulo reciprocal lattice vectors** — the lattice absorbs the difference. Processes where the total wavevector leaves the first Brillouin zone and is folded back are Umklapp processes, and they are genuine momentum non-conservation from the continuum point of view.

**Why it matters here.** This is the most consequential conceptual finding of the whole investigation. The roadmap's Stage 5 claim 1 is `ΔP_matter + ΔP_field ≈ 0` with `P` measured as `Σ_k k|ψ̃(k)|²` plus `Σ_x E×B` — a continuum-style functional. **No scheme, however derived, makes that exactly conserved on a lattice.** F389's and F390's residuals were never going to close, and the reason is prior to any question about how the two channels were derived.

The functional `Σ_k k|ψ̃(k)|²` is additionally not even the right representative of crystal momentum: it assigns each mode the `k` of its first-BZ representative, so an Umklapp event makes it jump discontinuously by a reciprocal lattice vector. A conserved observable has to be either the full quasi-momentum distribution or a branch-cut-free representative.

The audit's §6.2 draws the consequence: restate the target as (A) exact charge conservation, (B) exact crystal-momentum conservation via `Φ∘T = T∘Φ`, (C) matched-order approach to the continuum. (C) is what F389 §4 was really diagnosing, and it is the one the single-action route fixes.

**Status:** [verified] — standard solid-state physics. [Crystal momentum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_momentum); [Umklapp scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umklapp_scattering); [Reciprocal lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocal_lattice).

## 6. Quantum cellular automata with gauge fields — the nearest neighbours to casim

**The result.** There is a small but directly relevant literature building QCA/quantum-walk models with **dynamical** gauge fields and exact gauge invariance — i.e. precisely casim's problem, in casim's formalism rather than in a PDE solver's. The Schwinger-model and 1D-QED automata are the closest published relatives of what the F384–F390 chain is trying to build.

**Why it matters here.** These papers should inform the shape of `S_matter` in the audit's §6.1, and they carry the accumulated knowledge about which walk/gauge pairings actually close. The staggered construction in particular is the standard lattice answer to the doubling problem that §7 below raises.

## 7. The two obstructions to know before committing

### 7.1 Nielsen–Ninomiya

**The result.** No lattice Hamiltonian or action can carry a **single** Weyl fermion with locality, hermiticity, translation invariance and exact chiral gauge symmetry. Chiral modes come in cancelling pairs ("doublers"); equivalently, the net chirality summed over the Brillouin zone is zero.

**Why it matters here.** F384–F390 all declare Weyl-2-spinor-only scope, and that is exactly where the theorem bites. It is **not** an obstruction for a vector-like U(1) — ordinary electromagnetism couples both chiralities identically, and lattice QED with Wilson or staggered fermions is entirely standard. So the clean single-action route probably runs through the **Dirac** sector (`particles.dirac_bcc`), not the Weyl one. This should be settled *before* building the complex, since it changes what `S_matter` is.

Worth cross-checking against the project's own results here: F328 (discrete CPT theorem for the free BCC Dirac walk) and F378 (discrete CPT gauged kinetic theorem, mass-sector no-go) are the existing findings that bear on it, and F293/F324 on the colour side. A session taking this up should read those first.

**Status:** [verified] — textbook. [Nielsen–Ninomiya theorem](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Physics:Nielsen%E2%80%93Ninomiya_theorem); [Chiral Anomalies and the Nielsen–Ninomiya No-Go Theorem, Kadakia](https://mcgreevy.physics.ucsd.edu/s13/final-papers/2013S-215C-Kadakia-Nirag.pdf); [Lattice Chiral Fermions](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-lat/9509023).

### 7.2 Ginsparg–Wilson

**The result.** The standard escape: a lattice Dirac operator satisfying `Dγ₅ + γ₅D = aDγ₅D` has an **exact** (modified) chiral symmetry at finite lattice spacing, with a lattice index theorem. Overlap and domain-wall fermions realise it. The cost is that the operator is not ultralocal — it has exponentially decaying rather than strictly finite support.

**Why it matters here.** It is the known way to have exact chiral symmetry and exact gauge invariance simultaneously, so it bounds what is possible. The non-ultralocality is a genuine cost for a CA model whose whole premise is a strictly local update rule — worth weighing explicitly rather than discovering later. Note that casim's BCC walk is *already* non-ultralocal in a related sense: F384 §1 observes that the fractional-shift hop "spreads over the whole lattice" once Fourier-transformed onto the integer array, which is the stated reason no local bilinear can be an exact current.

**Status:** [verified] — textbook. [Lattice QCD with Ginsparg–Wilson fermions](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/hep-lat/9805015); [Analytic progress on exact lattice chiral symmetry](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-lat/0111035); [Generalized Ginsparg–Wilson relations, Phys. Rev. D 109, 014502](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevD.109.014502).

## 8. Riemann–Silberstein — the null condition

**The result.** The RS vector `F = E + iB` packages the free Maxwell field as a single complex 3-vector obeying a Schrödinger-like equation, and is the standard "photon wave function" formalism. A genuine **radiation** field is *null*:

$$F\cdot F = E^2 - B^2 + 2i\,\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |\mathbf E| = |\mathbf B| \ \text{ and } \ \mathbf E \perp \mathbf B$$

A plane wave is realised with a **complex** polarization vector, `ê = (ê₁ + iê₂)/√2`, not a real one.

**Why it matters here.** This is the exact statement of the companion audit's defect 1. `photon.build_beam_packet` builds `F = (\text{scalar envelope}) \cdot \hat e` with `ê` **real**, so `F·F = (\text{scalar})² \neq 0`: it is not a null field, `E ∥ B` pointwise, and `E×B ≡ 0` identically. Since the model's photon is *defined* (decision 5, F67–F69) as a De Broglie composite riding the RS analytic field, the null condition is the model's own consistency condition, not an imported one — and it is the derivation that fixes the helper. Verified in this session: with the complex circular polarization, `E·B/(|E||B|)` drops to `10⁻¹⁷` or below, `|Σ E×B| = 75.126`, and `cos(P, k̂) = +1.000000` on all three axes at both carrier modes.

**Status:** [verified] — standard, and independently reproduced numerically this session. [The Riemann–Silberstein vector as a photon wave function, Białynicki-Birula](https://indico.cern.ch/event/423687/contributions/1040123/subcontributions/88199/attachments/896013/1262445/Bialynicki-BirulaI_2005_received02.09.pdf); [Electromagnetism made simpler: the Riemann–Silberstein vector](https://arxiv.org/html/1211.2655v1); [Riemann–Silberstein vector](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Physics:Riemann%E2%80%93Silberstein_vector).

The repo's `references/mohr-2010-maxwell-photon-wf-summary.md` covers adjacent ground; the null condition specifically does not appear to be recorded anywhere in the tree, and it should be, because it is a one-line test any photon construction in the model can be checked against.

## 9. What the literature says the program should be

Synthesising §§1–8, in the order the companion audit's §8 recommends:

1. **The null condition (§8) is a free win** — a one-line fix to one test helper, and a one-line gate leg (`F·F = 0`, or equivalently `E·B ≈ 0` and `|E| = |B|`) that every photon construction in the model can be checked against forever after.
2. **The DEC complex (§2) is the structural fix** and has a direct published precedent for non-cubic grids. It makes both `d∘d` identities exact and removes defect 2 at the root.
3. **The current as `∂S/∂A` (§1) removes an entire problem class** — F384's free transverse sector, F388's inability to radiate, F389's disclosed constitutive choice. Verified in this session on a toy: continuity to `3.9×10⁻¹⁶` as a *consequence*, with a `0.74` transverse fraction against casim's `2×10⁻¹⁶`.
4. **The variational integrator (§3) fixes the order mismatch** F389 §4 measured, and eliminates the secular energy growth F389 also observed.
5. **Crystal momentum (§5) tells you what to measure.** This should be settled before (2)–(4), because it determines what success looks like — and as written, the current claim 1 has no success state.
6. **Nielsen–Ninomiya (§7.1) determines whether the matter sector is Weyl or Dirac**, which changes what `S_matter` is, so it should be settled before the complex is built.

The honest summary is that F389 §4 was right that this is "a strictly bigger undertaking," and also that it is a *well-charted* one — but that the first two steps on the list are small, and the fifth reframes the goal in a way that makes the rest worth doing.
