## Verdict

The finding's central claims all independently reproduce exactly, including the negative control. The two identities it distinguishes really are logically independent — `div(curl A)=0` is a vacuous scalar-triple-product identity for *any* vector field, while `curl(grad φ)=0` genuinely constrains `C` to be parallel to the gradient symbol — and I derived this distinction myself before reading the finding, its module, or its test. Re-running `check_curl_grad_identity_diagnostic` from a clean environment reproduced `0.7416917489634725` / `0.7071725769232295` bit-for-bit, and the declared control (`curl_symbol="true"`) reddened exactly the 5 of 7 legs claimed, with the exact `0.509`/`1.0`/`0.0` numbers described. No attack broke the claim as stated. Two soft spots were found and are already disclosed by the finding itself (2 of 7 legs are self-referential frozen baselines, not independent targets); one **new, previously unflagged, independently reproduced defect** was found in the *documented reproduction path* for the control (see Attacks 7 and Recommendations) — it does not affect the substantive claim, since the actual control-verification machinery the gate tier relies on works correctly.

## Independent re-derivation

Before reading the finding, module, test, or registry record, I reasoned from the claim card alone:

- `div(curl A)=0` reduces, once curl is modeled as `A ↦ C(k)×A(k)` with a single fixed generator symbol `C(k)` reused for divergence, to the scalar triple product `C·(C×A)`, which is algebraically zero for *any* real vector `C` and *any* `A` — a property of the cross product, carrying no information about whether `C` is a genuine curl generator. This matches the finding's framing exactly.
- `curl(grad φ)=0` reduces to `C(k)×G(k)·φ(k) = 0`, which is a real constraint requiring `C ∥ G` at every `k` — this is NOT automatically satisfied unless `C` is built from the same generator as `G` (in the continuum, both are `ik`, so it's trivial there; on a lattice, if `C` comes from a structurally different construction than the gradient, nothing forces parallelism).
- I judged a mean sine-residual of ~0.74 plausible (not a suspicious "too clean" or "too round" number) for a curl symbol built from a different discretization than the gradient it's compared against.
- I judged the exact on-axis fact (`+1` on x/z, `-1` on y, at *every* mode, not just small-`k`) consistent with — and in fact suggestive of — a real, structural sign convention in the underlying construction (an axis-dependent reflection baked into the generator), rather than numerical noise, precisely because it was reported as holding exactly rather than asymptotically.

This is "independent" only in the qualitative/structural sense (a route to the *classification* of the two identities, not a route to the specific numeric magnitude, which is inherently an empirical fact about a specific piece of code rather than something derivable from first principles absent the code). After reading the finding, I additionally reproduced the numeric claim directly rather than trusting it:

```
measured (this run):  spectral_mean=0.7416917489634725  forward_diff_mean=0.7071725769232295
finding claims:                    0.741692                          0.707173
```

Bit-for-bit match to the finding's rounded values. Running the declared control (`curl_symbol="true"`, via direct Python call and via `casim test --control --id F393-curl-grad-identity-diagnostic`) gave `n_pass=2` (5 of 7 legs red), with `spectral_mean=0.0`, `forward_diff_mean=0.509...`, x/z on-axis still `+1`, y on-axis flipping to `+1` (no longer `-1`) — exactly as described in the finding's §3 caveat and the registry's `control.reason`. I also found that `tools/verify_photon_momentum_diagnosis.py::defect_2()` is a separately-written implementation (its own `xn()` cross-product helper, its own k-grid call) that predates and matches the module function — a real cross-check against transcription error, but not an independent theoretical derivation, since both ultimately call the same `bcc_curl_symbol`.

## What would falsify this

For the finding as scoped ("Claim: none," test-infrastructure only): a future run of `check_curl_grad_identity_diagnostic` producing a `spectral_mean`/`forward_diff_mean` outside `±1e-4` of the recorded values, or an on-axis dot product deviating from `±1` by more than `1e-9`, would flip the gate red — and per the finding's own §6 caveat, that is explicitly the *expected and desired* outcome if `bcc_curl_symbol` is ever repaired or replaced (Part C, deferred to F394). ## Recommendations

Either make `parse_param` support an escape for a literal string that happens to spell `true`/`false` (e.g. quoted-string passthrough), or change `check_curl_grad_identity_diagnostic`'s `curl_symbol` parameter to accept a Python `bool` as an alias for `"true"`/`"bcc"`, or simply correct the test driver's docstring and this finding's own citation of the reproduction command to say `casim test --control --id F393-curl-grad-identity-diagnostic` instead of the broken `--param curl_symbol=true` form. This is a real, independently reproduced defect (`ValueError`) in a documented command, even though the gate's actual control machinery is unaffected.
2. Consider whether the two `_mean` legs (the only two of the seven that are pure frozen-baseline numbers with no independent target) belong under `kind: result_dump` rather than `kind: assertion`, for taxonomy consistency with D9 — or, if `assertion` is deliberately correct here (no external JSON to arm/restore), add a one-line note to the registry record's `notes:` field flagging that 2 of 7 legs are baseline-only, to make the distinction machine-visible and not just present in the finding's prose.
