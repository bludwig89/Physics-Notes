## Independent re-derivation

**Claim 1 — the blind agent confirmed the formula and refuted the sub-claim.** Working from a stated Condon–Shortley convention with no $l$-dependent phase, it derived $\hat r\cdot Y^m_{j,j-1}=+\sqrt{j/(2j+1)}Y^m_j$, $\hat r\cdot Y^m_{j,j}=0$, $\hat r\cdot Y^m_{j,j+1}=-\sqrt{(j+1)/(2j+1)}Y^m_j$ (coefficients measured to $10^{-13}$ spread over random points), so F18's $Y_E$ is exactly transverse — residuals $2\times10^{-17}$…$1.4\times10^{-16}$ for $j=1,2$, all $m$. It then fetched arXiv:0910.1874 and established this is **Mohr's own convention** (his Eq. 385 is the definition verbatim), with two caveats:

- **$Y_E = -X_2$ exactly**, not $+X_2$ (measured $\lvert$asserted$+X_2\rvert = 0.00\times10^{0}$). Harmless for transversality; load-bearing if helicity combinations $(X_1\pm iX_2)/\sqrt2$ are formed downstream.
- **F18's "alternate convention fails transversality by O(0.5)" is a correct number attached to a wrong diagnosis.** Under a Varshalovich-type $\tilde Y_{j,l}=i^l Y_{j,l}$ phase the *minus* variant is exactly transverse ($4.7\times10^{-17}$, $1.4\times10^{-16}$). The peak magnitude is analytically $[2\sqrt{j(j+1)}/(2j+1)]\max\lvert Y^m_j\rvert$ = 0.4607 ($j$=1), 0.6180 ($j$=2) — reproducible, but evidence of a convention mismatch, not an error in the reference.

The blind agent also flagged that in the $Y_{jl}$ basis only $Y_{jj}$'s transversality is manifest; **the mixed combination's transversality is precisely equivalent to getting the two coefficients right, so "$\hat r\cdot Y_E=0$" in that basis is a check on the coefficients and the convention, not an independent test of physics.** And a nomenclature hazard: in the waveguide/Mie taxonomy $X_1=Y_{jj}$ is the TE mode and is the *magnetic* multipole, so "transverse electric" collides with standard TE; separately, the E-field of an electric multipole is not purely transverse in the near field (Jackson §9.10), so the angular identity must not be read as the field-level one.

**Claim 2 — the blind agent refuted all three parts.**

## The $i^l$ phase question (the blind agent's stated blocker)

The referee settled it. `src/casim/engine/gauge/bilinear.py:1167`, `vector_spherical_harmonic`, is a bare CG sum with **no $l$-dependent phase**:

```python
    vec = np.zeros(3, dtype=complex)
    for m_l in range(-l, l + 1):
        m_s = m - m_l
        if abs(m_s) > 1: continue
        cg = _clebsch_gordan(l, m_l, 1, m_s, j, m)
        if cg == 0.0: continue
        y_lm = _scalar_sph_harm(l, m_l, theta, phi)
        idx = {1: 0, 0: 1, -1: 2}[m_s]
        vec[idx] += cg * y_lm
    return vec
```

Measured against an independent hand CG sum: `max|code − hand| = 0.000e+00`, ratio $1+0i$, all $(j,l)$ with $j\le2$. So the blind agent's verdict stands unmodified, and the convention is Mohr's own (Eq. 385).

The referee then found a **second, sharper reading** of F18's "alternate convention", and it is worse for F18 than the $i^l$ story:

## Claim 3 is refuted, three independent ways

F18: *"the naive Lorentz-form conservation is violated by $\mathcal O(1)$ even at small $k$ … consistent with [[finding-14-7-qg2-planck-LV]]: the lattice has Lorentz-violation residuals of size $\beta_\text{LV}(m),\gamma_\text{LV}(m)$."*

**(i) The number is direction-dependent and changes sign.** Closed form, verified to $10^{-8}$ at $\lvert k\rvert=10^{-3}$: $(\omega-\vec k\cdot\hat n)/\omega = 1-\sqrt3(1-2\hat k_y^2)$.

| $\hat k_y$ | 0 | 0.2 | 0.4 | **0.4597** | 0.6 | 0.8 | 1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| measured | $-0.7320508$ | $-0.5934868$ | $-0.1777946$ | **$-0.0000027$** | $+0.5150258$ | $+1.4849742$ | $+2.7320508$ |

It ranges over $[-0.732,+2.732]$ and **passes exactly through zero on a cone at $\hat k_y=0.4597$, at all $\lvert k\rvert$**. A genuine LV residual cannot do that. (The blind agent's $1-\sqrt3$ is the $\hat k_y=0$ special case; its derivation assumed $\hat n=\hat k$, true only in the $xz$-plane.)

**(ii) The cited source says the opposite.** `findings/F01-F15-findings.md:2351`: *"$\beta_\text{LV}(m)$… vanishes as $m\to0$. **The Weyl sector is exactly Lorentz-invariant at this order**; only the Dirac sector picks up the deformation, suppressed by $m^2$."* F18's current is `mass=0.0` — massless Weyl. Inventory Tier 1 #47 independently records "Massless limit $m\to0$: $\delta u'=0$ — SR velocity addition recovered exactly, **0** (sympy)". And $\beta_\text{LV}/\gamma_\text{LV}$ are **2D-square** coefficients (inventory #20/#21 say so), not BCC.

**(iii) The real residual was sitting in the dispersion F18 examined.** $\lVert\nabla_k\omega\rVert = 0.5774558$ at $\lvert k\rvert=10^{-3}$ vs $c_\text{lat}=0.5773503$. The BCC branch dispersion carries an exact anisotropic quadratic term
$$\omega = \frac{\lvert k\rvert}{\sqrt3} - \frac{\hat k_x\hat k_y\hat k_z}{3}\lvert k\rvert^2 + O(k^3)$$
with the fitted coefficient matching $-\hat k_x\hat k_y\hat k_z/3$ and **flipping sign between the $\pm$ branches** ($\mp0.0641500 = \mp\sqrt3/27$ on the diagonal; exactly 0 on the coordinate planes). That is the already-recorded **F65–F67 branch-split birefringence** $d\Omega=-\sqrt3/27\,k^2$ — the quantity that later retired this photon. F18 reported an $O(1)$ units artifact instead.
