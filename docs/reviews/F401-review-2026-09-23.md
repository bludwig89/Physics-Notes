## Verdict

The finding's core repair (an additive `threshold="closed"` option on `critical_coupling`/`threshold_wavefunction`, default unchanged) and its exact axis identity both hold exactly as stated. Two claims in the original draft overclaimed relative to what a wider check shows and are narrowed here: "clean, monotonic convergence... at every step" was true only at F169's own 7 quoted $L$ values, not at a denser scan (a real commensurability dip persists, e.g. at $L=92$) — narrowed to a quantified ~4x reduction in worst-case severity, not elimination; and "along a generic direction" implied more generality than checked — $(2,1,0)$ is a genuine counterexample to the decreasing-$g_c(k)$ trend, so the result is now scoped to $(111)$/$(3,1,1)$ specifically. Both narrowings are applied in this session to the finding, its module, its test, and its claim card.

The claim card deliberately left "a generic direction" unspecified (not naming (111)) to see whether the blind reviewer would treat any one direction as representative — it did not, and used the literal grid convention from the card's own formula, which turned out to match the module's own `_grid` function exactly.

## Independent re-derivation

Subagent A, given only the claim card, derived the axis identity symbolically from the Eq. 15/21 dispersion formula (the sign-dependent cross term vanishes identically when two of three momentum components are zero) and verified it numerically to machine precision by calling the engine's own `bcc_dispersion` directly — a **different route** (direct engine calls plus an independent symbolic check) reaching the identical closed form. For the two-body threshold, it found the true continuum floor via **global numerical optimization** (coarse scan + Nelder–Mead, cross-validated against `scipy.optimize.differential_evolution` to $\sim10^{-9}$) rather than the finding's own closed form `min(w+(k),w-(k))` — a genuinely different, independent route, and it matched the closed form to $\sim10^{-9}$. It reproduced the qualitative story exactly: the grid-minimum threshold is non-monotonic in $L$ at $|k|=0.2$ along $(111)$; the true-floor-anchored $g_c(k,L)$ converges markedly more cleanly; and $g_c(k)$ decreases monotonically along $(111)$. It flagged one real ambiguity: the grid-box convention (the literal $[0,2\pi)^3$ box versus the dispersion's own natural period $2\pi\sqrt3$) changes the *specific shape* of the non-monotonicity (though not the qualitative fact of it) — traced by the referee (attack 2) to a pre-existing convention in `photon_bound_state.py`'s own `_grid` function, unchanged and not introduced by F401.
