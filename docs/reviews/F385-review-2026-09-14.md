## Verdict

The core claims survive fully independent scrutiny: `A≡0` reduces exactly for all three forks, the spatially-uniform-`A` case is exactly unitary (a rigid momentum-argument shift), the global Ward identity is exact, norm drift for fork (a) is genuinely linear in `|qA|`, and fork (b)'s Strang-split genuinely does not change that power (only the coefficient). But two headline claims were overstated and one number was arithmetically wrong. Fork (d)'s "comparable order of magnitude to fork (a)" claim, as originally written, is **false in general** — the referee found the ratio grows unboundedly (up to 200×) for broader fields, true only for localised ones. The "~35%" Strang-split coefficient reduction is actually ~31%. Both fixed in this pass, along with a genuinely valuable addition the review surfaced: an already-existing, already-tested mechanism in this codebase (`test_P2_4_bloch_acceleration_exact_force_law`) that exactly solves the uniform/gradient-field case via a completely different route, sharpening what Stage 2's new machinery is actually needed for.

## The claim, as the reviewer understands it

TARGET: given the BCC Weyl walk's exact algebraic decomposition `U(k)=Σ_d M_d·e^{ik·d/√3}` (8 fixed directions, fixed matrices), does attaching an independent, position-dependent U(1) phase to each direction's term (a per-link Peierls construction) transfer real momentum while staying exactly unitary — for a generic spatially-varying field, for a special (uniform) field, and via alternative (Strang-split, per-site-renormalised) constructions?

## Independent re-derivation

The blind subagent (working from a claim card only, explicitly forbidden from `weak_wmu.py` past line 125 and from any F385 file) independently: (1) derived the mechanistic reason generic `A(x)` breaks unitarity (a DC-suppression/Jensen's-inequality argument for the k-diagonal block, plus uncancelled off-diagonal k-mixing) — a sharper, more general derivation than the finding's own; (2) found the identical uniform-`A₀` special case and verified it to the same machine-precision level; (3) measured a norm-drift log-log slope of `0.981` (vs the finding's `0.996` — same conclusion, independent field configuration); (4) confirmed the free step exactly conserves `⟨k⟩` and the coupled step doesn't, with direction-reversal and amplitude-linearity sanity checks the finding itself didn't run; (5) independently tried and rejected a midpoint-symmetrised (Strang-split-equivalent) construction, finding slope `0.993` — essentially unchanged, matching the finding's own negative result on fork (b) almost exactly; (6) found, unprompted, a distinct exactly-unitary construction for the curl-free part of `A` (a single global phase on the free-step output) — which the referee traced to an existing, already-tested mechanism in this codebase (Bloch acceleration, §5b of the revised finding).

## What would falsify this

- The masked/machine-precision legs (A≡0 reduction, uniform-A unitarity, global Ward, fork-d exact conservation) failing to hold to `<1e-10` for some field configuration would indicate a genuine construction bug.
- Fork (a)'s norm-drift slope moving outside `[0.5,1.5]` would refute the `O(|qA|·a)` claim (robust across `L∈{8,16,24,32}`, multiple seeds, per the referee's sweep).
