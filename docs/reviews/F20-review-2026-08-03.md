## Verdict

The simulation is correct and reproduces bit-for-bit at 15 significant figures, but almost
everything built on top of it is overstated. The headline "0.37% agreement with $c_\text{lat}$"
is a $+1.4\%$ torus-wrap bias in the arithmetic centroid partially cancelling a real $-1.74\%$
physical deficit; run wrap-free the same physics gives $0.5673401$, which matches the *correct*
analytic prediction $c_\text{lat}\langle \hat n_x^2\rangle = 0.5673395$ to $1.1\times10^{-6}$.
F20's stated mechanism for the deficit is wrong by exactly a factor of two. Its two
"machine-precision photon gates" are identities computed on an identically-zero field, and its
composite photon is the σ-bilinear object that F69/F302 retired — with no supersession banner, no
ledger entry and no test record.

**Corrected claim, one sentence:** Weyl and Dirac wavepackets propagate across the BCC lattice at
the group velocity the automaton's own dispersion predicts — $c_\text{lat}\langle\hat n_x^2\rangle
= 0.5673395$, measured to $1.1\times10^{-6}$ for the massless case once the packet is run
wrap-free, which is $1.74\%$ below $c_\text{lat}$ because of finite-aperture tilt *plus*
fixed-spinor branch admixture — while the "composite photon" leg tracks a non-conserved,
non-SO(3)-covariant, purely longitudinal bilinear of a photon construction the model has since
retired, and its velocity is a fit-window number.

## Independent re-derivation

Subagent A read only the engine (`lattice/bcc.py`, `particles/dirac_bcc.py`, `gauge/bilinear.py`)
and derived from the update rules. It confirmed it read no forbidden file.

**It found a stronger, exact result than the finding claims.** On a coordinate axis
$c_y=c_z=1,\ s_y=s_z=0$, so $u=\cos(k_x/\sqrt3)$ identically and

$$\omega(k\hat x) = \arccos\bigl(\cos(k_x/\sqrt3)\bigr) = k_x/\sqrt3$$

is an **algebraic identity**, not a small-$k$ limit — verified to $7.4\times10^{-15}$ over 500
points on $(0,\pi\sqrt3)$, both branches, with $\omega''\equiv0$. It then obtained the closed forms

$$\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial k_x} = c_\text{lat}\,\hat n_x(\mathbf k), \qquad
\frac{\partial\omega_\text{Dirac}}{\partial k_x} = \frac{c_\text{lat}\,n_\text{kin}\,n_x}{\sqrt{1-n_\text{kin}^2u^2}},\quad n_\text{kin}=\sqrt{1-m^2}$$

both verified against central differences at 200 random $\mathbf k$ (residual $2$–$5\times10^{-10}$,
the $h^2$ floor). And $\Omega(K)=2\omega(K/2)\Rightarrow d\Omega/dK = c_\text{lat}$ exactly on axis.

**Two finite-width corrections the finding does not have.** (i) an aperture/tilt term
$v_x(\mathbf k) = v_x(\mathbf k_0)[1-(k_y^2+k_z^2)/6W^2]$, $W^2=1-n_\text{kin}^2\cos^2 a$; and
(ii) **branch admixture** — seeding a *fixed* spinor $\chi_+(\mathbf k_0)$ is not an eigenspinor at
$\mathbf k\neq\mathbf k_0$, so weight $(1\mp\hat n_x)/2$ lands on the opposite branch moving at
$-v_x$. Therefore

$$\langle d\bar x/dt\rangle = c_\text{lat}\langle\hat n_x^2\rangle \quad\text{not}\quad c_\text{lat}\langle\hat n_x\rangle,$$

i.e. the tilt deficit **exactly doubles**. F20's Interpretation §3 states mechanism (i) alone.

### Two defects found outside the attack list

- **The tracked photon density is not conserved, and F20 says it is.** Interpretation §2 asserts the
  photon "conserves Poynting energy during the motion." Measured over F20's own 44 ticks,
  $\sum_i|G^i(x)|^2$ falls **1.000 → 0.6279** (identical to 7 digits at $L_x=64,96,128,192,256$, so
  it is physics, not a box effect) and → 0.2889 by $t=100$. The conservation claim comes from a
  *different object* at a *different momentum* ($k=0.05$, random directions) evolved by a
  hand-applied scalar phase with no lattice step. Fitting a line to the centroid of a density that
  has lost 37% of its weight is not an Ehrenfest group velocity.

  0.566470; $72$–$100$: 0.568654. Monotone and still climbing toward $c_\text{lat}$.

## What would falsify this

Nothing currently. That is the review's finding, not an omission of it. The claim as written is a
percent-level internal self-consistency demonstration with no external anchor and no test record
that can fail.

Three thresholds are *available* and would make it falsifiable:

1. $\omega(k\hat x) - k_x/\sqrt3 \neq 0$ at tolerance $0$ for any $k_x\in(0,\pi\sqrt3)$ would kill
   the exact on-axis identity.
2. A wrap-free Weyl centroid velocity differing from $c_\text{lat}\langle\hat n_x^2\rangle$ by more
   than $10^{-5}$ relative would kill the corrected quantitative claim.
3. The Dirac leg differing from $\langle(2w_+-1)\,\partial\omega/\partial k_x\rangle$ by more than
   $10^{-4}$ relative would kill the massive case.

The three judgements the referee was least sure of:

1. **The box-scan sign.** B's arithmetic-centroid scan trends *up* with $L_x$ (0.5752 → 0.5909);
   A's phasor-centroid scan trends *down* (0.574 → 0.569). Both converge on $\approx0.5673$

   intermediate trend is estimator-specific and should not be quoted as physics.
2. **Whether attack 4 is FAIL or WEAKENS.** For *generic* $\mathbf k$ directions
   $\lVert G_T\rVert/\lVert G\rVert$ is $O(1)$ (0.06–0.95 across the test's own random directions),
   so the gate is not degenerate everywhere — it is degenerate *at F20's own momentum*, and
   projection-enforced everywhere. Graded FAIL on the projection argument, which holds regardless.
3. **The novelty grade.** Paper 4's drift/diffusion result is recorded in the repo's own reference
   summary, but arXiv:1601.04842 was not read directly to confirm it treats the 3D BCC packet
   rather than the 1D case. If it is 1D-only, the 3D aperture correction may be genuinely new —
   which would *raise* the value of recommendation 1.
