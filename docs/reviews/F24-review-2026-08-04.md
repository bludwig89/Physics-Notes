## Verdict

The identity is true, exactly, for every spinor and every boost — the blind agent
proved it at the operator level, which is stronger than the bilinear form F24
checks. The computation reproduces bit-for-bit: `weyl_sl2c_4current_covariance()`
returns **3.707420040408194e-16** against the recorded 3.71e-16.

But it is not a derivation, it is a **convention and regression check of a textbook
identity**, and the finding's own framing overshoots that in three places.

**Narrowed claim, one sentence:** *`sl2c_boost` is implemented correctly — the map
$H\mapsto AHA^\dagger$ on rank-1 Hermitian $\psi\psi^\dagger$ reproduces the
SL(2,ℂ)→SO⁺(1,3) covering map on the null cone, which is the **definition** of that
map rather than a consequence of anything in this model; the check contains no
lattice content, uses a sign convention that is unstated and which, read against the
standard active boost, yields $\Lambda(-\zeta)$; and its quoted residual is a benign
12-draw sample, not a floor.*

## The claim, as the reviewer understands it

```
TARGET   Does the SL(2,C) pure boost A = cosh(z/2) I - sinh(z/2)(sigma.v_hat)
         induce the SL(2,C) -> SO(1,3) homomorphism on the Weyl 4-current
         j^mu = psi^dag sigma_bar^mu psi, i.e. is j'^mu = Lambda^mu_nu j^nu?
VALUE    yes, exactly; max relative residual 3.71e-16 over 12 random (k_hat, v_hat)
         pairs at |k| = 0.3, v/c = 0.6, using BCC eigenmodes.
INPUTS   none beyond the definitions.
CLASS    MACHINE precision.
```

Not stated, and load-bearing: **which sign convention $\Lambda$ carries.** F24 writes
$\bar\sigma^0 = I_2$, $\bar\sigma^i = \sigma^i$ and $A = \cosh(\zeta/2)I - \sinh(\zeta/2)(\sigma\cdot\hat v)$,
then says "$\Lambda$ is the 4×4 Lorentz boost matrix with rapidity $\zeta$ along
$\hat v$". Measured in this session over 400 random draws:

| $\bar\sigma^\mu$ | $A$ | vs $\Lambda(+\zeta)$ | vs $\Lambda(-\zeta)$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $(I,+\sigma)$ | $\cosh - \sinh(\sigma\cdot\hat v)$ | **4.963e+01** | **2.090e-15** |

So with the standard *active* boost, F24's pairing gives the **inverse** boost. The
finding is internally consistent — its `## Key identity` section, $j'^z = \cosh\zeta\,j^z - \sinh\zeta\,j^0$,
carries the matching sign — but a reader implementing from the `## Statement` gets a
relative error of 49. The blind agent predicted this from the algebra before seeing
any code.

## Independent re-derivation

Subagent A never opened `bilinear.py` and confirmed no forbidden file was read.

**It proved the identity at the operator level**, which is stronger and cheaper than
the bilinear check: with $\hat n\cdot\hat n = 1$ imposed,

$$A^\dagger\,\bar\sigma^\mu A = \Lambda^\mu{}_\nu(\hat v,\zeta)\,\bar\sigma^\nu
\qquad\text{as }2\times2\text{ matrices,}$$

so $j'^\mu = \psi^\dagger A^\dagger\bar\sigma^\mu A\psi = \Lambda^\mu{}_\nu j^\nu$
for **every** $\psi$ and every $(\hat v,\zeta)$ — no conditions, no restriction to
normalised or special spinors. One-line proof: $A$ is Hermitian with
$A^2 = \cosh\zeta\,I - \sinh\zeta(\sigma\cdot\hat n)$, so the time and $\hat n$-parallel
components mix by $\cosh/\sinh$; the perpendicular Pauli matrices anticommute with
$\sigma\cdot\hat n$, giving $A\sigma_\perp A = \sigma_\perp(\cosh^2-\sinh^2) = \sigma_\perp$.
That is exactly $\Lambda$.

A's convention table (sympy, exact) shows the mismatched pairings do not break
covariance — they give $\Lambda(-\zeta)$. There is **no** choice that fails to be
Lorentz; only choices that invert the boost.

**A's answer to the question that decides the grade.** $\psi^\dagger\bar\sigma^\mu\psi = \operatorname{tr}(\bar\sigma^\mu\,\psi\psi^\dagger)$,
and $\psi\psi^\dagger$ is rank-1 positive Hermitian. Under $\psi\to A\psi$ it goes to
$A(\psi\psi^\dagger)A^\dagger$ — and $H\mapsto AHA^\dagger$ on
$\mathrm{Herm}(2)\cong\mathbb R^{1,3}$ **is the definition** of the covering map. So
the claim reduces to *"the covering map, restricted to the null cone, is the covering
map."* Rank-1 ⇒ $\det = 0$ ⇒ $j$ is null, confirmed numerically
($j\cdot j/j_0^2 \le 9.1\times10^{-16}$): the test only ever probes null vectors.

A's qualification, which this review adopts: **it is not vacuous.** A Lorentz
transformation is determined by its action on the null cone, so the check does pin
$\Lambda$ uniquely, and it is a genuinely useful regression test — it would catch a
wrong exponent sign, $\zeta$ vs $\zeta/2$, a $\bar\sigma$ spatial sign mismatched to
the chirality, a transpose where a dagger belongs, a mis-assembled $\Lambda$, or an
imaginary part dropped by a numpy chiral transform (the standing hazard named in
`CLAUDE.md`). That is **code correctness, not physics**.

**On the residual.** A ran 50,000 random $(\psi,\hat v,\zeta\sim\mathcal N(0,2))$:
worst absolute 3.64e-12, worst relative **1.83e-13** at $\zeta\approx4.85$; a fixed
spinor at $\zeta = 1,5,10,20,30$ stays at 2e-16–7e-16. The limit is **conditioning,
not eps**: $j$ is null, so when the boost is anti-aligned with $\hat{\mathbf j}$,
$j'^0 = \cosh\zeta\,j^0 + \sinh\zeta(\hat n\cdot\mathbf j)$ is a near-total
cancellation and the relative error grows like $e^{2\zeta}\epsilon$. This session's
own 400-draw sweep gives worst relative **2.09e-15** against the quoted 3.71e-16.

**On the lattice.** A's answer, verbatim: *"Pure continuum 2×2 spinor algebra. No
lattice spacing $a$, no $c_\text{lat}$, no BCC geometry, no dispersion, no CA tick…
The physically load-bearing question for this project — whether the **lattice
evolution** commutes with a boost, i.e. emergent Lorentz invariance at finite $a$ —
is untouched by this identity, and would generically not be exact."* F24 draws its
$\psi$ from BCC eigenmodes, but the identity holds for any two-component complex
vector; the eigenmodes are decoration.

## What would falsify this

Nothing — and saying so is the review's finding. The statement is an algebraic
identity; no measurement can bear on it. What *can* fail, and is what the check is
actually for:

1. A sign error in `sl2c_boost`'s exponent, or $\zeta$ vs $\zeta/2$.
2. A transpose substituted for a dagger.
3. A dropped imaginary component in a chiral transform (the `CLAUDE.md` hazard).
4. A mis-assembled $\Lambda$.

Those are the thresholds this record legitimately guards, and the finding should say
that is what it is.

## Recommendations

For a future research session, under its own claim. This review does none of them.

**Verdict-changing**

1. **Restate the claim as a regression/convention test**, not a derivation. Rewrite
   `## Significance`: it verifies `sl2c_boost`, it does not close the Lorentz
   covariance loop. The loop that matters — whether the *lattice evolution* commutes
   with a boost at finite $a$ — is untouched and is the finding that should exist.
2. **State the sign convention explicitly** in `## Statement`, with the measured
   consequence: $\bar\sigma^\mu = (I,+\sigma)$ paired with
   $A = \cosh - \sinh(\sigma\cdot\hat v)$ realises $\Lambda(-\zeta)$ under the
   standard active convention (relative error 49 against $\Lambda(+\zeta)$).
3. **Replace 3.71e-16 with a worst-case over a real sweep** and say what limits it:
   conditioning on the null cone, growing like $e^{2\zeta}\epsilon$. The honest
   numbers are 2.1e-15 over 400 draws and 1.8e-13 at $\zeta\approx5$.

**Required hygiene**

4. **Add the rotation branch and a boost∘rotation composition.** A pure boost is
   Hermitian, so the current test is blind to $A$ vs $A^\dagger$ — the one place the
   placement actually matters is untested.
5. **Promote the check to its own gate record** with `kind: assertion` and a real
   `expect.exactness`. It currently rides on a six-finding battery `result_dump`
   with `has_assert: false`.
6. **Add a `## Prior art` section** (Peskin §3.2, Srednicki 34–35, Weinberg I §2.7,
   Wess & Bagger app. A) and separate "implemented and verified here" from "novel".
7. **Promote the (1,0) contamination analysis** out of `## Representation-theory
   context` and cross-link it to F302, which reached the same result independently
   two months later. It is the finding's real contribution.
8. Fix the pre-C9 `ca_maxwell.py` citation; decide whether F24 belongs in
   `supersessions.yaml` (the reviewer's view: **no** — the Hermitian 4-current is
   not the retired transpose bilinear, and F24 is the file that says so).
