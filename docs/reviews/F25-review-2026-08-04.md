## Verdict

Both of F25's numbers are right and both of its interpretations are wrong.

**P1, the real-rotation formula, is an identity, not a result.** Given
$G_T(t) = e^{-i\Omega t}G_T(0)$ and $E = 2\lvert n\rvert\,\mathrm{Re}\,G_T$,
$B = 2\lvert n\rvert\,\mathrm{Im}\,G_T$, the rotation law *is* the $2\times2$ real
matrix representation of multiplying a complex number by $e^{-i\Omega}$. F25's own
derivation section says as much — *"This is an exact identity — no small-$k$
approximation required"* — and its Results table nonetheless presents it as a
**prediction under test** that beats Maxwell "by 5 orders of magnitude". The blind
agent's diagnostic is decisive: the residual is $5\times10^{-17}$ at $k = 0.1$ **and
the same number at $k = 2.0$**, which is the signature of an identity, not of a
small-$k$ approximation. A "machine precision" residual here measures floating-point
arithmetic and implementation self-consistency.

**P2's $O(k)$ residual is a definitional artifact, and F25's own §3 is false.**
Item 3 of `## Physical interpretation` says Maxwell is recovered as $\Delta t\to0$
because $\big(E(t+\Delta t)-E(t)\big)/\Delta t \to \Omega B$, "which in position space
is $\partial_t E = c_\text{lat}\nabla\times B$". It is not. Measured here:

| $k$ | $\cos(B,\ \hat n\times E)$ | $\cos(\hat n\times B,\ -E)$ | $\lVert\Omega B\rVert / \lVert 2n\times B\rVert$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $10^{-1}$ | 1.0000000000 | 1.0000000000 | 1.0001379741 |
| $10^{-2}$ | 1.0000000000 | 1.0000000000 | 1.0000013880 |
| $10^{-3}$ | 1.0000000000 | 1.0000000000 | 1.0000000137 |

$B = \hat n\times E$ **exactly**, hence $\hat n\times B = -E$. So $\partial_t E = \Omega B$
says $E$ rotates *into* $B$, while $\nabla\times B \propto \hat n\times B = -E$ points
along $-E$. **The two are orthogonal 3-vectors**, and no $\Delta t$ changes that: the
residual is 0.4082469 at $\Delta t = 10^{-3}$, 0.4082468 at $10^{-6}$, and 0.4082469
for the exact derivative. Dropping the explicit $i$ also changes nothing (0.40825) —
orthogonality, not the $i$, is what binds.

**And the residual is the opposite of what F25 reads it as.** The last column above
shows the two sides agree in *magnitude* to 1 part in $10^8$; the $\sqrt2$ is pure
quadrature between two orthogonal equal-length vectors. So $R\to c_\text{lat}/\sqrt2$
is a restatement of $\lVert\text{LHS}\rVert = \lVert\text{RHS}\rVert$, not a
"leading Planck-scale signature".

**F25 was one step from the real result.** Reading $E$ and $B$ as complex analytic
amplitudes ($\hat E = \lvert n\rvert G_T$, $\hat B = \hat n\times\hat E$) collapses
the whole equation to the scalar condition $\Omega = 2\lvert n\rvert$, i.e.
$\omega(k/2) = \sin\omega(k/2)$, giving

$$R_{(b)} = \frac{k^2}{144\sqrt3} = \frac{c_\text{lat}^3 k^2}{48},$$

measured $3.97\times10^{-5}$, $4.01\times10^{-7}$, $3.95\times10^{-9}$ at
$k = 10^{-1},10^{-2},10^{-3}$ against the closed form
$4.009\times10^{-5}$, $4.009\times10^{-7}$, $4.009\times10^{-9}$. The curl equation
closes at $O(k^3)$. Three independent derivations in this review series reached the
same coefficient by different routes.

**Corrected claim, one sentence:** *The real-rotation law $E' = \cos\Omega\,E + \sin\Omega\,B$,
$B' = -\sin\Omega\,E + \cos\Omega\,B$ is an exact algebraic identity — the real form
of multiplication by $e^{-i\Omega}$, true by construction and carrying no lattice
content — while the $c_\text{lat}/\sqrt2$ "Maxwell curl residual" is quadrature
between two orthogonal vectors of equal magnitude ($B = \hat n\times E$ exactly), is
exactly $\Delta t$-independent so Maxwell is **not** recovered as $\Delta t\to0$, and
under the analytic-amplitude reading the curl equation closes at $O(k^3)$ with
coefficient $c_\text{lat}^3/48$.*

## The claim, as the reviewer understands it

```
TARGET   Two competing one-tick laws for the composite (E,B) pair:
         P1  E' = cosΩ E + sinΩ B,  B' = −sinΩ E + cosΩ B      (real rotation)
         P2  E' ≈ E + i(2n_{k/2})×B, B' ≈ B − i(2n_{k/2})×E    (Maxwell curl)
VALUE    P1 holds to 2.0e-16 / 3.3e-16; P2 deviates by 2.0e-2 — "5 orders of
         magnitude" — and curl_E/k → c_lat/√2 = 0.408248 flat across a decade.
INPUTS   the bilinear prescription; Ω = 2ω(k/2); the BCC walk.
CLASS    Tier-1 exact (#51, real-rotation) + Tier-2 machine (#15, k-scan slope).
```

Undeclared and load-bearing: the spinor pairing (the blind agent used same-helicity,
where $\lVert G_T\rVert = \sqrt2\lvert\hat n_y\rvert$; the harnesses of F21/F23 use
opposite-helicity), and the fact that $G_T$ is a **null** complex vector, which is
what makes $\lVert E\rVert = \lVert B\rVert$ and the coefficient
direction-independent.

## Independent re-derivation

Subagent A read only the definitions of `bilinear_G`, `EM_bilinears` and
`weyl_eigenmodes_3d_bcc`, plus `bcc.py`; it was forbidden from reading, grepping or
referencing the two functions under test, and confirmed it did not.

**On P1 — tautology.** A's hand derivation:
$G_T(t+1) = (\cos\Omega - i\sin\Omega)(A+iC) = (\cos\Omega A + \sin\Omega C) + i(-\sin\Omega A + \cos\Omega C)$;
multiply by the $t$-independent scalar $2\lvert n\rvert$ and the two boxed equations
fall out. A's verdict, verbatim: *"nothing more than the standard $2\times2$ real
matrix representation of multiplication by $e^{-i\Omega}$ in ℂ… Zero lattice input
enters — no BCC structure, no dispersion, no transversality."* A also identified the
one ingredient with real content — that $G_T$ evolves by a **scalar** phase, so all
three components share it — and noted it is *granted as background*, not tested.

**On the handedness.** A checked the sign independently: the stated $(+\sin,-\sin)$
arrangement is correct and self-consistent; the transpose gives residual
$0.115\to1.87$ as $k$ grows. Not ambiguous.

**On P2.** A measured $R(k\to0) = 0.4082469\ldots0.4082497$ over four non-degenerate
directions, derived $R\to c_\text{lat}/\sqrt2 = 1/\sqrt6$ in closed form, and then
ran the scepticism check: LHS purely real (imaginary fraction exactly 0.0), RHS
purely imaginary (real fraction exactly 0.0), $\lVert\text{LHS}\rVert/\lVert\text{RHS}\rVert = 1.0000000174$
at $k=10^{-4}$ and $1.00014 = 1 + k^2/72$ at $k=0.1$. Its conclusion, verbatim:
*"the (E, B) built as $2\mathrm{Re}/2\mathrm{Im}$ of one complex amplitude are a
**time-quadrature pair** of a single circularly-polarised mode, not a Maxwell
electric/magnetic pair… Read that way, the 'nonzero $O(k)$ constant' is in fact
evidence the two sides **match in magnitude to 1 part in $10^8$** — the opposite of a
Maxwell failure."*

**A's step-4 table** (which reading closes the equation):

| Reading | scaling | coefficient |
|---|---|---|
| stated: real pair, exact $d/dt$ | $O(1)$ | $c_\text{lat}/\sqrt2 = 0.4082483$ |
| (a) drop the explicit $i$ | $O(1)$ | $1/\sqrt6$ — unchanged |
| **(b) analytic amplitudes** $\hat E = \lvert n\rvert G_T$, $\hat B = \hat n\times\hat E$ | **$O(k^2)$** | $1/(144\sqrt3) = 0.00400938$ |
| (b′) analytic amplitudes, $i$ dropped | $O(1)$ | $1/\sqrt6$ |
| (c) $\Delta t = 10^{-3}$, real pair | $O(1)$ | 0.4082469 |
| (c) $\Delta t = 10^{-6}$, real pair | $O(1)$ | 0.4082468 |
| (c) exact derivative, real pair | $O(1)$ | 0.4082469 |

Under (b) the equation reduces to $\Omega = 2\lvert n\rvert$, i.e.
$\omega(k/2) = \sin\omega(k/2)$, with
$\Omega/(2\lvert n\rvert) - 1 = k^2/72$ (measured $1.3797\times10^{-4}$ vs
$1.3889\times10^{-4}$ at $k=0.1$) and $\Omega - 2\lvert n\rvert = k^3/(72\sqrt3)$.
A's remark on $\Delta t$: *"Δt → 0 changes nothing at all: $dE = \Omega B$ is already
the exact derivative, and the mismatch is directional, not a discretisation
artifact. Reporting a Δt-refinement here would be uninformative."*

**Degeneracy.** A used four generic directions, all with $\lvert\hat n_y\rvert\ge0.219$,
avoiding the $\hat n_y = 0$ surface (which contains the $x$ and $z$ axes) where
$G_T\equiv0$ and both P1 and P2 would be round-off ratios. It confirmed the
`EM_bilinears` identity $\lVert G_T\rVert = \sqrt2\lvert\hat n_y\rvert$ to six digits
and measured $\lvert G_T\cdot G_T\rvert/\lVert G_T\rVert^2 \le 2\times10^{-12}$ —
$G_T$ is null, hence $\lVert E\rVert/\lVert B\rVert = 1.0000000000$ and
$E\cdot B \le 5\times10^{-21}$.
