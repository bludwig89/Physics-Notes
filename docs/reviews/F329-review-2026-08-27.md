## The claim, as the reviewer understands it

TARGET: does every non-negative real-valued function $f$ on the rays of a finite-dimensional
complex Hilbert space $\mathbb C^d$, $d\ge3$, satisfying $\sum_i f(e_i)=W$ for every orthonormal
basis $\{e_i\}$ — with no continuity, boundedness, or measurability assumed in advance — admit a
Hermitian operator $T$ with $f(v)=\langle v|T|v\rangle$ for every unit vector $v$ (i.e. is $f$
automatically "regular")? Equivalently: is every non-negative frame function on such a space
automatically continuous? This claim card was handed to the blind subagent without F329's
dimension list, without any mention of Gleason's theorem numbers, and without the CKM citation —
only the abstract mathematical statement and the model's own prerequisite machinery (F304, F312).

No ambiguity was found between the finding's own statement of the claim and what was reviewed.

## Independent re-derivation

Subagent A (cold, blind, forbidden from reading any F329/CL264/`qi_gleason_regularity.py` file or
grepping "F329" anywhere in the repo) independently:

- proved the free part — non-negativity plus the frame condition alone gives $0\le f(v)\le W$ for
  every ray, with zero extra assumptions, by completing $v$ to a basis;
- reached **YES** via three independently-converging routes: (1) Gleason's own 1957 Theorem 2.8
  (oscillation-squeezing on $S^2$, via Lemmas 2.3–2.7) plus Theorem 3.5/Lemma 3.3 (completely-real
  reduction to any $\dim\ge3$); (2) Cooke–Keane–Moran's 1985 "great-circle descent," known only
  secondhand (their own PDF was also unreadable to this reviewer, independently confirming F329's
  own stated limitation); (3) an independent modern route via Cauchy's functional equation and the
  classical Ostrowski–Kestelman rigidity theorem, cross-checked against two papers it found
  unprompted (arXiv:1905.12751, arXiv:2603.07745) that F329 itself does not cite;
- independently diagnosed why $d=2$ fails structurally (only one great circle, so a discontinuous
  additive endomorphism composed with any continuous frame function remains a valid, discontinuous
  frame function) — matching F329's own stated exclusion mechanism;
- explicitly flagged, unprompted, that it could not fully reconstruct Gleason's Lemma 2.7
  oscillation-covering argument from primitives, and could not obtain machine-readable CKM 1985
  text — both stated as real, acknowledged limitations rather than papered over, and both
  independently matching limitations F329 itself discloses in its §5 honesty section.

This is the "different route reaching the same value" case the command's own grading table calls
the strongest possible support — subagent A's Cauchy's-equation route is genuinely independent of
Gleason's own oscillation-geometry route, and it converged with two more routes found unprompted.
