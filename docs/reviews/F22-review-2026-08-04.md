## Verdict

F22 makes three claims. One is correct and is a restatement of F15; the other two
do not survive.

1. **"The SR Lorentz boost acts exactly on the QCA 4-momentum $(\omega,k)$" is
   false**, and fails at *leading* order. Boosting linearly and asking whether
   $\omega' = \omega(k')$ gives a deviation first order in $v$ with coefficient
   $1/\rho - 1 = 2\beta_\text{LV}/(1-2\beta_\text{LV})$ — measured
   $-0.03105 / -0.20732 / -0.58071$ at $m = 0.3/0.7/0.95$ against the predicted
   $-0.031139/-0.208936/-0.588081$. At a finite boost it is a **7.1%** error in
   $\omega$ at $m=0.7$, $k=1.0$, $v=0.3$. The finding **contradicts itself**: its
   own Step 4 reports $d_\text{qca} \approx v(1-1/\rho) = 3.35\times10^{-6} \neq 0$
   and calls it "the fundamental LV mismatch".
2. **$\rho(m) = \tan\theta/\theta = 1-2\beta_\text{LV}$ is correct** — confirmed by
   an independent route at sympy zero — but the finding's *check* of it is a
   **definitional tautology**. `derive_velocity_addition.py:199-206` sets
   `beta_LV_sym = Rational(1,2)*(1 - rho_derived)` and then verifies
   `rho_derived - (1 - 2*beta_LV_sym)`, which is $x - (1-2(1-x)/2) \equiv 0$ for
   *any* expression. Substituting a deliberately wrong $\rho = m/\arcsin m$, and
   even $\rho = 42$, both still print residual **0**. F15's independently derived
   closed form is never imported or compared.
3. **The "deformed velocity-addition formula" is undeformed.** In the nonlinear
   variables $E = \sin\omega$, $P = n\sin(ka)/a$ one has $E^2 - c_\text{lat}^2P^2 = m^2$
   identically and $u_g = c_\text{lat}^2 P/E$ exactly, so the group velocity
   composes by **ordinary Einstein addition** — verified to $5.6\times10^{-17}$.
   Worse, F22's own formula misses the lattice's actual boost response by
   $10^2$–$10^3\times$: the true leading deviation is first order in $v$,
   $v(1-1/\rho)$, not the third-order $8\beta_\text{LV}uv(u+v)$ F22 headlines.

**Corrected claim, one sentence:** *On the 2D-square $k_y=0$ axis, the linear SR
boost of $(\omega,k)$ does **not** preserve the arccos mass shell — it fails at
$O(vk)$ with coefficient $1/\rho-1$, where $\rho(m) = \tan(\arcsin m)/\arcsin m =
1-2\beta_\text{LV}(m)$ is F15's coefficient re-expressed as the $k\to0$ ratio
$u_p/u_g$; the QCA's true kinematics are a nonlinear (DSR-type) representation in
which velocities compose by **undeformed** Einstein addition, as published for the
Dirac QCA in 2013–2016.*

## Independent re-derivation

Subagent A read only `CLAUDE.md` and `findings/F15-*.md`, never F22 or its module.
It confirmed no forbidden file was read.

**A found the structure the finding is missing.** Since
$\sin^2\omega = 1-n^2\cos^2u = m^2 + n^2\sin^2 u$, define $E \equiv \sin\omega$ and
$P \equiv n\sin(ka)/a$. Then

$$E^2 - c_\text{lat}^2 P^2 = m^2 \quad\text{identically (sympy: 0)},$$

with the exact rapidity parametrisation $E = m\cosh\eta$, $aP = m\sinh\eta$,
$u_g = c_\text{lat}\tanh\eta$, and $u_g \equiv d\omega/dk = c_\text{lat}^2P/E$
exactly at all $k$ (sympy: 0). **There is an exact Lorentz-invariant mass shell —
but in nonlinear variables, not in $(\omega,k)$.** The map $(E,P)$ is A's own
construction; the claim card supplied none, and everything in (1)-exact and (3)
rests on it.

**On (1).** The level set is not preserved. To first order in $v$,
$\Delta = v(\omega u_g/c^2 - k) + O(v^2)$ with
$\Delta/(vk)\vert_{k\to0} = 1/\rho - 1$. Structurally it cannot work: on the shell
$\omega$ is bounded in $[\theta,\pi-\theta]$ and periodic in $k$, while boost orbits
are unbounded hyperbolae. Allowing a *free* effective speed
$c_\text{eff} = c_\text{lat}/\sqrt\rho$ kills the $O(vk)$ term but $c_\text{eff}$ is
$m$-dependent, so no single speed works, and it still fails at $O(vk^3)$. In the
deformed variables invariance is exact to $10^{-61}$ at 60 dps — but the action is
only **partial**: $\sin\omega\le1$ forces $m\gamma\le1$, i.e. $\lvert\beta\rvert<n$,
and boosts beyond that map onto no lattice mode; and $\omega\mapsto\sin\omega$ is
2-to-1 across the BZ, so the lift back to $k$ is ambiguous.

**On (2).** A re-derived $\beta_\text{LV}$ from F15's *definition* rather than its
stated closed form, obtaining $\beta_\text{LV} = \tfrac12(1-T/\theta)$ and, as a
free cross-check, $\gamma_\text{LV} = \tfrac18 - \tfrac1\theta(\tfrac T8 + \tfrac{T^3}{24})$
— both matching F15 at sympy zero. Then $1-2\beta_\text{LV} = \tan\theta/\theta = \rho$,
sympy difference $\equiv 0$: an exact identity, not an approximation.
**A's own caveat, verbatim:** *"both sides reduce to $\tan\theta/\theta$, so this is
an algebraic restatement, not independent corroboration — it is the same object
reached two ways."*

A also observed what the finding does not: $\rho = c_\text{lat}^2/(v_\text{ph}v_g)$,
i.e. $\rho$ *is* the failure of the relativistic identity $v_\text{ph}v_g = c^2$;
$u_p$ is **superluminal for $ka>\pi/2$**, peaking at $\pi/(\pi-\theta)$ (1.200 at
$m=0.5$, 1.554 at $m=0.9$), so it is not a signal velocity; and $\rho$ runs at
finite $k$ (at $m=0.5$: 1.1027 at $k=0$, 1.1547 at the BZ edge, divergent there).

**On (3).** $c_\text{lat}$ is the invariant asymptote of the composition law but is
**not attainable**; the lattice's kinematic ceiling is $\beta_\max = n = \sqrt{1-m^2}$,
which is $m$-dependent and hence **not invariant**. And the accessible domain is
**not closed** under the composition: $m=0.4$, $k=1.1$, $v=-0.4$ gives
$\lvert u_g'\rvert = 0.6758 > n\,c_\text{lat} = 0.6481$ — off-lattice.

**A's headline, verbatim:** *"the addition law is **undeformed**; all the deformation
sits in the nonlinear map $k\mapsto u_g$ and in the bounded, non-closed domain.
Calling it 'deformed velocity addition' would be a misnomer."*

Free inputs: **one** ($m$), zero fitted — $a = c_\text{lat}$ cancels identically
because $\omega$ depends on $k$ only through $u = ka$. That part of the finding is
sound.

## Adjudication of the blind agent's two headline claims

Both were re-tested by the referee rather than taken on report.

**(1) FALSE — confirmed, from F22's own data.** Boosting $(\omega,k)$ linearly and
asking whether $\omega' = \omega(k')$: deviation $-1.58\times10^{-5}$ ($m=0.3$),
$-1.06\times10^{-4}$ ($m=0.7$), $-2.96\times10^{-4}$ ($m=0.95$) at $k=0.5$,
$v=0.001$ — first order in $v$. Leading coefficient measured $-0.03105/-0.20732/-0.58071$
against the predicted $1/\rho-1 = -0.031139/-0.208936/-0.588081$ (residual is
finite-$k$ drift). The level set is not preserved.

**(3) UNDEFORMED — confirmed, and stronger.** With $E=\sin\omega$, $P=n\sin(ka)/a$:
$E^2-c^2P^2-m^2 \le 2.2\times10^{-16}$, $u_g - c^2P/E \le 1.1\times10^{-16}$, and
boosting $(E,P)$ then re-reading $u_g$ reproduces plain Einstein composition to
$\le 5.6\times10^{-17}$ at every $(m,k,v)$ sampled. Additionally, **F22's own formula
does not describe the lattice**:

| $m$ | $k$ | $v$ | $d_\text{qca}$ (lattice $-$ SR) | F22 formula $-$ SR | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 0.01 | 0.001 | $3.347\times10^{-6}$ | $3.247\times10^{-8}$ | **103×** |
| 0.5 | 0.01 | 0.001 | $9.310\times10^{-5}$ | $2.864\times10^{-8}$ | **3251×** |
| 0.5 | 0.30 | 0.001 | $9.473\times10^{-5}$ | $2.526\times10^{-5}$ | 3.75× |

The real leading deviation is first order in $v$, exactly $v(1-1/\rho)$ ($m=0.5$,
$v=10^{-3}$ → $9.3104\times10^{-5}$, matching to five digits), not the third-order
$8\beta_\text{LV}uv(u+v)$ F22 headlines.

## What would falsify this

The corrected claim is falsifiable and the original was not:

1. The off-shell coefficient $\Delta/(vk)\vert_{k\to0}$ differing from $1/\rho-1$ on
   the 2D-square walk would kill the corrected claim 1.
2. Group velocities failing to compose by *undeformed* Einstein addition in the
   $(E,P)$ variables, at any $(m,k,v)$ inside $\lvert\beta\rvert<n$, would kill the
   corrected claim 3.
3. $\rho \neq \tan(\arcsin m)/\arcsin m$ at any $m\in(0,1)$ would kill claim 2 —
   which survives.

Note what does *not* falsify anything: the effect vanishes for photons, so the
strong astrophysical LIV bounds are structurally inapplicable, and the massive-sector
prediction sits $\sim10^{-41}$ below the best time-dilation sensitivity. That should
be stated as a limitation, not left implicit.
