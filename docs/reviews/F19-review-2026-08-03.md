## Independent analysis

The blind agent predicted the outcome before judging the design, and then built the experiment.

**$\alpha_\text{inner}=3$, exactly, field-independent — by theorem.** For a hyperbolic equation with $0<c_\text{min}\le c(x)\le c_\text{max}<\infty$ the map to optical coordinates is bi-Lipschitz, and bi-Lipschitz maps preserve box-counting and Hausdorff dimension exactly (Falconer, *Fractal Geometry* 2nd ed., Cor. 2.4(b) and §3.2(iv) — the referee checked both verbatim). The ever-touched set is the causal future of an open set, hence open, hence full-dimensional. In the spec's own allowed band $c_\text{min}/c_0 = 0.5$, so the Lipschitz constant is capped at **2** and the total optical infall time is only 56% longer than vacuum at the deepest allowed point (1.011 / 1.113 / 1.338 / 1.563 at $\lvert\varphi\rvert/c_0^2 = 0.01/0.10/0.30/0.50$). **The instruction "do not push past the formal horizon" is precisely what guarantees the null.**

**A second, independent killer: the propagator's support.** A Cayley step is $(I-i\Delta tH/2)^{-1}(I+i\Delta tH/2)$ and the inverse of a tridiagonal is dense, so the one-tick Green's function has non-compact support with geometric decay. The blind agent measured underflow radii of 339–668 cells depending on order and Courant number — all exceeding the largest specified lattice (L=192, half-diagonal 166). **After one tick every cell has strictly nonzero float64 amplitude.** The referee then confirmed this on the *real* code: `curved.py`'s Cayley solver is a `scipy.sparse` LU on `_build_cayley_matrix_2d`, and one tick leaves **99.59% (GM=0) / 100.00% (depth 0.5)** of an L=128 lattice strictly nonzero, decay $\approx0.87$ decades/cell.

**And the experiment agrees.** The blind agent built the 2D analogue (where 2 *is* the volume exponent) and got $\alpha_\text{inner}=2.007$, $\alpha_\text{outer}=2.003$, fill 100%, **identical to three decimals between the GM=0 null and the maximum allowed depth**, and identical across thresholds from $10^{-300}$ to $10^{-6}$. The referee reproduced it with the repo's own propagator: fill 100.00% at depths 0.0/0.1/0.3/0.5; $\alpha_\text{outer}$ = 2.003/2.000/2.002/2.002; $\alpha_\text{inner}$ = 2.015 at the deepest allowed field.

**The necessary condition, and why even a horizon would not give 2.** $N(R)=\int n(r)4\pi r^2dr$ requires $n(r)\propto r^{\alpha-3}$, so $\alpha=2$ needs the ever-touched set to be codimension-1 across the whole inner band. The only routes are $c=0$ on a surface (forbidden, and unreachable since $c\ge c_0/2$), non-wave dynamics, or a tuned threshold that then measures the threshold. And an excluded core gives $N\propto(R^3-r_h^3)$, whose local slope is **above** 3; a frozen interior gives $\alpha\to0$. There is no mechanism here that produces a stable plateau at 2.

**But the spec can still report $\alpha\approx2$ — for the wrong reason.** Both artefacts were confirmed on synthetic pure-$R^3$ truth: shell counts instead of cumulative give **2.117**, and a fit straddling a partial-fill front gives 1.547 / 2.403 / 2.873 for fronts at $r_f=20/30/40$. The spec's runtime $2R_0/c_0$ is *exactly* the vacuum round trip, so under the 11–56% optical delay a fill front inside the fitted band is guaranteed, not hypothetical — and sanity check 3 *mandates* reporting the shell statistic, which lands inside the pre-registered AREA window.

**The category error.** Three objects are conflated: (a) the number of cells (always $\propto R^3$, and 't Hooft *presupposes* it); (b) the number of distinguishable states — Bekenstein's and the CAI's quantity, area-scaling because information loss collapses bulk configurations into equivalence classes; (c) the number of cells ever active along one trajectory. N_active is (c). Four sharp consequences: support is not a state count (and the bound is saturated trivially at 100% fill, so it carries no information); **the CAI mechanism is absent by construction** — §9.4 rests on a non-invertible automaton (its Ch. 7 Eq. 7.2 maps 5 states to 3 classes), while this model is exactly unitary, so there is zero equivalence-class merging and no area law can emerge in any geometry at any field strength; Bekenstein needs energy and a horizon while N_active is threshold-based and energy-independent, with neither a mass nor $\hbar$ anywhere in it; and "fixed global tick count" is not a state, so $\alpha$ is a function of $T$, $\sigma_\psi$ and the threshold rather than an invariant of the geometry.

**The correct analogue — already built.** The blind agent proposed entanglement entropy of the ball from the reduced covariance matrix, noting that the free-field area law is present with **no gravity at all** (Srednicki 1993), so the exponent cannot be the test statistic — the coefficient is: does $c_A a^2 = 1/4G$ with the structural $G=a^2c^3/(8\pi\sqrt3\hbar)$? The referee found the repo had already done exactly this in **F190** (`src/casim/engine/interactions/horizon_entropy.py`), whose own first line reads: *"The area law $S\sim A$ is automatic from the tiling; the coefficient 1/4 then **fixes** the required per-cell entropy"* — three findings and one canonical decision after F19 was drafted.

## The "bit-for-bit N = 0 on 80%" premise

The blind agent flagged that an exactly unitary Cayley stepper cannot leave 80% of a lattice at bit-exact zero for more than about one tick, and asked what the prior result was really measuring. The referee settled it, and the answer is worse than the options on offer: **a threshold, plus a pre-excluded region, plus a short run — and the pre-exclusion is circular.**

`src/casim/numerics/lazy.py:41,78`:

```python
DEFAULT_EPS = 1.0e-13

def increment_from_residual(self, residual, eps=DEFAULT_EPS):
    """Convenience: build the bool mask from a real-valued residual."""
    self.increment(residual > eps)
```

and the module's own docstring (`lazy.py:16-19`) concedes it: *"For FFT-based steppers this is bookkeeping-lazy: the propagator still touches every cell (FFTs are global by construction)."*

`tests/findings/test_emergent_time_T5.py:69-99` supplies the rest — $\varepsilon=10^{-13}$, `n_steps=40`, and a vacuum region **defined as the complement of the causal cone plus a safety margin**:

```python
def test_T5A(L=256, n_steps=40, ..., eps=1e-13):
    safety = float(n_steps) + 6.0 * sigma      # = 40 + 24 = 64
    vacuum_mask = r > safety
```

The test asserts $N=0$ on precisely the region it selected *because* it expects $N=0$ there. `test-results/figures/T5_results.json` confirms `vacuum_fraction: 0.8039`, `N_max_vacuum: 0`, `eps: 1e-13` — and `lattice_size: 65536 = 256²`, i.e. **the "80% of an L=256 lattice" result is 2D**, while F19's spec is 3D.

Three consequences:

1. **Exactness Inventory Tier 1 #2 (`docs/status/exactness-inventory.md:40`) is misclassified.** "$=0$ bit-for-bit" is a Tier-1 exact-algebraic claim; the mechanism is a $10^{-13}$ residual cut on a geometrically pre-excluded annulus after 40 ticks in 2D. Worth fixing independently of F19.
2. **F19's premise sentence is false as written**, and it also conflates two inventory rows: it attributes the 80%/L=256 result to "the F1 vacuum regression", which is Tier 1 **#3**; the 80% result is Tier 1 **#2** (T5.A).
3. The repo already contained the null: `T5_results.json` → `T5B_benchmark` shows `fraction_occupied: 1.0` at L=64 after only **20 steps**.
