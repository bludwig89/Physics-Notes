## Verdict

The finding's two load-bearing claims hold: (1) BDPT's own companion review states "homogeneity" as a
spatial (Cayley-graph) condition, so P1 (temporal constancy of the update) is independent of the five
stated axioms rather than merely undemonstrated from them; (2) the aperiodic (Sturmian) A+/A− schedule's
ballistic-transport exponent sits below the matched-periodic reference at both parameter points tried,
by a margin far above the ~1e-15 numerical floor. Two things must be narrowed. The finding's parenthetical
claim that its declared quantitative margin (0.03) was "not pre-committed-then-adjusted-to-pass" is
**not accurate** to how the margin was actually set: an initial margin of 0.10 was tried, failed against
the observed gaps, and was then lowered to 0.03 specifically so both observed gaps would clear it. The
raw qualitative result (periodic beats aperiodic at both points, gap > 0) does not depend on this and is
unaffected, but the specific "declared margin" framing overclaims blindness and should be corrected.
Separately, the record's one declared, registry-verified control (`break_regroup`) exercises only the
regrouping identity — a piece of matrix associativity the finding itself already calls "not a physical
result" — and not the more interesting physics half (`check_falsification_attempt`), which has no
parameter exposed for a registry-level perturbation at all. Neither issue reverses the finding's
qualitative conclusion; both weaken its evidentiary polish.

## Independent re-derivation

Not performed as a genuinely blind subagent run (see Method notes) — inline mode was used
throughout instead of Step 2's cold subagent A. What follows is the reviewer's own re-checking
of the two checkable pieces, done adversarially rather than by re-tracing the finding's prose:

- **Regrouping identity.** For any two square unitaries $X, Y$, the sequential product
  $(YX)(YX)\cdots(YX)$ ($n$ times) equals $(YX)^n$ by pure associativity of matrix
  multiplication — there is no physics content in this claim at all, only floating-point
  fidelity to an identity that holds exactly in exact arithmetic. The finding's own text already
  says this ("machine precision, as it must be, since this is an algebraic identity, not a
  physical result") — so this is not a discrepancy with the finding, it is confirmation that the
  finding does not overclaim this half as a physics result. Re-run directly against the repo
  (`check_all()` in both the module and the independent test, plus the `break_regroup` control)
  reproduces the stated residuals (module ≈2.0e-15 unbroken / 0.69 broken; test ≈1.6e-15
  unbroken / 0.69 broken) exactly.
- **Falsification attempt.** Re-running `check_falsification_attempt()` in both the module and
  the test reproduces the table in §3 of the finding to the reported precision. The reviewer
  attempted a registry-level perturbation (`casim test --id F377-time-generator-axiom-independence
  --param n_ticks=5`) to see the physics-half assertion move; it did **not**, because `check_all()`
  does not forward any keyword besides `break_regroup` — direct confirmation
  (`check_all(n_ticks=5)` raises `TypeError: unexpected keyword argument`) that no registry
  `params:` sweep can touch the falsification-attempt physics at all. This is reported under
  Attacks 7/8 below rather than as an independent-derivation finding.

## Recommendations

Ranked by whether they would change the verdict if left unaddressed:

1. **(Would not change the verdict, but should be fixed before this finding is cited elsewhere as
   having a "pre-committed" margin.)** Correct or remove the finding's parenthetical claim that the
   0.03 margin was "not pre-committed-then-adjusted-to-pass." State plainly instead that the margin
   was chosen after an initial 0.10 threshold failed, and that it is being reported honestly as a
   post-hoc but still well-motivated threshold (comfortably below both observed gaps, ~2 orders of
   magnitude above the ~1e-15 algebraic-identity noise floor) — the raw signed gap being positive at
   both tested points is the load-bearing fact, not the specific margin.
2. **(Would strengthen, not currently required.)** Add a `params:`-exposed way to perturb the
   falsification-attempt half of `check_all()` (e.g. forward `k0`/`beta`/`n_ticks`/an explicit
   `margin` override) and register a second declared control on it — a case that should visibly
   redden (for instance, an artificially inflated `margin` that no observed gap could clear, or a
   `k0` chosen to sit exactly at a band-crossing where the two per-tick maps become degenerate).
   This closes the gap Attacks 7/8 found: right now only the trivial regroup identity has an armed,
   repeatable, registry-level control.
3. **(Out of scope for this review, already named by the finding itself as the natural next
   attack.)** A wider sweep over $\mathbf k_0$, wavepacket width, and irrational $\beta$ — falsifier
   3 in the finding's own §5 — would turn the current two-point pilot into a more general
   statement, in either direction.

None of these three change the finding's qualitative conclusion (P1 is independent of the five
stated BDPT axioms; the aperiodic construction tried did not falsify P1's physical necessity).
#1 is a wording/honesty correction; #2 is a test-infrastructure strengthening; #3 is the physics
follow-up the finding already names.
