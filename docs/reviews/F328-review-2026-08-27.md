## Verdict

The core algebraic theorem is correct, independently re-derivable by a cold agent, machine-verified
against the real implementation, and honestly controlled (the declared negative control reddens
exactly the four legs it claims to). One real defect was found: the finding's own §2 (and the module
docstring) asserted that BOTH of its two algebraic identities are specific to the BCC lattice's cos/sin
closed form, while its own §3.1 and §3.2 correctly show that identity (I) — the one the headline CPT
theorem actually uses — is a **generic** fact about any real-$(u,\mathbf n)$-parametrized Pauli-basis
unitary, not a BCC-specific one. Only identity (II), needed for the companion parity no-go, is
BCC-specific. This is a genuine internal contradiction in the exposition (not in the mathematics or the
numerics), and it has been corrected in both `findings/F328-...md` §2 and the module docstring as part
of this review's attack-and-fix pass (this session authored F328 originally, so the fix was applied
directly rather than merely recommended — see Method notes).

**Narrowed claim:** $\Theta D(\mathbf k)\Theta^{-1}=D(\mathbf k)^{-1}$ with $\Theta^2=-1$ holds for
**any** Dirac-type block unitary built from a real-$(u,\mathbf n)$-parametrized Weyl kinetic term paired
with its own dagger and a real mass — the BCC lattice is the specific instance realized here, not a
precondition of the identity. The companion no-go (no fixed unitary parity at finite $\mathbf k$) **is**
genuinely BCC-specific, since it rests on the cos/sin closed form's behaviour under $\mathbf k\to-\mathbf k$.

## Independent re-derivation

Subagent A (cold, blind, forbidden from reading any F328/CL285/discrete_cpt.py/A4r file) read only the
two prerequisite implementation files (`bcc.py`, `dirac_bcc.py`) and independently:

- derived identity (I), $A^+(\mathbf k)^*=\sigma_y A^+(\mathbf k)\sigma_y$, from the real implementation;
- constructed $M=\sigma_x\otimes\sigma_y$ (its own notation for "chirality-swap tensor spin-flip") and
  proved $\Theta D(\mathbf k)\Theta^{-1}=D(\mathbf k)^{-1}$ by block algebra, verifying numerically to
  $5\times10^{-16}$ over 200 random $(\mathbf k,m)$ trials with $|k_i|$ up to 2.5 rad (well outside any
  small-$k$ regime);
- computed $\Theta^2=MM^*=-\mathbb1_4$ exactly, independently identifying it as a Kramers-type signature;
- independently derived the parity no-go from $u^+(-\mathbf k)=u^-(\mathbf k)\ne u^+(\mathbf k)$
  generically, confirmed by comparing eigenphases of $D(\mathbf k)$ and $D(-\mathbf k)$ at a generic
  point (0.9197 vs 1.7427 — clearly different spectra).

The adversarial referee (subagent B, full access) verified by direct matrix multiplication that
subagent A's $M=\sigma_x\otimes\sigma_y$ is the **literal same matrix** as the finding's
$\Sigma\cdot(\sigma_y\!\oplus\!\sigma_y)$ (max elementwise difference $0.0$), not merely an equivalent
construction under a different name. This is a same-route confirmation — the two identities close on
the same forced ansatz — rather than an independent-route confirmation, since the reality identity
$A^*=\sigma_yA\sigma_y$ is the natural (arguably the only) way in on this problem. Per the review's own
grading rule this is graded "forced-route... note it, do not oversell," but the numerical agreement to
machine precision on a completely independently written verification script is still strong evidence
against a coding error or an algebra slip specific to the finding's own script.

## What would falsify this

A momentum $\mathbf k$ and mass $m$ (within $|m|\le1$) at which
$\lVert\Theta D(\mathbf k)\Theta^{-1}-D(\mathbf k)^{-1}\rVert\ne0$ to machine precision — not expected,
since the identity is proved in closed form (§3.2) rather than fit or sampled into agreement.
Discovery of some other fixed unitary $\Pi\ne\Sigma$ satisfying $\Pi D(\mathbf k)\Pi^{-1}=D(-\mathbf k)$
for all $\mathbf k$ would falsify the companion no-go — ruled out by the spectral-mismatch argument,
which holds for any fixed $\Pi$, not just $\Sigma$.
