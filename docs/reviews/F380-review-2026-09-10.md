## Verdict

F380's central promotion claim — that F331's mass-dependent $\kappa_{100}$ residual table collapses to one, approximately mass-independent named object, and that the dynamically NJL-selected mass $m^*$ is unremarkable within it — is TRUE and survives all 13 attacks. It is independently corroborated by a blind re-derivation that reached the same structural conclusion by a genuinely different analytical route. **Narrowed for one substantive reason**, caught by the adversarial referee and confirmed by the blind agent's own independent work: the finding's original mechanism was wrong. It claimed the algebraic prefactor was $r^{-1}$ ("2 transverse real momenta integrate out via stationary phase… EXACTLY the continuum static Yukawa propagator's $e^{-\kappa r}/r$… same structure, same reason") and used a crude window-midpoint proxy $K=(\kappa_\text{measured}-\kappa_{100})\cdot\bar r_\text{mid}=1.71\pm0.04$ as the named object. The model's dispersion $\omega(a)=\arccos(n\,u(a))$ in fact has a **square-root branch point** at the axial pole (not a simple pole, which is what the continuum-Yukawa analogy assumes), contributing an *extra* $r^{-1/2}$ on top of the transverse $r^{-1}$, for a total power $p=3/2$. Re-deriving the named object using the exact OLS regression-bias formula at $p=3/2$ (rather than the crude midpoint proxy) gives $p_\text{eff}=1.489\pm0.039$ (2.65% relative spread) — landing within $0.71\%$ of the theoretically derived $3/2$, a materially cleaner and now theoretically anchored result. Fixed at the source in the finding, module, test, claim card and completeness row (not merely re-labelled) as of this review.

## The claim, as the reviewer understands it

**TARGET:** F331 validated its exact closed-form $\kappa_{100}(m)=\sqrt3\operatorname{arccosh}(1/\sqrt{1-m^2})$ against the model's own dispersion by fitting a plain exponential to a numerically measured correlator over a finite, mass-dependent window, and found a measured/exact ratio that is *not* 1 and varies with $m$ (1.53 at $m=0.05$ down to 0.95 at $m=0.95$).
**VALUE claimed:** this entire mass-dependent residual reduces to one, approximately mass-independent quantity when the fit's known systematic bias is correctly accounted for.
**CLASS claimed:** `quantitative` — the named quantity is measured, not yet derived to machine precision; row A10 promoted QUANT→PARTIAL, not to MACHINE.
**Ambiguity in the finding's original statement:** the mechanism section asserted a specific theoretical origin (transverse stationary phase alone, power 1) with textbook-certainty language, when in fact a second effect (the axial branch point) was required and missing; this was the one place the finding's confidence exceeded what its own derivation supported.

## Independent re-derivation

A cold `general-purpose` subagent was given a non-leading claim card describing F331's measurement method and F380's structural question (does the residual reduce to one object?) without F380's mechanism or its module. Forbidden from reading F380, its module, its test, or its results. It:

- derived, from the model's dispersion's own functional form, that the axial direction has a square-root branch point (via $\arccos(1-\epsilon)\sim\sqrt{2\epsilon}$), contributing $r^{-1/2}$ via Watson's-lemma endpoint asymptotics on top of the transverse Gaussian's $r^{-1}$, for total power $p=3/2$ — reaching this independently, not by confirming a stated answer;
- used only F331's own module as a numerical tool to measure $\kappa_\text{measured}(m)$ and $\kappa_\text{exact}(m)$ at several masses, and combined the bias with the fit window's *exact* OLS covariance weighting (not a crude midpoint) at $p=3/2$, obtaining a normalized bias of $0.996\pm0.028$ — essentially exactly 1, i.e. exact agreement with its own $p=3/2$ prediction;
- explicitly checked $p=1$ under the same exact-weighting method and found it gives $1.494\pm0.043$ — internally just as *stable* (same relative spread) but not anchored to any predicted value, which is precisely what distinguishes a real mechanism from a rescaling that happens to be self-consistent.

This is graded as strong, genuine, independent support: a different derivation route (branch-point/Watson's-lemma reasoning from the dispersion's functional form) reaching the same numerical target the adversarial referee later confirmed by a second, also-independent method (a direct free-power numerical fit at wide converged windows, $p_\text{fit}\in[1.48,1.52]$).

## What would falsify this

A full symbolic (sympy) expansion of the BCC dispersion's phase at the dominant saddle, computed to the order that fixes the sub-leading asymptotic coefficient, disagreeing with the measured $p_\text{eff}=1.489\pm0.039$ band (already anchored near the leading $p=3/2$ to $<1\%$) would falsify the mechanism as stated. A genuinely independent numerical method (arbitrary-precision direct summation, not FFT) giving a different power or failing to reproduce the $\sim\!2.65\%$ mass-independence would likewise undercut the claim.
