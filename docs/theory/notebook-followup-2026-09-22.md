## Part I — The p.77 charge partition (F396)

### I.0 The proposal, in the author's words

> charge "is partly due to intrinsic properties of a particle, and partly not. These intrinsic qualities, then, would become W± bosons in a more advanced theory. With such an understanding, we might then turn around and understand mass as arising quite naturally from another field."

The supporting argument is an analogy with two legs:

- **Leg A** (asserted as known): a Dirac mass term appears to violate spin conservation — it flips the spin — but total angular momentum *J = L + S* is conserved, because the field takes up the difference.
- **Leg B** (the proposal): the weak mass term likewise appears to violate charge conservation, and the resolution has the same shape — the W± carries the missing charge. Charge is then "intrinsic + field", exactly as angular momentum is "spin + orbital".

If true, this would reach the model's helicity-selective mass step by different bookkeeping than F27's β-gauging, and would make the W a consequence rather than a posit.

### I.1 Why the model is a fair place to test it

F27's mass step is an explicit swap of the two chiral halves of the spinor:

> η_new = cos(m·dt)·η + i·sin(m·dt)·(U⊗I)·χ
> χ_new = i·sin(m·dt)·(U†⊗I)·η + cos(m·dt)·χ

On a lattice where every component carries a definite charge label, a step that *moves amplitude between components* is exactly where a charge-conservation question becomes concrete rather than rhetorical. F27 answers it by making U(x) a pure gauge direction — a compensator, not a boson. p.77 answers it by making the field leg physical and charged. Same step; two different claims about what the W is. That is testable.

### I.2 Leg A, step by step

**Step 1 — ask whether the mass step moves spin at all.** Apply the mass step alone to random spinor states and measure the change in the expectation value of the spin operator Σ_z.
**Result:** the change is 2.8×10⁻¹⁷ — machine zero. The mass step **conserves spin exactly**. Mechanically this is because the mass step is the pointwise η↔χ rotation, generated by β, and β commutes with Σ.
**Meaning:** what the mass step actually flips is **chirality** (handedness, the γ₅ eigenvalue), not spin. The notebook itself says this fourteen pages later, on p.91 — *"the mass term … does not mix handedness — i.e. it conserves helicity/spin."* Pages 77 and 91 contradict each other and p.91 is the correct one.

**Step 2 — find what *does* move the spin.** Run the full step (kinetic + mass) on a seeded wavepacket for 12 ticks at m = 0.2 and track ⟨S_z⟩.
**Result:** ⟨S_z⟩ does swing — by 0.107 at small k and 0.215 at larger k — but the swing comes from the **kinetic** step, not the mass step.

**Step 3 — ask whether J = L + S rescues it anyway.** Measure the ratio (swing in ⟨J_z⟩)/(swing in ⟨S_z⟩); if J is conserved while S is not, the ratio should be ~0.
**Result:** 0.068 at small k, 0.218 at larger k, drifting roughly linearly in k.
**Meaning:** J_z is conserved only in the continuum limit, not exactly.

**Step 4 — find out why.** Test which rotations are exact symmetries of the BCC Weyl block.
**Result:** the exact point group is **D₂** — the twofold rotations about x, y and z hold to 1.1×10⁻¹⁶, while the fourfold C₄z and the threefold C₃ fail outright (residual 1.47).
**Meaning:** on this lattice there is **no continuous rotation**, hence no exact orbital angular momentum L_z, hence no exact J_z for the field to hide the missing spin in. Leg A's mechanism does not exist here even in principle, beyond O(k). *This is a genuinely new structural fact about the substrate and is worth keeping independently of p.77.*

**Step 5 — a representation trap found on the way.** The Weyl block is A ≈ 1 − i k·σ*/√3 — the **conjugate** representation — so the spin generator is S_z = −σ_z/2, not +σ_z/2. Using the naive sign inflates the apparent J_z drift by about a factor of two.
**Meaning:** a sign convention that would have silently corrupted any later angular-momentum result in this sector. Now recorded.

**Leg A verdict:** the analogy's stated premise is **false in this model**, and the rescue mechanism it appeals to does not exist exactly on this lattice. The correct transcription of the analogy is not "spin ↔ charge" but *"the mass step violates the **chirality-linked** charges (T₃, Y) and conserves the electric one"* — which is Leg B.

### I.3 Leg B, step by step

All steps use the hypercharge kernel `gauge.hypercharge.mass_step_doublet_su2xu1y` on random states with random pointwise U(x) ∈ SU(2) and α(x), 40 samples.

**Step 1 — check the charge algebra itself.** Verify Q = T₃ + Y/2 as an exact operator identity on (ν_L, e_L, ν_R, e_R), using exact fractions.
**Result:** exact, no correction term. The notebook's own neutral-charge form from pp.103–104, Q′ = t₃·cotθ − t₀·tanθ, holds to 4.4×10⁻¹⁶.
**Aside:** at the model's sin²θ_W = 1/4 (F138) those coefficients evaluate to √3 and 1/√3, and 1/√3 is c_lat. Recorded as a coincidence; **not claimed**, and nothing rests on it.

**Step 2 — measure the actual charge change of the mass step.** Compute ΔQ, ΔT₃ and ΔY per tick, in the unitary-gauge frame η̃ = U†η where the step is isospin-diagonal.
**Result:** ΔQ = 0 to 2.2×10⁻¹⁶, while ΔT₃ = −ΔY/2 ≠ 0 (up to 2.6×10⁻² per sample).
**Meaning:** the mass step **violates the weak charges and conserves the electric one**. Measured instead on the raw η (before the frame rotation), ΔQ is non-zero — but for a pure-η_e state it is exactly sin²(m·dt)·|b|²·‖η_e‖², which vanishes when the off-diagonal entry b of U vanishes. It is a **frame artifact**, not a physical charge leak.

**Step 3 — test the hoped-for identity with the W.** p.77's strong form requires the mass step's missing charge to be the charge that the W actually carries. F54's charged current is the left-handed bilinear J⁺ = η_ν*·η_e. Compare them tick for tick.
**Result:** **fails**. On the pure-η_e state, J⁺ is identically zero while the raw-frame ΔQ is finite. They are different objects: the raw ΔQ is a mixed-chirality gauge artifact; F54's current is a species-level, Q-conserving vertex. There is no tick-for-tick equality to be had.

**Step 4 — the discriminator.** Set U = I and α = 0. Under β-gauging there is still a mass gap with no field present. Under p.77 there would be no field leg, hence no charge sink, hence the charge-changing part of the mass step should be **forbidden**.
**Result:** the mass step is fully allowed: ΔQ = 5.6×10⁻¹⁷, ΔT₃ = 6.9×10⁻³, ΔY = 1.38×10⁻².
**Meaning:** at U = I there is **no charge-changing component at all** — the step is isospin-diagonal — and the T₃/Y-violating chirality flip is allowed, and *is* the mass. β-gauging is right about the mass gap; p.77's strong reading is not what the model does.

**Step 5 — follow the missing T₃.** Since ΔT₃ = −ΔY/2 identically, ask which direction absorbs it.
**Result:** **hypercharge** — the neutral α/B direction of F41 — not a charged carrier. In unitary gauge the W± plays no part in the bookkeeping of the mass step at all. The off-diagonal entries b of U, which would be the "W± leg", exist only in a frame where U is not diagonal: they are the Goldstone/Stueckelberg directions that gauge away, exactly as F27's Ward identity says.

### I.4 The honest caveat the finding records about itself

ΔT₃ = −ΔY/2 follows **algebraically** from ΔQ = 0 together with Q = T₃ + Y/2, and ΔQ = 0 in unitary gauge is **guaranteed** because the F27 coupling is species-diagonal (ν↔ν, e↔e). So the machine-precision legs confirm that the kernel implements the expected structure — they do not show it could have come out otherwise. The declared control `mass_mixing=charged` reddens exactly the Q leg, confirming that this is a property of the F27 coupling and not of the measurement. **The result is the standard statement** that mass breaks T₃ and Y, preserves Q, and the Goldstones are eaten. No prior art is exceeded.

### I.5 Part I conclusion

- p.77's **weak form** — "conserved charge = fermion part + field part, under the joint transformation ψ → Vψ, U → VU" — is *literally* F27's Ward identity, already in the tree since 2026-05. A relabel: **"intrinsic + field leg" = "ψ + Stueckelberg U"**.
- p.77's **strong form** — the W± as a physical charged field that carries the missing charge — finds no role. The diagonal part's missing T₃ is balanced by the neutral direction; the off-diagonal part is gauge.
- **Does it predict anything about mass?** No. The mass step's ΔT₃ is fixed by m·dt, which is an input. The notebook's own pp.10–11 self-field-energy hierarchy is independent of the partition and untouched by it.
- **F143 constrains it further:** to be a dynamical charge sink the field leg needs its own kinetic term, and the transverse induced wrap stiffness of U is exactly zero (F42's wrap is conjugation), longitudinal 0.16–0.36% of v². The F42/F143 fermion loop cannot supply it. This is F143 applying, not a new no-go.
- **F320 is untouched:** m_W and m_Z are already absolute predictions from ρ = 1 and rank; the partition adds no parameter and changes none.
- **Audit §4.2** (the "A ↔ spin, W ↔ isospin" duality of pp.62–67 and 90) is closed as *recorded, relabel*. It does **not** derive the Weinberg angle; that stays with F138/F49/F45.

**Disposition: closed as a relabel with no predictive content.** Do not reopen.

## Part II — The factorization route, pp.176–182 (F397)

### II.0 The two theorems

**T1 — factorization.** Every *null* 4-vector is literally a spinor outer product. Writing a 4-vector as a 2×2 matrix in the Pauli basis, σ·V = ψψ†, with ψ = (α, β). Componentwise αα* = V₀−V_z, ββ* = V₀+V_z, α*β = −V_x−iV_y, and the determinant — which is the Minkowski norm — is zero automatically.

**T2 — decomposition.** Every *non-null* 4-vector is exactly the sum of two null vectors, V = a + b with a·a = b·b = 0, along a free direction â.

The structural claim: a gauge field is a vector; a vector is two null vectors; a null vector is a spinor bilinear. So a boson is a multi-spinor object **by identity** — no binding, no interaction, no energy. Massless vs massive becomes null vs timelike: one leg vs two, with the two-leg case carrying a free direction the one-leg case does not have. This is a *different kind* of explanation from the de Broglie/Cooper-pair route the model adopted for the photon (notebook pp.5–6 → F69), not a variant of it.

### II.1 Not the audit's §3.3 object

The audit already records a **negative** result about this region of the notebook: pp.161–175's construction, applied to a single spinor, forces longitudinal-only solutions, so "the photon must be a bilinear". That warning concerns the **dynamics** of one spinor — the Weyl operator σ^μ∂_μ acting on a null V. T2 is the **algebra** of decomposing a non-null V. Different objects. The warning is correct about its own object and does not bear on T2; F397 never touches the former.

### II.2 Resolving the source before computing anything

The notebook contradicts itself. Its classification table says *"two possible decompositions for spacelike, only 1 for timelike"*, while the closed form admits a direction â. If â is not free, half the investigation is void, so this was settled first, from the **scans**, not the transcription.

**Result:** â **is** free, an entire 2-sphere. The same pages state that *(t,0)* decomposes as *(t/2, û·t/2) + (t/2, −û·t/2)* **"for any unit vector û"**, and record the light-cone intersection as an ellipse — a 2-surface. The table's "1" is the *canonical* choice â = V̂. Confirmed numerically: the Jacobian of the decomposition with respect to â has rank 2 (singular values 0.921, 0.584).

**Parameter counting agrees:** a is null (3 free parameters), b = V − a is then determined, and requiring b null imposes 1 equation — leaving 2, which is the sphere.

### II.3 An error in the notebook, found and corrected

The boxed closed form on pp.176–182,

> a₀ = (1/2V₀)·(V₀² − |V|² + 2â·V)

is **wrong**. It is dimensionally inconsistent — it adds a quantity of dimension [V²] to one of dimension [V] — and it does not produce a null pair (measured null defect 3.83 at the test seed).

The correction comes from the author's **own preceding line on the same page**, a₀(V₀ − â·V) = ½(V₀² − |V|²), which rearranges to

> **a₀ = (V·V) / (2·(V₀ − â·V)),  a = a₀·(1, â),  b = V − a**

This is exact: null residual 2.7×10⁻¹⁵, with both legs future-pointing (a₀, b₀ > 0) for every â. At â = V̂ it reproduces his own canonical split ½(V₀+|V|)(1, V̂) + ½(V₀−|V|)(1, −V̂) exactly. A 2007 algebra slip, nineteen years unnoticed, now fixed in the tree.

### II.4 The structural results

**Step 1 — T1 is exact.** σ·V = ψψ† verified to 5.8×10⁻¹⁵, with the only freedom being ψ → e^{iθ}ψ.

**Step 2 — what the leg freedom actually is.** For M = σ·V = ΨΨ† with Ψ = (ξ, η), the matrix U = M^(−1/2)Ψ is unitary (residual 1.5×10⁻¹⁴, using a closed-form matrix square root, no eigensolver). So the free direction â is the coset **U(2)/U(1)×U(1) = S²**, and the spinor pair's 8 real parameters split cleanly as 4 (the vector V) + 4 (two for â, two leg phases).

**Step 3 — the covariance gate.** Under a Lorentz transformation, M → SMS†. Decomposing the boosted vector along the boosted direction returns exactly the boosted legs (residual 5.2×10⁻¹⁴, gated at 10⁻¹²). Mechanically: the U(2) leg freedom acts on the **right** of Ψ, Lorentz acts on the **left**, so they commute. **â is a genuine covariant label**, not frame-dependent structure. Consistent with F24. Had this failed, the route would have died here.

**Step 4 — the massless case.** The photon's 4-vector V = (Ω_even, c·k) is null (to leading order), so T1 applies and T2 does not. As V·V → 0 the sphere of decompositions collapses: what remains is only the collinear family a = xV, b = (1−x)V — a one-parameter momentum fraction, and **no â**. This is the precise sense in which a massless boson has one leg and no free direction.

### II.5 The number that came out: the F169 offset

**Step 1 — expand the dispersion.** The audited BCC branch energies are

> ω^±(q) = c|q| ± ε(q) + O(q³),  ε(q) = −q_x·q_y·q_z / (3|q|)

ε is odd, homogeneous of degree 2, and vanishes on the coordinate planes.

**Step 2 — locate the two-body floor in the continuum.** With ε switched off, the two-body threshold at fixed total momentum k is *exactly* the T1 null condition, T(k) = c|k|, and it is reached along the **whole collinear valley** q₁ = x·k, q₂ = (1−x)·k — **flat in x**. Every way of splitting the momentum costs the same.

**Step 3 — let the lattice lift the valley.** Restoring ε tilts it linearly:

> E₀(x) = c|k| + ε(k)·(2x−1) + O(k³)

So Ω_even — which *is* the symmetric split x = ½ — sits exactly at the **zero of the lift**, i.e. at the midpoint of a tilted valley, while the true minimum runs off to the endpoint x ∈ {0, 1}. Hence

> **Ω_even(k) − T(k) = |ε(k)| + O(k³) = |k_x·k_y·k_z| / (3|k|) + O(k³)**

**Step 4 — verify.** The measured ratio to |ε| is 1.054, 1.026, 1.013 as |k| halves three times — converging to 1 with error linear in |k|. On coordinate planes, where ε = 0, the offset drops to exponent 3.00. Cross-checked against 2M random Brillouin-zone points and Nelder–Mead from the zero-energy BZ nodes: nothing sits below.

**Step 5 — what this corrects.** See Part III.

**Honest attribution, as the finding records it:** the coefficient comes from the **lattice dispersion**, not from the factorization; F30 already carried the (111) piece of the same ε. Factorization contributes the *interpretation* — that the flat collinear valley is the null degeneracy of T2, and the lifted minimum is its degenerate endpoint. What is new is the general-direction closed form and its identification with the two-body floor.

### II.6 What the route did not deliver

**The binding coupling g_c — not reached.** T1 is an identity between a *field* and a spinor bilinear. It is silent about the *spectrum*: it carries no coupling, so it neither predicts nor removes g_c = 2.2596. "Zero binding forced by identity" holds only if the photon is *defined* as the bilinear operator — which is a definition, not a statement about the Fock-space state. On that reading the route is **redundant with F168**, which already supplies the structural reason. g_c stays the one external input in this channel.

**â ↔ W± — negative.** Chirality carries no data in T2 at all: the right-handed leg is completely determined by the left one (ψ̃ = σ_y·ψ*, verified to <10⁻¹³). F91's "right-branch weight ≡ 0" is a *label* the algebra cannot see. And a degenerate two-null decomposition (b = 0) requires V to be null; for any timelike V both legs carry strictly positive energy, a₀, b₀ ≥ (V₀−|V|)/2 > 0. **A massive W cannot correspond to an absent leg.**

**â ↔ Z — no correspondence, and *not* an exclusion.** The â-dependence of the leg energy is the full spread |V|, mass-independent and O(k). F91's axial split is ~k³. The review pass correctly flagged the original reading as a **category error**: â is an internal, unobservable label on a decomposition of a *fixed* V, while the axial split is a propagation effect. They are not comparable quantities, so the honest statement is *no matching structure and no mechanism by which â would enter a propagator* — not "excluded".

**U(1) from the phase — negative.** The free phase of T1 **is** local: ψ → e^{iθ(x)}ψ leaves V exactly invariant and shifts the induced connection a_μ = Im(ψ†∂_μψ) by ∂_μθ (residual 2.3×10⁻¹¹). That much was the interesting part. But it is the **Hopf/Berry redundancy** of parametrizing V: it acts trivially on V, and its field strength is the pull-back area form f_μν = ½·n̂·(∂_μn̂ × ∂_νn̂) — **degree 0 in the amplitude of V**, where Maxwell's F is degree 1, and quadratic in gradients. It does not reproduce F68's minimal coupling or F250's gauge pole. Closed negative; the rest of the route survives independently.

**Degree-of-freedom arithmetic.** Null: V has 4−1 = 3 parameters against a spinor's 4 minus 1 phase = 3 ✓. Timelike: 4 against 8, leaving 4 = â (2) + leg phases (2). The review corrected an earlier draft that called these "unrewarded structure": they are the **massive little group**. This is standard massive spinor-helicity — a massive momentum p = λ^I·λ̃_I carries an SU(2) little-group index, and â is the spin-quantization axis of the legs in the rest frame. The route **re-expresses** the 2-vs-3 polarization count; it does not derive it.

## Part III — Corrections landed on 2026-09-22

### III.1 F169's threshold search was wrong, and is now unnecessary

**The defect.** F169's C3 obtained the two-body floor T(k) from `true_threshold`, a **local stochastic descent** starting at the symmetric split p = 0 with a decaying step size.

**Why it failed.** By II.5 the true minimum is at the **collinear endpoint**, far from p = 0 in relative momentum, at the far end of a valley that is **flat at leading order**. A local random-walk descent therefore had nothing to follow. Measured degradation: ratios to |ε| of 1.043, 0.986, 0.876 at |k| = 0.1, 0.05, 0.025 along (111), and in a generic direction such as (321) it returned **no improvement at all** over Ω_even at |k| = 0.025.

**Consequence.** F169's quoted **fit exponent 2.10 was a fit to search noise**, not physics. It is withdrawn.

**The fix — the threshold has a closed form, so no search is needed.** At the endpoint p = ±k/2, one constituent carries all of k and the other carries zero. Since ω^±(0) = arccos(1) = 0 identically, the endpoint values are *exactly* ω⁺(k) and ω⁻(k):

> **T(k) = min( ω⁺(k), ω⁻(k) )**  — exact

Verified at 60-digit precision: E₀(k, k/2) − min_b ω^b(k) is **identically 0**, with all neighbouring points strictly higher. Cross-checked against a deterministic coordinate pattern search from five starts, 2M random BZ points, and Nelder–Mead from the zero-energy BZ nodes.

**Converged numbers.** Exponent **2.007** on |k| ∈ [0.0125, 0.1] along (111) — replacing 2.10. Ratio to the closed-form |ε| is 1.0084 at |k| = 0.05 and → 1 as |k| → 0. The offset is **direction-dependent** and vanishes on the coordinate planes (exponent 3 there); it is *not* a single universal k² law.

**A third error found in the same file.** The module docstring of `photon_bound_state.py` asserted *"for small k the continuum bottom sits AT the symmetric split p = 0, so T(k) = Ω_even(k) exactly"*. This is false, and is contradicted by F169's own C3. Corrected in the same edit.

**What changed in code.** `casim.engine.gauge.photon_bound_state` now exposes `threshold_closed_form(k)` and `threshold_offset_closed_form(k)`. `true_threshold` defaults to the closed form; the original search survives only as `method="stochastic"`, documented as non-convergent and retained purely so this correction is reproducible. The F169 test gained a deterministic pattern-search cross-check and tightened gates (exponent in [1.98, 2.05], ratio to |ε| within 1%, endpoint-vs-pattern-search below 10⁻⁷). **Re-run: 6/6 PASS**, exponent 2.007.

**What did *not* change.** C1, C2, C4, C5 and C6 are untouched. All of them are statements at k = 0, or at fixed finite k about the secular problem, and none used the search. **The claim CL149 stands**: the photon is a normalizable threshold bound state whose masslessness is inherited from gapless constituents. Only the C3 exponent moved.

### III.2 A flag raised, and where it lands

Because T(k) is attained where one constituent carries all the momentum and the other none, the configuration *at* the floor is not the symmetric pair. The spin-1 identification lives at p = 0, **above** T by |ε(k)|. So the phrase "the photon is the marginally bound threshold state" is exact only at k = 0 and to leading order in k.

This does not break anything, and the physical resolution already exists: by F168/F250 the EM channel rides Ω_even through the **gauge/identity structure**, which is why it is non-birefringent — the gauge pole does not descend to the free-continuum floor. F250's §6 has been sharpened with the closed forms accordingly. Recorded in F169 as **C3-note**.

### III.3 The O(k⁴) "null defect" is the dimension-6 operator — derived

F397's R5 measured that the paired photon's lattice 4-momentum is **not exactly null**: V·V = −κ(k̂)·|k|⁴, with the cubic terms cancelling between the two branches, κ(111) = 0.00412, and κ ≈ 0 along a coordinate axis. κ was left underived. It is now derived, and it is **not new physics**.

**Step 1 — expand.** From ω^± = c|q| ± ε(q) + O(q³) and Ω_even(k) = ω⁺(k/2) + ω⁻(k/2), the ε terms cancel exactly (ε is odd, and the two constituents sit at ±k/2 on opposite branches), so Ω_even = c|k| + O(k³). The model's leading photon Lorentz violation is therefore **cubic in the dispersion**, i.e. a dimension-6 operator — which is F246/CL274, already established.

**Step 2 — convert variables.** If Ω_even = c_lat·|k|·(1 + C(k̂)·|k|²) + O(k⁵), then

> V·V = Ω_even² − c_lat²·|k|² = 2·c_lat²·C(k̂)·|k|⁴ + O(k⁶)

so κ = 2·c_lat²·|C|, with c_lat² = 1/3 giving the factor 2/3.

**Step 3 — check against the established coefficient.** CL274's exact rational is C(k̂) = −[(1 − Σᵢk̂ᵢ⁴)/144 + (k̂_x·k̂_y·k̂_z)²/24]. Hence

> **κ(k̂) = (1 − Σᵢ k̂ᵢ⁴)/216 + (k̂_x·k̂_y·k̂_z)²/36**

**Step 4 — verify.** Richardson-extrapolated in t² at 60-digit precision over **16 directions** (10 named + 6 random integer triples): worst absolute difference **2.2×10⁻¹⁶** against κ-values of order 10⁻³. The range is κ ∈ [0, 1/243], maximal along ⟨111⟩ and exactly zero along ⟨100⟩ — matching CL274's stated C-range [−1/162, 0] under the same factor 2/3. The direct dispersion check reproduces C to 10⁻⁶ relative at |k| = 0.02.

**The sentence that matters.** *"The paired photon's lattice 4-momentum is not exactly null"* and *"the model's leading photon Lorentz violation is a dimension-6 operator with coefficient C"* are **the same statement written in two different variables.** The O(k⁴) defect is precisely the quantity the GRB/LHAASO gate and the F107 cell-size adoption were already built around. It is not a newly discovered flaw.

## Part VI — Re-run audit after the F169 fix (2026-09-22, later the same day)

### VI.3 A fourth defect, found by the re-run and *not* caused by it

Auditing which functions recompute T(k) internally turned up a separate problem in the same module, older than today's work.

**What it is.** `critical_coupling` and `threshold_wavefunction` take T as `E.min()` over the L³ relative-momentum grid.

- **At k = 0 this is exact.** The floor is at p = 0, which *is* a grid point, and T(0) = 0 identically.
- **At finite k it is a grid artifact.** The true floor is at the collinear endpoint p = ±k/2, generally not a grid point, so the grid value lands somewhere between the closed-form floor and Ω_even — and **non-monotonically in L**. Measured fraction of the offset still missed at |k| = 0.2 along (111): 1.00, 1.00, 1.00, 1.00, 0.36, 0.25, 0.36 at L = 12, 24, 32, 48, 96, 144, 192. It does not converge cleanly over that range, because E₀ carries several near-degenerate minima and which grid point wins depends on commensurability between the grid and the narrow valley. Nothing ever falls *below* the closed form, consistent with F397's exhaustive search.

**What it does and does not touch.**
- **C1, C2, C5, C6 — unaffected.** All are evaluated at k = 0, where the grid threshold is exact. **The quoted g_c = 2.2596 is a k = 0 value and stands.**
- **C4 — conclusion unaffected.** C4 compares two diagonalisations of the *same* H = diag(E₀) − g|c⟩⟨c|; T enters only through the arbitrary scale g = 1.5·g_loc. The two-method agreement at 5.6×10⁻¹⁴ tests the **solver**, and remains valid.
- **What is not warranted** is reading the finite-k `critical_coupling` output as *the marginal coupling*, or the finite-k T as *the continuum threshold*.

**Disposition: documented, not repaired.** Substituting the closed-form T at finite k would change g_c and every quantity scaled by it — a physics change, not a bug fix, and it belongs in a finding of its own with its own review pass. In the meantime the artifact is made **visible rather than silent**: the test now records `offset_fraction_missed` and `grid_never_below_closed_form` in its result artifact, both module docstrings carry the caveat, F169 carries a **C2/C4-note**, and ledger record S24 names it.

**Repaired, 2026-09-23 — [F401](../../findings/F401-photon-bound-state-finite-k-threshold-repair.md).** `critical_coupling`/`threshold_wavefunction` now accept `threshold="closed"` (default stays `"grid"`, so this section's own diagnostic keeps running unchanged). Two results: an EXACT identity along coordinate axes (both chiral branches collapse to the same isotropic cone ω±(q,0,0)=|q|/√3, so Ω_even(k)=T(k) to *all* orders on-axis, not just O(k³) as the general off-axis formula gives — the structural reason this section's own axis-adjacent convergence numbers above are noisier than a generic direction); and clean, monotonic L-convergence along (111) at these SAME L values, with the re-derived g_c(k) decreasing monotonically from 2.2596 (k=0) to 1.9738 at |k|=0.4. Scope, stated plainly: this is the Koster–Slater floor coupling at T(k), not necessarily the physical photon's own finite-k coupling (which per F168/F250 rides Ω_even(k), above T(k) generically) — that harder question remains open.

*A methodological note worth keeping.* The first version of this caveat, written from two spot checks, asserted that the grid minimum simply sits at p = 0 so that T_grid ≡ Ω_even, and estimated "L > 108 at |k| = 0.4" for resolving the endpoint. Both were wrong: a proper sweep over L = 12…192 showed the argmin is often *elsewhere* in the Brillouin zone, and that the error is non-monotonic rather than converging. The wrong version had already been written into the module docstring and the test before the sweep caught it. Two spot checks are not a scan.
