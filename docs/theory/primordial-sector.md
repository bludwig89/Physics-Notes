## 1. The question this document answers

Standard cosmology hands four jobs to inflation: explain the horizon (why causally disconnected patches match), explain flatness, produce the primordial spectrum $P(k)$, and dilute unwanted relics. The model was found (2026-08-02 completeness sweep, row K4) to have no inflaton sector at all. Three findings then established:

- **F282** — it cannot have one. Every scalar direction fails slow roll by $O(10)$, for one exact reason.
- **F283** — the obvious escape, an elastic lattice with a variable spacing, is excluded four ways *and* would not have helped: the obstruction is exactly scale-invariant.
- **F284** — so the substrate is rigid, and cosmic expansion is the conformal mode of the dielectric $K$ over a fixed lattice.

This document says what that leaves as an account of the early universe, and — more importantly — **what it costs**.

## 2. The picture, in one paragraph

The BCC lattice is eternal, rigid, and unchanging. Its spacing $a$ is a constant of the substrate, not a dynamical variable, and F79 makes Newton's constant a *consequence* of it ($G=a^2c^3/8\pi\sqrt3\hbar$). What we call cosmic expansion is the evolution of the emergent metric's conformal factor $K$ — a field living *on* the lattice — which assigns growing proper distance to a fixed number of cells. Redshift is dielectric, not metric-stretching. The Big Bang is a hot, dense **state** of the automaton, not a creation event for the substrate; the earliest resolvable epoch is the one whose Hubble radius is a single cell, at $H_\text{max}=3^{-3/4}M_\text{Pl}$, one cell-crossing ($\sqrt3$ ticks) after which the lattice can represent structure at all. The primordial power spectrum is part of the automaton's **initial state**: specified at $t=0$ across the whole lattice, not generated by any subsequent process.

## 4. The one number that distinguishes this from "space itself expands"

$$\dot G/G\equiv0\quad\text{(rigid substrate)}\qquad\text{vs}\qquad \dot G/G=2H_0=1.38\times10^{-10}\,\text{yr}^{-1}\quad\text{(comoving lattice)}.$$

This matters because it is not a fitted small number — it is *exactly zero*, forced by $G$ being structural. Lunar laser ranging (Hofmann & Müller 2018) bounds $\lvert\dot G/G\rvert<1.5\times10^{-13}\,\text{yr}^{-1}$, already 919× inside the comoving prediction; BBN independently agrees, and a fully comoving lattice misses BBN by **17.2 decades**. The elastic fraction is pinned at $q\lesssim10^{-3}$: **the lattice is rigid to about 0.1%.**

Any credible detection of $\dot G\ne0$ falsifies the gravity sector outright. There is no parameter to absorb it.

## 6. What "initial condition" is allowed to mean

The move made here — the spectrum is in the initial state — is only respectable if it is constrained. It is worth being precise about what it does and does not license.

**It is legitimate because:** a cellular automaton's state at $t=0$ is a primitive of the theory, specified globally. It is the one object that can carry super-horizon correlations without a causal mechanism, which is exactly the property inflation exists to manufacture. F282 shows the alternative (a field-theoretic generator) is closed, so this is *forced* rather than chosen.

**It is not a licence to:** fit $P(k)$ freely and call it explained. Three constraints already bind it:

1. **It must be one function, not many.** $A_s$, $n_s$, the running, *and* F238's $\beta$ at the PBH scale must all come from the same $P(k)$. F238 already showed the small-scale amplitude must be $\sim2\times10^6$ above the CMB value — that is a strong internal consistency requirement on a single free function, not five free numbers.
2. **It must be compatible with a fixed mode set.** Modes are not created; the spectrum is defined on the lattice's own $k$-space, cut off at the Brillouin zone. That is a real restriction on admissible $P(k)$ and has not been explored.
3. **It should not require super-horizon *causal* structure.** Causal-seed / active-source models are excluded by the observed acoustic-peak phase coherence, so the initial state must be laid down coherently, not built by a process.

> **The hardest observation for this model is not $n_s\approx1$. It is $n_s\ne1$.** Near-scale-invariance is cheap — any scale-free initial condition gives it. The 3.5% tilt is the expensive part, and it needs exactly the slowly-evolving scale F282 excluded.

**What this changed in the ledger.** §3 previously listed $P(k)$ as "a free function", which is unfalsifiable and therefore cheap. It is now **one number with a named cause**: the model has no principle that picks 0.9649 over 1. That is a worse-looking but far more useful position.

## 8. The second scale must be a logarithm (F286)

F286 attacked the residual with a measurement this account had not been using: **Planck's *running*, not just its index.** If the tilt depends on a second scale through $x=k\xi$, then $n_s-1=F(x)$ and $dn_s/d\ln k=xF'(x)$, so the ratio of two measured numbers bounds the *shape*:

$$\left|\frac{d\ln F}{d\ln x}\right|=\frac{|dn_s/d\ln k|}{|n_s-1|}<\frac{0.0067+0.0045}{0.0351}=0.32.$$

| Shape | Value | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A **length** $\xi$: $F=Cx^p$ | $=p$, integer ($p=2$ for the lattice) | **excluded** |
| A **logarithm**: $F=C/\ln(k_\text{UV}/k)$ | $=1/L=0.0075$ | **allowed** |

> **Every length scale is excluded by the shape of its $k$-dependence, not by its distance.** This is stronger than §7's "58 decades away" and it retires §7's first suggested direction outright: a condensate correlation length surviving to cosmological scales **would not help even if one existed**. The search space is now one-dimensional.

**The class prediction.** Any log-type mechanism anchored at the lattice gives, with no free parameter,

$$\frac{dn_s}{d\ln k}=-\frac{1-n_s}{L}=-2.62\times10^{-4},\qquad L=58.26\ln10=134.15,$$

using §7's own separation. It is 25.6× below Planck's current 1σ — a target, not a test.

**The magnitude is not the problem.** The required coefficient is $C=(1-n_s)L=4.709$, an ordinary $O(1)$. So the tilt is **exactly one one-loop logarithm across the model's own 58 decades with a natural coefficient**. What is missing is the loop, not a small number.

**But nothing supplies the loop.** $\alpha$ is 30× short and barely runs at CMB momenta; $\alpha_s$ is 38 decades into confinement there; block-spin leaves the index exactly marginal (§7 direction 3); and $G$ **cannot** run — F79 makes it structural and F284 predicts $\dot G/G=0$ exactly.

> **The irony, stated plainly.** The model's sharpest and cleanest prediction ($\dot G/G\equiv0$, §4) is precisely what removes its most natural candidate second scale. **The rigidity that makes this account clean is the rigidity that makes the tilt unreachable.** A future attempt should expect that trade rather than be surprised by it.

**A coincidence, recorded and rejected.** $1-n_s=\delta^*/2\pi=1/(9\pi)=0.0353678$ sits 0.064σ from Planck, and its shape is the right kind of object — a one-loop anomalous dimension of the exact $O_h$ weight $\delta^*=\tfrac29$ (F175). It is **not evidence**: over a family fixed *before* looking, 396 candidates give **6** hits in the 1σ window, so $p=0.32$ that something lands this close by chance. Recorded as a coincidence under the F253/F256 standard. It would become evidence only if a mechanism predicted the shape **first** — which is the order of operations the class prediction above exists to enforce.
