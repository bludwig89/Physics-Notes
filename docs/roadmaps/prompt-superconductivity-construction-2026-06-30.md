**Task 1 — derive the model-native pairing glue (the attractive channel).**
An electron gas has repulsive Coulomb; superconductivity needs a *net attraction* in the spin-singlet s-wave channel. This is a fundamental-physics lattice, **not** a material crystal, so there is no assumed ionic phonon — the glue must be derived from the model's own content. Evaluate the three candidates and settle which one (or combination) gives a net attraction, algebraically where possible:
  1. **Dielectric over-screening (lead candidate).** Reuse the lattice dielectric $K$ (F64) and its gap-coupled, *retarded* form (F117). A frequency-dependent $K(\omega, q)$ that over-screens (crosses $\varepsilon<0$ / $\mathrm{Re}\,\varepsilon(q,\omega)<0$ in a window below a characteristic lattice frequency) yields a net attraction in exactly the Fröhlich-retarded way, with the lattice's own $\omega_D$-analog as the cutoff. Derive the sign and the window.
  2. **Lattice-phonon analog.** The CA grid has vibrational/displacement modes (block-spin / F130–F134 machinery exposes a coarse elastic sector). Ask whether these supply a genuine retarded Fröhlich coupling $g^2 \omega_q/(\omega^2-\omega_q^2)$ that is attractive for $\omega<\omega_q$. Identify the model's Debye-analog cutoff $\omega_D^{\text{lat}}$ from the BZ edge.
  3. **Retarded EM / paired-spinor channel.** Whether the F69 pairing (which is *already* the attractive bound channel for the neutral scalar) extends to two like-charge electrons once retardation + the dielectric are included.

Deliverable: a derived attractive kernel $V_{\text{eff}}(q,\omega) < 0$ in the singlet s-wave channel with an explicit lattice cutoff. State which mechanism wins and why; if more than one contributes, give the combined kernel. Success criterion: sign of $V_{\text{eff}}$ settled analytically at small $q$; the cutoff $\omega_D^{\text{lat}}$ tied to a lattice quantity (BZ edge / $c_{\text{lat}}$), not fit. **Falsification:** if every candidate is net-repulsive in the s-wave channel, the model does not superconduct conventionally — document as a no-go and pivot to whether an unconventional (non-s-wave, e.g. d-wave from BCC anisotropy) channel is attractive instead.

**Task 2 — the BCS gap equation as the F77 NJL gap; solve for $\Delta(T)$, $T_c$, and the universal ratios.**

Deliver:

  - The coherence length $\xi_0 = \hbar v_F/\pi\Delta(0)$ in lattice units, then SI via the F107/F123 canonical cell.
Success criterion: both universal ratios reproduced to machine precision from the gap equation alone (independent of coupling), $T_c$ and $\Delta$ derived (not fit) from Task 1's kernel.
