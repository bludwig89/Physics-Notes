Chapter 2 established, by abstract algebra, that the model's update is generated by iterating a
single object — the commutant of the free walk contains nothing but shifts and powers of one
matrix $A$ (R2.6–R2.9) — and fixed the concrete lattice that matrix lives on (BCC, $a=2/\sqrt3$,
R2.10–R2.13). This chapter is where that abstract object becomes a literal recipe: it writes down
$A(\mathbf k)$ explicitly, shows the single Fourier-space multiplication it performs is exactly the
unitary rotation-propagator Ludwig's own notes derive by hand at p.37–39, shows the recipe is local
and bounded in the sense Chapter 4 will need, and then reports the one place — not in this exact
free-walk construction itself, but in its natural generalization to a *position-dependent*
propagation speed — where the naive implementation of "the same idea" silently failed at
zeroth order, and what F276 had to add to fix it.

## 3.1 Inputs

**Postulates used.** **P1** (discreteness — the update acts once per indivisible tick). **P2**
(locality — the update must be, and is shown to be, a finite-support operator). **P3**
(homogeneity/isotropy — the update is the same operator at every site, and Ch.2's R2.12/R2.13
already fixed exactly how much point-group covariance it can and cannot exactly carry). **P4**
(linearity, exact unitarity, $\mathcal U=e^{-iH}$ for Hermitian $H$ — this chapter exhibits $H$
explicitly for the free walk). **P5** (the per-cell field is $\psi\in\mathbb C^2$ — the object the
update acts on). **P6** is not used directly in this chapter — it enters only through Ch.2's R2.2
lower bound, which is a Ch.2 result, not re-invoked here. **P7** is not invoked; nothing in this
chapter involves choosing among several equally-consistent constructions on aesthetic grounds.

**Prior results used, precisely.** **R2.2** (the Bloch decomposition $A(\mathbf k)=u(\mathbf
k)\mathbb I-i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\tilde{\mathbf n}(\mathbf k)$, $u^2+\lVert\tilde{\mathbf
n}\rVert^2=1$, §2.2 of Ch.2 — the object this chapter unpacks into a real-space recipe). **R2.6–R2.9**
(the commutant computation: $d_\text{time}=1$, the shift/power-of-$A$ split, no second candidate

and its $O_h$ infrared limit — carried over as the covariance property the concrete stepper of

(`01-postulates-and-ontology.md` §1.2, P4, citing `physics-notes-complete.md` p.39) is the same
numerical fact this chapter reports in full rather than in summary.

**Free inputs consumed, stated plainly.** Two, of different character:

1. **A discrete residual labelling choice survives inside the free-walk construction, and it is
   not fixed by unitarity, locality or the point group alone.** F344's exhaustive search (cited via
   Ch.2 R2.12) found 576 unitary sign conventions realizing the same $D_{2h}$/$D_{4h}$ covariance
   class, and BDPT's own uniqueness theorem (imported at Ch.2 §2.1, item 2) states the walk is
   unique only "up to conjugation and the chirality sign." The overall $U(1)$ phase $\zeta$ of
   R2.6a is physically inert (a global phase on $\psi$), but the **chirality sign** — which of the
   two mirror-image walks is "the" left-handed one this chapter writes down — is a genuine binary
   labelling choice the algebra does not resolve; §3.2 states the shipped convention explicitly
   and flags that the mirror convention is an equally admissible walk, not a different physical
   theory.
2. **The operator-ordering question for the position-dependent generalization of §3.5 looks, on
   its face, like a free implementation choice, but F276's result is that it is not one.** Only the
   Weyl-symmetric ordering is anti-Hermitian; every other symmetrization of the same generator
   either fails unitarity outright or (as measured) fails it in a way no step-size refinement
   repairs. This is recorded here as a *false* free input — an apparent degree of freedom that
   collapses to a unique forced choice once P4 (exact unitarity) is actually imposed — rather than
   silently presented as if it had never looked free.

## 3.2 The derivation

### 3.2.1 The concrete free-walk stepper

Chapter 2 fixed the Bloch-space form of the update restricted to the free single-particle sector,
$A(\mathbf k)=u(\mathbf k)\mathbb I-i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\tilde{\mathbf n}(\mathbf k)$ (R2.2), on
the BCC lattice with hop vectors $\boldsymbol\delta_{\mathbf d}=\mathbf d/\sqrt3$,
$\mathbf d\in\{\pm1\}^3$ (R2.10). Writing $c_i=\cos(k_i/\sqrt3)$, $s_i=\sin(k_i/\sqrt3)$, the
shipped convention (F344 §2, carried into this chapter without re-derivation, since Ch.2 already
adopts it via R2.12) is

$$u(\mathbf k)=c_xc_yc_z+s\,s_xs_ys_z,\qquad
\tilde n_a(\mathbf k)=p_aM_a^{(1)}+q_aM_a^{(2)},\qquad
p=(+1,-s,+1),\ q=(-s,+1,+s),\tag{R3.1}$$

with $s=\pm1$ the chirality branch label (free input 1 above), $M_a^{(1)}$ the monomial carrying a
single sine in slot $a$ (the $O(k)$, leading-order term) and $M_a^{(2)}$ the monomial carrying a
single cosine in slot $a$ (the $O(k^2)$ term). This is the entire content of the abstract $A$ of
R2.6–R2.9, written out. $u^2+\lVert\tilde{\mathbf n}\rVert^2=1$ identically (F344 leg C1, exact —
the eight squared monomials are an exact rank-8 basis over $\mathbb Q$), so $A(\mathbf k)$ is
exactly unitary for every $\mathbf k$, with no approximation and no step-size dependence: it is a
finite trigonometric polynomial in $\mathbf k$, not a truncation of one.

**The real-space recipe.** Because $A(\mathbf k)$ is, by construction, a finite sum of the eight
Bloch monomials $e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf d/\sqrt3}$, $\mathbf d\in\{\pm1\}^3$, its inverse Fourier
transform is a finite sum over exactly those eight real-space neighbor displacements. One tick of
evolution is

$$\psi(\mathbf x,t+1)=\sum_{\mathbf d\in\{\pm1\}^3}M_{\mathbf d}\,\psi\!\left(\mathbf x+\frac{\mathbf d}{\sqrt3},\,t\right),\tag{R3.2}$$

where the eight fixed $2\times2$ matrices $M_{\mathbf d}$ are read off from (R3.1) by matching
Bloch monomials to hop directions ($\sum_{\mathbf d}M_{\mathbf d}\,e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf
d/\sqrt3}=A(\mathbf k)$). This is the literal, mechanical one-tick update: at every site, take the
spinor values currently sitting at the eight body-diagonal BCC neighbors, apply eight fixed
$2\times2$ unitary-weighted matrices to them, and sum. Nothing about (R3.2) refers to the site
$\mathbf x$ itself — homogeneity (P3) is exact, not approximate, in this construction, and nothing
in it refers to any site beyond the eight listed — locality (P2) is likewise exact: the update's
support is the fixed 8-element neighbor set of Ch.1 P2's own definition, verbatim, with no larger
or smaller set possible once R2.10's BCC connectivity is fixed.

$$\boxed{\;\text{one tick}=\text{a fixed linear combination of the eight BCC body-diagonal neighbors, exactly (R3.2)}\;}\tag{R3.3}$$

### 3.2.2 This is Ludwig's split-step propagator, generalized from cubic to BCC

Ludwig's own notes derive the identical *kind* of object independently, on the simpler cubic
lattice, and the identification is worth making explicit rather than left as a coincidence of
notation. Starting from the naive second-order (two-previous-tick) discretized wave equation
(`physics-notes-complete.md` p.37) and making "the Dirac reduction" to a spinor field so that only
$t-1$ is needed (Ch.1 P5's own sourcing), the notes arrive at the continuum equation
$\partial_t\psi=-\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\nabla\psi$ (p.38) and then test two discretizations of it
numerically (p.39, "Simulation notes"): an explicit-Euler centered-difference scheme, found
"unconditionally unstable… consistent with the general result that explicit Euler applied to a
skew-Hermitian operator is always unstable," and the exact per-mode unitary

$$U(\mathbf k)=\cos(c\kappa)\,\mathbb I-\frac{i\sin(c\kappa)}{\kappa}(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf k),\qquad\kappa=\lvert\mathbf k\rvert,\tag{cited, p.39}$$

reported "exactly unitary for all $c$," with time-reversal residuals at machine precision and norm
conserved to double precision at every step. (R3.1) is the *same construction* — a per-mode
$2\times2$ unitary of the form $\cos\theta\,\mathbb I-i\sin\theta\,(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot
\boldsymbol\sigma)$, i.e. an exponentiated rotation generator $H(\mathbf k)=\theta(\mathbf
k)\,\hat{\mathbf n}(\mathbf k)\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$ with $\mathcal U(\mathbf k)=e^{-iH(\mathbf
k)}$ (Ch.1 P4's $\mathcal U=e^{-iH}$, made explicit) — evaluated not on the continuum/cubic
dispersion $\kappa=\lvert\mathbf k\rvert$ but on the BCC lattice's own finite trigonometric
polynomial $u(\mathbf k)=\cos\theta(\mathbf k)$, $\tilde{\mathbf n}(\mathbf k)=\sin\theta(\mathbf
k)\,\hat{\mathbf n}(\mathbf k)$. The rotation angle $\theta(\mathbf k)=\arccos u(\mathbf k)$ is
exactly the single-branch dispersion $\omega(\mathbf k)$ that Chapter 6 will read $c_\text{lat}$ off
of (CLAUDE.md Core Design Decision 2, flagged as a forward reservation in Ch.1's notation table and
as Gap G-2's second premise in Ch.2 §2.1) — that identification is Chapter 6's derivation to carry
out in full, not this chapter's, and is only pointed at here so the reader sees where $\theta(\mathbf
k)$ goes next.

$$\boxed{\;A(\mathbf k)=e^{-iH(\mathbf k)},\quad H(\mathbf k)=\theta(\mathbf k)\,\hat{\mathbf n}(\mathbf k)\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma,\quad \theta=\arccos u\;}\tag{R3.4}$$

### 3.2.3 Time, concretely: the tick is "apply $A$ once"

Chapter 2's commutant computation (R2.6–R2.9) is an abstract statement about which operators
commute with the update inside the ring of local homogeneous unitaries. Its operational content, in
the vocabulary of §3.2.1, is now literal: $\ker(\det)=\{\zeta A^n:n\in\mathbb Z\}$ (R2.6b) says that
the *only* homogeneous local unitaries obtained by repeating a single step are the integer powers
of the one matrix (R3.1) already written down, up to an inert overall phase. "Time" is not a fifth
coordinate glued on afterward; it is, by construction, the index $n$ counting how many times (R3.2)
has been applied. There is no other commuting flow to apply instead (R2.9), and the flow does not
survive as a *distinct* symmetry once genuine interaction is switched on (R2.8) — the free walk's
apparent branch-doubled second flow $V$ is an artifact of freeness, not a second clock. This
chapter adds nothing to that argument; it only states plainly what "the update commutant singles
out shift-by-one-tick as the generator" (the chapter's own charge, §0 above) cashes out to
mechanically: one tick of the model **is** one application of (R3.2), full stop, and every other
homogeneous local unitary reachable by the free dynamics is a power of it.

$$\boxed{\;t\mapsto t+1\ \equiv\ \psi\mapsto A\psi,\ \text{literally, with no other commuting generator available (R2.6–R2.9)}\;}\tag{R3.5}$$

### 3.2.4 Locality and boundedness, stated for Chapter 4's use

Two properties Chapter 4 (reflection positivity, CPT, spin–statistics, cluster decomposition) needs
cited rather than re-derived are exact and immediate from (R3.2)–(R3.5), and are recorded here so
that chapter can simply invoke them:

- **Finite causal cone.** By (R3.2), the value at $\mathbf x$ after $n$ ticks depends only on the
  values at sites within $n$ BCC hops of $\mathbf x$ at $t=0$ — a finite, linearly growing light
  cone, exactly bounded by the hop length $\lvert\boldsymbol\delta\rvert=1$ (R2.10) per tick. No
  influence propagates faster than one hop per tick, by construction, not by an approximate bound.
- **Bounded, norm-preserving generator.** $A(\mathbf k)$ is unitary for every $\mathbf k$ (§3.2.1),
  so the update is an isometry of $\ell^2$: $\lVert\mathcal U\psi\rVert=\lVert\psi\rVert$ exactly,
  for every $\psi$, at every tick — not merely conserved on average or asymptotically. Both
  properties hold with **zero residual**, because (R3.2) is an exact finite sum, not a truncated
  or iteratively-refined scheme; there is no discretization-error budget to report for the free
  walk itself (contrast §3.5 below, where one appears the moment the speed is allowed to vary).

$$\boxed{\;\text{exact locality (finite causal cone, radius 1/tick) and exact unitarity, with no residual, for the free walk (R3.2)}\;}\tag{R3.6}$$

### 3.2.5 Point-group covariance, carried over rather than re-derived

Ch.2's R2.12/R2.13 already settled exactly how much of the point group this stepper carries: the
shipped convention (R3.1) realizes $D_{2h}$ (order 8) under strict unitary covariance, $D_{4h}$
(order 16) once step-reversal-accompanied rotations are admitted, and never the full $O_h$ (order
48) of the neighbor shell's geometry — with the $C_3$-violating residual an exact $O(k^2)$ effect
that vanishes at leading order (F344 §4, cited via Ch.2 R2.13). This chapter does not repeat that
computation; it notes only that (R3.2) is the concrete object R2.12/R2.13 are statements *about*,
so nothing further needs checking here — a reader wanting the covariance proof itself is pointed
back to Ch.2 §2.3.7, not asked to redo it.

### 3.2.6 The stepper generalized: position-dependent propagation speed

Everything above holds for the **free** walk, where $u(\mathbf k)$ and $\tilde{\mathbf n}(\mathbf
k)$ are fixed functions of momentum alone. Later chapters need a generalization in which the
propagation speed itself varies with position — a gravitational dielectric $K(\mathbf x)$ (forward
to Ch.18) or, more generally, any position- or field-dependent coupling. Write the continuum
generator this generalizes, following the same $H=c\,\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat p$ form as (R3.4),
with $c\to c(\mathbf x)=c_0+\delta c(\mathbf x)$:

$$H(\mathbf x)=c(\mathbf x)\,\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat p,\qquad \hat p=-i\nabla.\tag{setup}$$

Because $\delta c(\mathbf x)$ and $\hat p$ do not commute, $A(\mathbf k)$'s clean per-mode
diagonalization (§3.2.1) is no longer available in closed form, and the engine instead
Strang-composes an exact FFT step for the constant part $c_0$ with a half-step for the
inhomogeneous remainder, $\delta H=-\delta c(\mathbf x)\,\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat p=:W$ up to sign
(F276 §"The two corrections"; this is the module `casim.engine.lattice.curved`,
`_half_step_dH`/`weyl_step_2d_varc_strang`, implemented on the engine's 2-D **square**-lattice
core, `lattice.core.weyl_step_2d_splitstep` — the "reference implementation, continuum-limit
regression target, not canonical (D1)" banner-labelled module (`src/casim/engine/lattice/core.py`
line 7), not the canonical BCC construction of §3.2.1–§3.2.5. §3.6 below states precisely what this
means for how far the correction is shown to reach.)

**Two defects in the naive implementation, both diagnosed and fixed by F276.**

**(i) Ordering.** $\delta c(\mathbf x)$ is a position operator (Hermitian) and $\boldsymbol\sigma
\cdot\hat p$ is anti-Hermitian; their bare product is neither. The pre-F276 code used the
asymmetric $W_\text{asym}=\delta c\,(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\nabla)$, whose half-step $e^{-hW_\text{
asym}}$ is therefore **not unitary at any step size** — the non-unitarity lives in the generator
itself, not in a discretization of it. The Weyl-symmetric choice

$$W=\tfrac12\{\delta c,\ \boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\nabla\}\tag{R3.7}$$

is anti-Hermitian by construction (the anticommutator of a Hermitian and an anti-Hermitian operator
is anti-Hermitian), so $e^{-hW}$ is exactly unitary for every $h$. This is the "false free input"
flagged in §3.1: among symmetrizations of $\delta c\,\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat p$, only the
Weyl-ordered one is anti-Hermitian, so P4 (exact unitarity) does not leave a choice here — it forces
(R3.7).

**(ii) Truncation order.** Even with (R3.7), keeping only $\mathbb 1-hW$ (the code's prior
truncation of $e^{-hW}$) leaves an $O(h^2)$ local error that the surrounding Strang composition
does **not** cancel, making the scheme globally first order at best. Restoring the next term,

$$e^{-hW}=\mathbb 1-hW+\tfrac{h^2}2W^2+O(h^3),\tag{R3.8}$$

costs one further application of the same operator $W$ and is what a second-order Strang split was
always supposed to deliver.

**Measured effect (F276 Results 1–2, reproduced from `findings/F276-curved-weyl-ordering-second-order.md`
without re-derivation — cross-checked against the finding's own tables, which are the source for
these numbers; no independent result-JSON re-run was performed for this chapter, per the
documentation-only scope stated in the build instructions).** At $L=64$, 20 ticks, $\delta
c=0.05\tanh(x/8)$: the pre-correction (asymmetric, first-order) scheme's norm-drift ratio under
$32\times$ sub-step refinement is $1.00$ — a **plateau**, not a shrinking error; ordering alone
((R3.7) without (R3.8)) converts this to $1/n_\text{sub}$ convergence (ratio $2.00$); adding
(R3.8) converts it to $1/n_\text{sub}^3$ (ratio $\approx8.00$), reaching $1.3\times10^{-12}$ at
$n_\text{sub}=32$ — the project's own machine-precision gate. Self-convergence against a
$n_\text{sub}=512$ reference shows the **global order** of the scheme moves from a measured
**$0.00,\,0.00,\,0.00$** (pre-F276: sub-stepping does not reduce the solution error at all — it
converges, but to the wrong operator, at a fixed $\approx2.4\times10^{-2}$ offset) to **$2.00,\,
2.00,\,2.00$** (F276, Weyl-ordered, second order) — the sharpest single number in the finding, since
"global order zero" is not "less accurate than hoped," it is "refining the step size buys nothing."

$$\boxed{\;\text{naive variable-}c\text{ stepper: unitary error plateaus (never removable by refinement); global order}=0.00\;}\tag{R3.9}$$

$$\boxed{\;\text{Weyl-ordered + }h^2W^2/2\text{ stepper: norm error}\sim1/n_\text{sub}^3\text{; global order}=2.00\text{, exactly, and exactly unitary}\;}\tag{R3.10}$$

(F276 Results 1–2, gate-tier test `F276-curved-weyl-ordering-second-order` — see §3.6 for its exact
scope and the one declared control.)

### 3.2.7 Why this generalizes beyond gravity, and what it forces on later chapters

The variable-speed construction of §3.2.6 is not gravity-specific: $c(\mathbf x)\to c_0+\delta
c(\mathbf x)$ is the generic pattern for *any* position- or field-dependent modification of the
free stepper's rotation rate — a gauge coupling that varies in space, a dielectric background, or
any other mechanism later chapters build by perturbing (R3.4)'s generator rather than replacing it
outright. F276's result is therefore a standing constraint on every such later construction, stated
once here rather than re-derived per chapter: **a naive (asymmetric-ordered, first-order-truncated)
discretization of a variable-rate rotation generator is not merely less accurate — it can be
globally zeroth order and non-unitary in a way no refinement fixes**, and the remedy (Weyl-symmetric
ordering, plus the generator's own $h^2W^2/2$ term) is forced by requiring exact anti-Hermiticity,
not chosen by taste. Chapter 18 (gravity, the $K=\exp(2GM/rc^2)$ dielectric) and any later gauge-field
chapter that varies a coupling in space inherit this constraint directly.

## 3.4 Comparison with measurement

Not applicable at this level, and this is stated honestly rather than papered over: nothing in this
chapter is checked against an experimental number. The free-walk stepper (R3.1–R3.6) is an exact
algebraic/internal-consistency construction — it is *the* object later chapters compute
predictions from, not itself a prediction. R3.9/R3.10 are numerical-methods results (a
discretization scheme's convergence order and unitarity residual), checked against the scheme's own
exact-unitary target, not against data. The two places this chapter's content eventually meets
measurement are both forward references, not this chapter's own comparisons: **Chapter 6** derives

resulting Lorentz-invariance-violation coefficients into the observational bounds (GRB time-of-flight,

  the general result that explicit Euler applied to a skew-Hermitian operator is always unstable"
  (p.39, Simulation notes). This is not this chapter's own result — it is Ludwig's own numerical
  finding, cited as the historical reason the split-step/exact-rotation construction (R3.4) was
  adopted at all rather than the naive finite-difference update.
- **The asymmetric-ordered, first-order-truncated variable-$c$ generalization** (§3.2.6, pre-F276).
  Excluded by R3.9: it is not an approximation with a controllable error budget, it is a scheme
  whose norm-conservation defect *lives in the generator* and does not shrink under refinement, and
  whose solution error is globally zeroth order. F276 is explicit that "a discretisation error that
  does not fall with refinement is not a discretisation error" — it is evidence the wrong operator
  is being exponentiated.
- **A mirror chirality-sign convention for the free walk.** Not wrong — F344's search found it
  equally unitary and equally $D_{2h}$/$D_{4h}$-covariant — but not the one this chapter or the
  wider tree ships; recorded in §3.1 as a free input rather than presented as if forced.

## 3.6 What is still open

1. **F276's fix is demonstrated on the engine's 2-D square-lattice "reference implementation,
   continuum-limit regression target" module (`lattice.core.weyl_step_2d_splitstep`,
   `lattice.curved`), not on the canonical 3+1 BCC construction of §3.2.1–§3.2.5.** See Gap G-3
   below for why this is flagged rather than silently bridged.
2. **F276 itself records an open item rather than closing it fully**: `curved.measure_refraction`'s
   outgoing-angle diagnostic is wrong by roughly $150°$ under *every* stepper tested, including the
   exactly-unitary `method='cayley'` construction, traced to the lattice's periodic boundary
   letting the wave packet wrap and contaminate the late-time centroid measurement — a defect in
   the measurement, not the propagator, and explicitly unrepaired by F276 ("Recorded as an open
   item, not repaired here"). No published claim in the tree currently rests on that diagnostic
   (F276 §"Result 4"), so nothing this chapter states is undermined by it, but a reader chasing "is
   Snell's law reproduced" should not expect this chapter's stepper to have answered that yet.
3. **"Any F64-fork variable-$c$ number quoted from before 2026-08-01 should be re-run before it is
   used quantitatively"** (F276's own closing instruction) — this chapter does not audit which, if
   any, later-chapter numbers predate that date; it is flagged here as a standing caveat for
   Chapter 18's author to check directly against F276 rather than assume resolved.
4. **The R3.7 "uniqueness" of the Weyl ordering is bounded by what F276 actually compared**
   (asymmetric vs. Weyl-symmetric), not by an exhaustive sweep of every possible operator ordering
   of $\delta c\,\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat p$ — recorded honestly in the results table (R3.7) rather
   than overstated as a completeness proof.

> **Gap [G-3]:** F276's correction (Weyl-symmetric ordering, R3.7, plus the $h^2W^2/2$ truncation
> term, R3.8) is derived and numerically verified on the engine's non-canonical 2-D square-lattice
> spectral module (`casim.engine.lattice.curved`/`lattice.core.weyl_step_2d_splitstep`, banner-labelled
> in `src/casim/engine/lattice/core.py` a "reference implementation, continuum-limit regression
> target, not canonical (D1)"), where the derivative $\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat p$ is a continuum-like
> spectral operator. The canonical stepper this chapter derives in §3.2.1–§3.2.5 is instead the
> discrete 8-neighbor BCC hop (R3.2), where a position-dependent generalization would presumably
> mean letting the eight coefficient matrices $M_{\mathbf d}$ of (R3.2) themselves vary with
> $\mathbf x$, not inserting a continuum $\delta c(\mathbf x)\,\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat p$ term into
> a spectral half-step. Neither F276 nor any other finding checked for this chapter states whether
> the same ordering-plus-truncation argument transfers unchanged to that discrete setting, whether
> it needs modification, or whether the two constructions (spectral variable-$c$ on the regression-
> target lattice; a hypothetical variable-hop BCC stepper) are even the same generalization viewed
> in two bases. This matters directly for Chapter 18 (gravity, the $K(\mathbf x)$ dielectric), which
> is the chapter most likely to need a variable-speed stepper on the *canonical* lattice rather than
> the regression-target one, and it is flagged here rather than assumed bridged. Logged in
> `docs/monograph/GAPS.md`.

## 3.7 Falsifiers

Thin at this level, and this is stated honestly rather than manufactured. The free-walk stepper
(R3.1–R3.6) is an exact construction, checked against itself (unitarity is an algebraic identity,
R3.1; locality is a statement about which sites appear in a finite sum, R3.2) rather than against a
falsifiable numerical prediction distinct from later chapters' — Chapter 2's own falsifiers (§2.8,
items 4 and 7, concerning the commutant and the point-group covariance this chapter's stepper
realizes) are the operative falsifiers for the *content* of R3.1–R3.6, and are not repeated here.
The one falsifier genuinely native to this chapter is F276's own, stated for the variable-speed
generalization of §3.2.6:

- **F276's falsification handle** (quoted from the finding): "If the Weyl-symmetric generator is
  the right one, its norm error must fall as $1/n_\text{sub}^3$ *and* the global order must be
  exactly 2 — both are sharp, and both are measured above. A measured order of 1, or a norm error
  that plateaus at any level above the machine gate, falsifies the construction." This is a
  numerical-methods falsifier (it falsifies a discretization scheme's correctness, not a physical
  prediction), and is recorded as such rather than inflated into a physics falsifier the chapter
  does not otherwise have.
