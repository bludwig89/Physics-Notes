## 1.0 What this chapter establishes

This chapter fixes, once and for all for the rest of the monograph, the list of things that are *assumed* rather than derived — numbered `P1`–`P7` — and the minimal notation every later chapter cites by name. Nothing here is proved; everything here is adopted, on stated grounds (a historical physical argument, an external mathematical uniqueness theorem, or a bare methodological choice), and every later chapter either derives consequences of these postulates or further motivates them. The chapter's second job, inseparable from the first, is to answer precisely what is *physically real* in this model — the **beable**, in 't Hooft's sense — since every later chapter's claims about "what the lattice is doing" presuppose an answer to that question, and getting it wrong here would mis-cast the entire monograph as either a hidden-variable theory it is not, or a purely instrumental calculational device it also is not.

## 1.2 The derivation: the postulates, in dependency order

### P1 — Discreteness of spacetime

> **P1.** The universe is not a spacetime continuum. It consists of a discrete, countable set of "cells" (lattice sites) together with a discrete time parameter: physical evolution proceeds in indivisible steps ("ticks"), not by continuous flow. Continuum space, continuum time, and the differential field equations of classical/quantum field theory are *emergent, coarse-grained descriptions*, valid only at scales far above the fundamental cell spacing — they are not the fundamental ontology.

**Source.** This is the project's founding move, stated as the Core Idea in this project's `CLAUDE.md`: "if our universe is a cosmic lattice of cellular automata... we should be able to model it on a tiny scale on a computer." In Ludwig's own notes it first appears as an open question rather than an assertion: "Would it be possible to model spacetime geometry and elementary particle physics using cellular automata? ... Geometry from rules of interaction is implicit in cellular automata rules" [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.35]. The QCA literature treats the same choice as its own unproven starting axiom: Fredkin's *Digital Mechanics* is "the original CA-as-physics manifesto: a single reversible CA rule is hypothesized to model all of microscopic physics exactly" [`qca-papers-1-4-overview.md`, Paper 5], and Bisio–D'Ariano–Perinotti–Tosini's five informational principles (§P2 below) are applied *to* an already-discrete automaton, not used to derive discreteness itself [`qca-papers-1-4-overview.md`, Paper 1]. 't Hooft's cellular-automaton interpretation of QM makes the identical starting move for different (interpretive, not constructive) reasons [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §3.3].

**Status of the claim.** As flagged in §1.1, this is a free ontological choice, not a derived result — no source offers an argument that spacetime *must* be discrete; the literature and this project both adopt it and then ask what follows. Chapter 7 (LIV bounds) and Chapter 17 (the SI lattice-spacing anchor) are where this postulate becomes, in principle, falsifiable — see §1.5 and §1.8 below.

**Forward pointers.** Chapter 2 derives *which* discrete structure (dimension count, lattice type, point group) is consistent with the further postulates below; Chapter 6 derives the emergent light-cone structure from the discrete update; Chapter 17 sets the numerical scale of "how discrete."

### P2 — Locality

> **P2.** The state of a cell at the next tick depends only on the states of a fixed, finite set of other cells ("neighbors") at the current tick — never on the whole lattice, and never on cells at unboundedly large separation. The neighbor relation is the *primitive* structure; distance and geometry are to be read off it, not assumed in advance of it.

**Source.** Ludwig's notes state this as a general principle before any spinor content is introduced, illustrated with Conway's Game of Life: "The state of $A$ in the next time increment depends on the state of $A$, 1, 2, 3 & 4 *now*. But, that $A$ depends on 1, 2, 3 & 4 and *not* 23, 47, 217, 1098 **implies** that 1, 2, 3 & 4 are neighbors. And the lists of all neighbors creates a... lattice geometry" — summarized in the notes' own boxed maxim, "**Dependence implies neighborhood. Rules imply geometry**" [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.35]. The same requirement is the third of the QCA literature's five informational principles: "(1) linearity, (2) unitarity, (3) locality, (4) homogeneity, (5) isotropy" [`qca-papers-1-4-overview.md`, Paper 1], and underlies the "Cayley graph as emergent geometry" construction in which space and its metric are *derived* from the word-metric on the automaton's own dependency graph, with "no spacetime background... assumed" [`qca-papers-1-4-overview.md`, Paper 1]. Ostoma & Trushyk's independent, non-rigorous CA essay adopts the same locality requirement as its first postulate ("every cell updates from local rules in one global 'clock' tick") [`special-relativity-derived-from-ca-summary.md` §2, P1].

**Forward pointers.** Chapter 2 uses locality (together with P3 below) to derive the specific connectivity (BCC, 8 neighbors) rather than merely asserting it; Chapter 3 states the concrete update rule this bounded neighborhood realizes.

### P3 — Homogeneity and isotropy of the connectivity

> **P3.** The neighbor relation and the local update rule are the same at every cell (homogeneity: no cell is distinguished from any other), and are compatible with the action of some finite point-symmetry group on the neighbor set (isotropy: no *direction* among the allowed neighbor-displacements is distinguished from any other). This fixes only that *some* regular, symmetric lattice with *some* point group exists — it does not by itself fix the number of spatial dimensions, the specific lattice (simple cubic, BCC, ...), or the specific point group. That further selection is a derived result, not a postulate.

**Source.** Ludwig's own notes approach this from the connectivity side rather than the symmetry-group side: "It would appear that a CA can only establish a certain dimensionality based on the number of connections it can establish... **4 connections** can form a 2D lattice or a 3D lattice, or something like a 3D tube or torus" [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.36] — the notes explicitly leave the resulting geometry underdetermined by connection count alone, which is exactly the gap Chapter 2 closes with the additional constraints below. The QCA literature supplies homogeneity and isotropy as the fourth and fifth of its five informational principles, and shows that together with locality, linearity and unitarity (P2, P4) they force the cells onto "the vertices of a **Cayley graph** of some group $G$... [with] a covariance condition: there exists a finite subgroup $L$ of graph automorphisms and a faithful unitary representation $U_l$" satisfying the automaton's own symmetry equation [`qca-papers-1-4-overview.md`, Paper 1, Eq. 5]. It is this same chain of constraints, applied on top of P1–P4, that later forces the *specific* choice of the body-centered-cubic lattice in three spatial dimensions with point group $O_h$ (Chapter 2) — deliberately **not** re-derived or assumed here.

**Forward pointers.** Chapter 2 (dimension count, BCC selection, lattice constant, point symmetry) is entirely the derivation this postulate defers.

### P4 — Linearity and unitarity of the update; the amplitude is physically real

> **P4.** The per-cell state is an element of a complex Hilbert space (not a classical bit, not a real number), and the discrete-tick update is an exactly linear, unitary map on that space: the norm of the total state is conserved tick to tick, and the map is invertible (no information is destroyed by the fundamental rule). This amplitude is treated as *physically real* — a genuine quantum-mechanical superposition, not a bookkeeping device that merely encodes an observer's ignorance of some sharper, always-definite classical value hiding underneath it. This project does **not** additionally postulate a 't Hooft-style single "ontological" classical state of which the amplitude is a mere re-basing; whether such a deeper deterministic layer exists beneath the amplitude is left explicitly open (§1.7).

**Source and the ontology question.** This is the second of the QCA literature's five informational principles ("unitarity") [`qca-papers-1-4-overview.md`, Paper 1], and it is the *quantitative* form of Ludwig's own numerical discovery: an explicit-Euler discretization of the spinor update (P5, below) "is unconditionally unstable... consistent with the general result that explicit Euler applied to a skew-Hermitian operator is always unstable," while the exact split-step propagator $U(\mathbf{k})=\cos(c\kappa)I - \tfrac{i\sin(c\kappa)}{\kappa}(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf{k})$ built from the *same* page-38 equations "is exactly unitary for all $c$" and conserves norm to double precision at every step [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.39, "Simulation notes"]. The project's actual ontological stance — what is real, in 't Hooft's beable/changeable/superimposable vocabulary [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §3.1] — is stated explicitly in the sources rather than left implicit: "Our model does not need [a single ontological basis element] — we evolve the *templates themselves* unitarily on the lattice and reproduce QM directly. We do not commit to whether the per-cell amplitude $\psi(\vec x,t)\in\mathbb C^2$ is a template or an ontic state" [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §5.4, "The biggest single conceptual gap"]. This is the chapter's answer to the beable question, discussed at length in §1.3.

**Forward pointers.** Chapter 3 states the concrete unitary update built from P2–P5. Chapter 4 derives the structural consequences of exact unitarity (reflection positivity, CPT, spin–statistics, cluster decomposition). Chapter 5 is where the *appearance* of definite classical outcomes from this postulated unitary substrate — Born's rule, the pointer basis, measurement, classicality — is derived, per findings F281 (measurement/pointer-basis/Born-rule), and F304/F312/F329 (Gleason-premises work), per this chapter's citation-accuracy check against `docs/monograph/00-plan.md`'s Chapter 5 assignment.

### P5 — The primitive field is a two-component complex (Weyl) spinor

> **P5.** The fundamental content of a cell is not a scalar number, not a classical bit, and not a tensor — it is a two-component complex spinor $\psi(\mathbf{x},t)\in\mathbb{C}^2$, transforming under the lattice's point-group action as a Weyl spinor. At the postulate level this primitive field is massless; mass is not carried by the primitive field itself (see P6).

**Source — two independent lines of argument, both cited, neither invented here.** Ludwig's own route to this postulate is a concrete locality argument, not an appeal to representation theory: starting from the naive discretized scalar wave equation, he observes "the problem with this is that $f(m,n,t+1)$ is *not* dependent just on $f(-,-,t)$ but also on $f(m,n,t-1)$. To get it to depend only on the previous time step we have to make the Dirac reduction and make each cell spinor-valued" [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.37] — i.e. a second-order-in-time (scalar, Klein–Gordon-type) update is incompatible with a strictly first-order, single-previous-tick local rule (P2), and the fix is to carry a spinor rather than a scalar per cell. This is reinforced by a second, independent argument from the mass side: "it makes sense to start with massless spinor fields as the 'basic' matter fields, and to 'clothe' them with mass derived from their interactions" [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.11], following from the observation that "mass is and ought to be generated by the interactions of... particles and their associated fields" [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.10] — motivating a *massless* Weyl-spinor primitive specifically, consistent with P6 below. Independently of both arguments, the QCA literature proves the same conclusion as a uniqueness theorem from the axioms of P2–P4 plus a minimality-of-internal-dimension requirement: "each cell carries a Fermionic field with $s$ components, and $s$ is chosen to be the smallest value admitting a non-trivial evolution. The result: $s=2$ (a 2-spinor) in dimensions 1, 2, 3" [`qca-papers-1-4-overview.md`, Paper 1]. Ludwig's notes independently characterize *what kind of object* a spinor is, geometrically: "A spinor, then, is a direction without a magnitude" [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.110–124, "What is a Spinor?"], mapped one-to-one to a point on the sphere by stereographic projection — a characterization used nowhere else in the sources as a *derivation*, but adopted here as the chapter's answer to "what is a spinor, physically" for later reference.

**Forward pointers.** Chapter 2/Chapter 4 carry the QCA-literature uniqueness proof in full rigor (the $s=2$ result is not re-derived here). Chapter 8 builds the paired-spinor photon directly from two such primitives. Chapter 11 derives how the "clothing" (P6) converts the massless primitive into the observed massive-fermion spectrum.

### P6 — Mass and hypercharge from a postulated chiral $SU(2)$ connection, not a Higgs field

> **P6.** Complex mass is not introduced by a fundamental scalar (Higgs) field with a symmetry-breaking potential. It is introduced by *gauging* an ambiguity already present in how the spinor's own matrix representation is chosen from point to point — promoting that choice to a local $SU(2)$ connection ("Ludwig's chiral $SU(2)$"). Hypercharge is carried on this same connection $U(x)$, not on a separate scalar sector. This is adopted as the project's postulated gauge-theoretic starting point for the entire massive-fermion and electroweak sector; its detailed construction and consequences are derived in full only in Chapters 11–12.

**Source.** This is stated directly as Core Design Decisions 1 and 3 of this project's `CLAUDE.md`: "We are using Ludwig's SU(2) derivation instead of the standard model," and "Hypercharge is included on $U(x)$, avoiding any need for the Higgs field." The construction traces to Ludwig's own 2007 notes: starting from the observation that the Dirac matrices $\vec\alpha,\beta$ used to take the square root of $p^2c^2+m^2c^4$ are not unique — "one could just as well write $\beta = (a\sigma_1+b\sigma_2)\otimes\sigma_0$ where $a^2+b^2=1$ and get the same solution" [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.59] — Ludwig gauges this freedom, $\beta_g = (U\sigma_1 U^+)\otimes\sigma_0$ with $U={\scriptstyle\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&e^{i\theta}\end{pmatrix}}$, and notes "It is fascinating to note that such a gauge symmetry acts on only one half of the bispinor... that half... corresponds to one particular helicity. This would seem to tie in with the weak interaction, which is also helicity-dependent and *thus related to mass*" [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.60]. Pages 61–69 work through a Weinberg–Salam-style electroweak Lagrangian built directly on this chiral coupling, explicitly "without the Higgs since it isn't physical" [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.62], including the cross-coupling Ludwig calls "very elegant" [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.67] between how $A_\mu$ acts on the isospin doublet and how $W$ acts on the spin doublet. Ludwig's notes are explicit that this whole line of attack is a response to an unresolved question about the Standard Model's own gauge-group choice: "why did God/Nature choose $U(1)\times SU(2)$, and not something else... Our supposition is that there is something important to be learned, probably about the structure of spacetime, in the particulars of $U(1)\times SU(2)$" [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.98 region, "Pages 92–97"] — this question is posed, not answered, in the source, and is **not** resolved by this chapter; the working answer this project gives (that the $SU(2)$ structure is forced by the lattice construction rather than chosen freely) is Chapter 11/12's job, not this one's.

**Forward pointers.** Chapter 11 (mass without a Higgs) derives the complex-mass mechanism and Ludwig's chiral $SU(2)$ construction in full. Chapter 12 (hypercharge and the electroweak sector) derives $U(1)_Y$ on $U(x)$, anomaly cancellation, the $W$/$Z$ spectrum and the Weinberg angle. Chapter 15's weight-as-phase principle (`docs/theory/key-decisions.md`, the 2026-07-16 decision) is a *later*, independently-adopted decision about the lepton condensate's shape angle — it rests on P6 but is not itself part of this chapter's postulate set, since it was adopted long after and on different grounds (representation-theoretic, not gauge-theoretic) from Ludwig's original notes.

### P7 — Methodological: the elegant-design heuristic

> **P7 (methodological, not physical).** Among constructions that are equally consistent with P1–P6 and with observation, the project's working practice is to prefer the structurally simpler, more economical one — on the explicit stance that the universe's fundamental rule is simple and, in principle, completely comprehensible. This is a heuristic for *which* extensions and constructions get pursued and written up; it is not itself a claim about what is physically true, and it carries no predictive content on its own.

**Source.** Stated directly as Core Design Decision 6 of this project's `CLAUDE.md`: "We are operating under the philosophy of elegant design, that the universe can both be completely understood, and is elegant and simple in it's construction." The same aesthetic recurs as an unstated working criterion throughout Ludwig's own notes, applied as praise or complaint rather than argument: a derivation that "seems to single out a single time direction... is not very elegant... It would be much nicer if it were entirely covariant" [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.48] is treated as a defect to be fixed, while a cross-coupling between $A_\mu$'s action on isospin and $W$'s action on spin "looks very elegant" [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.67] is treated as evidence of being on the right track. **No source — not `CLAUDE.md`, not Ludwig's notes, not the cited QCA literature — offers an independent argument for *why* aesthetic elegance should track physical truth.** This is flagged explicitly below.

> **Gap [G-1]:** The elegant-design stance (P7 / CLAUDE.md Core Design Decision 6) is adopted and used throughout the sources as a working criterion for which constructions to pursue, but no source gives an argument for why elegance should be evidentially reliable — it is asserted as a philosophical commitment, not justified from anything more primitive. This is disclosed here rather than papered over; see also `docs/monograph/GAPS.md`.

**Forward pointers.** None specifically — this postulate is invoked implicitly whenever a later chapter chooses among several logically available constructions (e.g., Chapter 8's exclusion of the birefringent $\sigma$-bilinear photon in favor of the paired-spinor photon partly on grounds of the latter being the more natural reading of "the identity channel U(1) forces," Chapter 15's weight-as-phase principle). Each such instance should be traced back here rather than re-justified from scratch.

## 1.3 The beable question, in full

't Hooft's cellular-automaton-interpretation vocabulary gives the sharpest available language for this question, and this project's own comparison against that vocabulary [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §5] is the most direct source available, so this section follows it closely.

't Hooft distinguishes three kinds of operator: **beables** (diagonal in the privileged "ontological basis," commuting with each other at all times, the only operators with a classical "really happening" reading), **changeables** (operators, like the evolution operator itself, that permute ontological basis states), and **superimposables** (operators, like position or spin in a generic basis, that map an ontological state onto a superposition of others) [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §3.1]. The CAI's central claim is that the universe is, at every instant, in *one* basis element of a privileged ontological basis, full stop — wavefunctions ("templates") are unitary changes of basis on this single ontic state, and Born's rule, collapse, and Schrödinger's cat are all consequences of that single assumption [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §2].

This project's model does not adopt that assumption, and the sources are explicit that this is a considered choice, not an oversight: "Our model does not need this — we evolve the *templates themselves* unitarily on the lattice and reproduce QM directly. We do not commit to whether the per-cell amplitude $\psi(\vec x, t)\in\mathbb C^2$ is a template or an ontic state. ... [T]he question 'but what is *really* happening?' is not well-formed below the QCA level — because the QCA is, by construction, the unique informational realisation of the Schrödinger equation in a discrete, local, isotropic, homogeneous, unitary, linear setting" [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §5.4]. Concretely, per the comparison table: the model's "macroscopic readouts (centroid position, energy density...) are diagonal in the per-cell basis by construction. No superposition of dead/alive ever appears in simulation" [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §5.1] — so classical outcomes *are* beables in the operational sense that they never appear superposed at the readout level — but the model does not additionally assert that the *per-cell amplitude itself* is, underneath the amplitude, always secretly one classical value. The per-cell complex spinor $\psi(\mathbf x,t)$ of P5 is therefore this chapter's answer to "what is the beable": **it is the physically real state, taken to genuinely exist in superposition, with no further hidden classical layer postulated beneath it — while remaining explicitly agnostic on whether such a layer could exist.** This agnosticism is a postulate stance in its own right, not a placeholder for an answer supplied elsewhere; it is folded into P4 above rather than given a separate number, because it is a direct qualification of what "unitary update on a Hilbert space" is taken to *mean* physically, not an additional independent assumption.

Two further points from the same comparison sharpen this stance and are worth recording here because later chapters (4, 5) will lean on them:

- **Bell/CHSH.** 't Hooft's CAI reconciles determinism with observed Bell-inequality violation via *superdeterminism* — a constructed correlation between detector settings and the hidden ontic state, engineered so that the observed quantum correlations come out as a marginal [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §3.5]. This project's approach is different in mechanism though not in observed conclusion: Bell violation at the Tsirelson bound is obtained "out of the unitary evolution, *not* out of a constructed correlation... If the CAI is right, our test is sampling from the marginal of $W$. If we are right, the lattice produces Bell violations from local unitary evolution alone (modulo the entangled initial state). Either reading is consistent with the data" [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §5.2]. Superdeterminism is therefore **not** postulated here; nor is it ruled out.
- **Information loss.** 't Hooft's CAI needs non-unitary (information-losing) classical automata at the base layer, with the ontological basis then re-defined as equivalence classes of merged histories, partly to solve a Hamiltonian-positivity problem [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §3.6, §4.2]. This project's automata are, by construction (P4), exactly unitary and reversible in every implemented sector, with a positive Hamiltonian obtained directly rather than via information-loss/equivalence-class machinery [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §5.2, row "Information loss..."]. This is a different strategy for the same underlying technical problem (Hamiltonian positivity, locality, additivity — all three are flagged as open by 't Hooft [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §3.4] and are not claimed solved here either; see §1.7).

## 1.5 Comparison with measurement

Mostly not applicable at the postulate level: postulates are judged by what they collectively predict once combined and carried through many chapters of derivation, not individually against data — there is no experiment that tests "is the universe a cellular automaton" in isolation from a specific automaton's specific predictions. Two exceptions are worth flagging explicitly rather than leaving silent:

- **P1 (discreteness) is, in principle, directly falsifiable**, not merely a philosophical stance: a genuinely discrete spacetime at scale $a$ generically predicts an energy- or frequency-dependent modification of dispersion relations (Lorentz-invariance violation, LIV) that grows with the ratio of a photon's or particle's energy to the scale set by $a$. This is exactly the phenomenology the QCA literature computes explicitly — "(c) Frequency-dependent speed of light... $c(\mathbf k)\approx 1\pm\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}k$... testable via differential arrival times of cosmologically distant gamma-ray bursts" [`qca-papers-1-4-overview.md`, Paper 4] — and it is the mechanism behind the canonical-SI-ruler adoption's own falsification handle: "any $n=2$ time-of-flight bound above $1.36\times10^{19}$ GeV kills the adopted cell" [`docs/theory/key-decisions.md`, Canonical SI ruler entry]. Chapter 7 (relativity on a lattice, LIV coefficients, observational bounds) carries this out in full; it is forward-cited here only to record that P1 is not an untestable metaphysical add-on.
- **P4's ontological reading (no hidden classical layer postulated) makes no distinct observational prediction of its own** relative to 't Hooft's alternative stance, by the sources' own account: both readings of the CHSH/Bell result "are consistent with the data" [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §5.2]. This is recorded honestly rather than claimed as a point in either stance's favor.

All other postulates (P2, P3, P5, P6, P7) have no direct measurement comparison at this level; their consequences are what Chapters 2–20 check against data.

## 1.6 What was excluded, and why

The sources are honest, in most places, about *not* arguing against alternatives at the postulate level — this section reports that honestly rather than inventing exclusion arguments that are not in the record.

- **Continuum field theory as the starting point.** Not explicitly argued against anywhere in the cited sources; it is simply not the thing being modeled, per the project's own stated goal (`CLAUDE.md`'s "universe in a bottle"). The QCA literature's own framing is comparative rather than adversarial: a discrete automaton is shown to *reproduce* continuum free QFT (Weyl, Dirac, Maxwell) in the appropriate limit [`qca-papers-1-4-overview.md`, Paper 1, "Recovery of Weyl, Dirac, Maxwell"], which is offered as a reason to take the discrete starting point seriously, not as a refutation of starting from the continuum instead.
- **A scalar (rather than spinor) primitive per cell.** This *is* explicitly excluded, with a concrete argument, in Ludwig's own notes: the naive discretized scalar wave equation requires two previous time steps ($t-1$ *and* $t-2$-dependence via the second-order equation), which is incompatible with the strict single-previous-tick locality this project wants (P2); "to get it to depend only on the previous time step we have to make the Dirac reduction and make each cell spinor-valued" [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.37]. This is a genuine, sourced exclusion argument, not an invented one, and it independently corroborates the QCA-literature's minimality theorem for P5.
- **A generic classical automaton (integer/bit states per cell, à la 't Hooft's $Q(\vec x,t)\in\mathbb Z_N$ [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §3.3]).** Not explicitly excluded by argument in any source; the project simply commits to the complex-Hilbert-space / unitary route (P4) rather than to 't Hooft's classical-automaton-plus-reinterpretation route, for the historical reason that this is the branch of the literature (Bisio/D'Ariano/Raynal) the project's own architecture descends from, not because a case against 't Hooft's classical route is made anywhere in the sources.
- **A fundamental Higgs scalar for mass generation.** Excluded by explicit project decision (P6, CLAUDE.md decisions 1/3), on the stated grounds that Ludwig's chiral $SU(2)$ construction reproduces the phenomenology "without the Higgs since it isn't physical" [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.62] — a stated preference in the source, not a proof of the Higgs mechanism's falsity. The Standard Model's Higgs mechanism is not claimed to be wrong; it is claimed to be *unnecessary* given P5/P6, which is a different and weaker claim, and this distinction is preserved rather than overstated.
- **A specific alternative gauge group for the electroweak sector** (e.g. $SU(2)\times SU(2)$, per the notes' own comparison to Sachs' unification program [`physics-notes-complete.md`, p.9], or $U(1)\times SO(6,2)$ [`physics-notes-complete.md`, "Pages 92–97"]). Explicitly posed as an open question by the source itself, not resolved by exclusion argument: "why did God/Nature choose $U(1)\times SU(2)$, and not something else... Our supposition is that there is something important to be learned... in the particulars of $U(1)\times SU(2)$" [`physics-notes-complete.md`, "Pages 92–97"]. This chapter reports the question as open rather than manufacturing an answer; Chapters 11–12 are where this project's actual working answer (that the group structure is forced by the lattice/spinor construction, not freely chosen) is to be found.

## 1.7 What is still open

Recorded honestly rather than papered over, because the sources themselves are honest about each of these:

1. **Whether a deeper deterministic substrate exists beneath the per-cell amplitude of P4/P5** is genuinely unsettled in the sources — not resolved either way, and not merely "not yet derived." The project's own comparison document states plainly: "We do not commit to whether the per-cell amplitude $\psi(\vec x,t)\in\mathbb C^2$ is a template or an ontic state" [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §5.4]. This is the single most consequential open item in this chapter, since it determines whether the entire monograph is, at bottom, a hidden-variable theory (à la 't Hooft) or a directly-quantum discrete theory with no hidden layer — and the honest answer, per the sources, is that this has not been decided.
2. **Hamiltonian positivity, locality, and additivity for the full coupled multi-sector stack** are flagged by 't Hooft as open problems for *any* CA-based theory of this kind [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §3.4], and the project's own comparison concedes the same gap for its combined Weyl+Dirac+Higgs-replacement+gravity stack: "the additivity... of those Hamiltonians is unverified for the *full* coupled... stack" [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §6, item 7]. Each sector is positive and unitary on its own by construction; the combined object has not been checked.
3. **Why $U(1)\times SU(2)$ (rather than some other gauge group) is Nature's choice** is posed as an open question in Ludwig's own notes and not answered there [`physics-notes-complete.md`, "Pages 92–97"]; whether Chapters 11–12's lattice-forced construction actually answers it, or merely relocates the same question to "why this lattice," is left for those chapters to state plainly rather than assumed resolved here.
4. **Lorentz invariance** is broken by construction at the postulate level (a rigid discrete lattice, P1–P3, picks out a preferred rest frame) and is only asymptotically/approximately recovered; deformed (DSR-style) approaches are proposed in the literature but "whether it survives more rigorous scrutiny is an open question" even by the QCA literature's own account [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §5.2, row "Lorentz invariance"]. Chapter 7 addresses the current state of this in detail; it is not resolved at the postulate level and should not be read as resolved here.
5. **Whether the elegant-design heuristic (P7) is doing any real epistemic work**, as opposed to functioning as post-hoc narrative around choices made for other reasons, is not addressed anywhere in the sources (see Gap G-1) and is left open rather than defended.

## 1.8 Falsifiers

What would falsify a *postulate itself*, as distinct from a downstream derived result that merely fails a numerical comparison:

- **P1 (discreteness) fails** if a lattice-scale-dependent effect this monograph commits to (frequency-dependent light speed / LIV at the adopted cell size, Chapter 7; the canonical-SI-ruler falsification handle of `docs/theory/key-decisions.md`) is excluded by observation at the specific scale this project adopts. This does not falsify "the universe might be discrete somewhere," only "the universe is discrete at *this* scale with *this* lattice" — a falsifier of the adopted realization, not of discreteness as an abstract ontological category.
- **P2/P3 (locality, homogeneity, isotropy) fail** if the specific lattice Chapter 2 derives from them turns out not to reproduce the continuum Weyl/Dirac/Maxwell limit at all — i.e., if the uniqueness theorems this chapter forward-cites (Papers 1/2) turn out not to apply to the actual construction used from Chapter 2 onward. This is a consistency-of-construction failure, checkable in principle by direct calculation, not an experimental falsifier.
- **P4 (unitarity, and the "no hidden classical layer" reading) is not, by the sources' own admission, distinguishable from the alternative (a 't Hooft-style hidden deterministic layer) by any experiment performed to date** [`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §5.2] — so there is currently no falsifier that would discriminate between this chapter's ontological stance and 't Hooft's. What *would* falsify unitarity itself (as opposed to the ontological reading of it) is any confirmed violation of exact probability conservation or time-reversibility at the fundamental scale — not currently within reach of any proposed test in these sources.
- **P5 (spinor primitive) is falsified in construction**, not by experiment, if a rigorous re-derivation of the QCA uniqueness theorem under this project's actual axioms yields $s\neq2$ or admits a non-spinor alternative of equal or lower dimension — this would undercut the claimed convergence between Ludwig's physical argument and the QCA-literature theorem.
- **P6 (chiral $SU(2)$, no Higgs) is falsified observationally** if a fundamental scalar with the specific quantum numbers and self-interaction structure of the Standard Model Higgs boson is required by data in a way this project's chiral-$SU(2)$ mass mechanism cannot reproduce — the relevant comparisons are carried out in Chapters 11–12, not here.
- **P7 (elegant design) has no falsifier** — by its own nature as a stated methodological preference rather than a physical claim, it cannot be checked against data, only judged (subjectively) by whether it continues to lead to constructions that survive contact with P1–P6 and with measurement.
