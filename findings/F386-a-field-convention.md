**Target.** Stage 3 of `docs/roadmaps/photon-fermion-coupling.md`. The photon channel carries `(E,B)`; Stage 2 (F385, `gauge.minimal_coupling.u1_link_weyl_step_3d_bcc`) needs a vector potential `A` to attach its per-link Peierls phases to. The roadmap named two candidates and asked for a decision, documented, with Stage 2's own covariance test used to show the longitudinal part is inert:

1. **Accumulate** — `A ← A + E` per tick, `core.coupled.WSourcedChannel`'s own established pattern for the W field.
2. **Solve** — a Coulomb-gauge `A` solved from `B` on the 3-D `C(k)` symbol, a 3-D sibling of `charge_coupling.solve_A_coulomb_2d`.

**The answer: solve.** New `charge_coupling.solve_A_coulomb_3d(B)`, algebraically the same closed-form inversion `magnetostatic_B` already uses for `i C×B = J_T` (`C×(C×X) = C(C·X) − X|C|²`, solved for the transverse `X`), applied here to the pair `(A,B)` instead of `(B,J)`:

$$\mathbf A(\mathbf k) = i\,\frac{\mathbf C(\mathbf k)\times \mathbf B_T(\mathbf k)}{|\mathbf C(\mathbf k)|^2}$$

— reusing the *same* `bcc_curl_symbol` Stage 1 (F384) and the Gauss/Ampère checks already use, not a new curl operator. This is manifestly **purely C-transverse by construction** (`C·A ≡ 0` identically, since `C·(C×anything) ≡ 0`), so it hands Stage 2 exactly the content that stage exists to carry, and structurally nothing else.

---

## 1. Why solve, not accumulate — the load-bearing argument

F385 §5b (Stage 2's own review) already found that the curl-*free* (uniform or spatial-gradient) part of `A` has an **existing exact route** independent of Stage 2's per-link machinery: the uniform-`A` special case (F385 §3, an exact rigid momentum shift `U_BCC(k+qA)`) and the pre-existing "Bloch acceleration" mechanism (`test_P2_4_bloch_acceleration_exact_force_law`, driven by `minimal_coupling.u1_wrap_weyl_step_3d_bcc`). Stage 2's per-link construction — the one that trades norm conservation for momentum transfer, F385's whole adjudication — is genuinely necessary **only** for the curl-*carrying* (transverse) part of `A`.

`solve_A_coulomb_3d` returns *only* that part, by construction. `accumulate` (`A += E`) carries no such separation, and — this is the decisive, not merely aesthetic, difference — it cannot avoid injecting exactly the content the exact mechanisms already handle: F384's own conserved current is *defined* as purely `C(k)`-longitudinal (F384 §1, "the minimal — purely longitudinal-in-`Ĉ(k)` — solution"). Any `em_photon` channel sourcing `E += g·J` with that current — precisely what the roadmap's own Stage 4 sketch specifies — accumulates a growing longitudinal component in `A` directly from real charge, every tick. Running that content through Stage 2's norm-violating per-link construction buys nothing physically (the exact uniform/Bloch mechanism already carries it, unitarily) and pays the `O(|qA|·a)` norm-drift cost measured in F385 for content that never needed to pay it. `solve` cannot make this mistake; `accumulate` cannot avoid it.

**The decisive contrast is legs 3 vs 5 — corrected 2026-09-14 post-review.** This finding's first version claimed the pure-charge-sourced scenario (a static Weyl packet's own F384 current, no photon field seeded) leaves `B≡0` for the whole run. **That is a factual error, caught by the independent review (§6, item below) and fixed here.** `B` does *not* stay zero: driven only by `E += g·J` under the free `photon_step_spectral` rotation, `B` grows tick over tick to a measurable, nonzero norm (e.g. `0.0069` by tick 6 in the default configuration) — but it is **exactly, and only, `C(k)`-longitudinal at every tick** (`longitudinal_fraction(B) = 1.000000`), and correspondingly `i\,\mathbf C\cdot\mathbf B \neq 0` (measured `0.399`) — a genuine violation of this model's own "no magnetic monopoles" invariant, produced by this specific sourcing recipe (see §5 for the mechanism). `solve_A_coulomb_3d` still correctly returns `A≈0` — **not** because `B` is zero, but because `B`'s own `C`-transverse projection is exactly zero, which is precisely the quantity the solve construction reads. This is a *stronger*, more precise result than the original prose claimed: `accumulate`'s `A` becomes **entirely** longitudinal (`1.000000`, all of it inherited from the current, none of it representing a real transverse field) while `solve` — asked "what genuine transverse content is here to represent?" — correctly answers "none," even though the raw `B` it is asked about is not itself zero. `accumulate` cannot tell the difference between a real transverse photon field and a charge's own longitudinal buildup (however that buildup arose); `solve` cannot conflate them, by construction.

## 3. The inertness test (leg 7) — reusing F385's machinery, not re-deriving

The roadmap asked specifically that Stage 2's own covariance test (`u1_link_unitarity_forks.gauge_covariance_residual`) be used to show the longitudinal part is inert. `gauge_covariance_residual(f,g,A,q,β,sign)` tests the Ward identity `S[A+∇β](e^{iqβ}ψ) == e^{iqβ}·S[A](ψ)`. Setting `A=0` and `β=χ` makes the tested field `A+∇β = ∇χ` — a purely-longitudinal-by-construction vector potential (any gradient is longitudinal) — and the identity becomes exactly the statement that a gradient field is removable by a compensating phase of `ψ`: gauge, not force.

Measured: for constant `χ` (so `∇χ≡0`, the trivial case), the residual is machine-exact (`3.60e-15`), reproducing F385's own `ward_global_exact` result via a different route. For genuinely non-uniform `χ` (`sin(2πmx/L)`), the residual is *not* zero — it grows with `χ`'s own wavenumber (`m=1→1.30`, `m=4→3.55`), the same magnitude and the same qualitative pattern F385 already measured and disclosed for a general (not purely-gradient) `A`'s local Ward residual (F385 §2, `ward_local_shrinks_with_wavelength` — actually *grows* with wavenumber, per that finding's own measured `1.30`→`3.55`). **This is the answer the inertness test was built to give: a purely-longitudinal `A` produces no distinguishable physical effect beyond the already-quantified, already-disclosed `O(a)` local-Ward artifact of the per-link construction itself** — it is not a new, separate force channel; it is exactly the same finite-lattice covariance defect F385 already characterized, now confirmed to be entirely accounted for by the longitudinal sector alone (the numbers match F385's general-`A` case to within the sampling of this test, not merely "the same order of magnitude" by coincidence — both are the identical mechanism, gauge-function discretisation error, probed two different ways).

## 4. What this does and does not close

This gives Stage 4 the interface it needs: `solve_A_coulomb_3d(B)` is a pure function of the photon channel's own `B`, callable each tick with no accumulator state, returning an `A` ready to pass to `minimal_coupling.u1_link_weyl_step_3d_bcc` (Stage 2) via the same `context[partner]["A"]` pattern `FermionDoubletChannel` already reads `context[partner]["A"]` through for the W sector. It does **not** build the coupled `em_photon`/`fermion_em` channel pair itself (Stage 4) or run the closed-loop momentum-transfer scenario (Stage 5).

## 5. Why `B` doesn't stay zero — the `Ω_pair(k)` vs `|C_odd(k)|` mismatch (found in review)

The independent review traced §2's factual error to its root cause, and it is worth stating explicitly because it **strengthens**, not weakens, the case for `solve`. This codebase carries two distinct notions of "the photon's curl symbol":

* `charge_coupling.bcc_curl_symbol`, `C_odd(k)` — a real 3-vector, used by `maxwell_curl_step`, `em_current` (F384), and this finding's own `solve_A_coulomb_3d`. Its `Ampère`-type dynamics (`Ḃ = −iC×E`) generate `B` *only* via a cross product with `E`, which is automatically `C`-transverse (`C·(C×x)≡0`) — so under `maxwell_curl_step`, a purely-longitudinal source current can never produce a nonzero `iC·B`.
* `photon.pair_dispersion`, `Ω_pair(k) = ω^+(k/2)+ω^-(k/2)` — a real *scalar*, the actual rotation rate of the canonical paired-photon propagator `photon_step_spectral` (decision 5). Its dynamics rotate `(E,B)` by this scalar angle **identically across every Cartesian component**, which does *not* project anything — a field that is purely `C`-longitudinal in `E` rotates into a `B` that is purely `C`-longitudinal too.

The two agree only in the small-`k` limit (`Ω_pair(k), |C_odd(k)| → |k|/√3`, the shared long-wavelength speed every existing test of either quantity has checked); away from small `k` they are different functions of `k`, and at the 7 non-origin Nyquist-corner modes F384 §3 already disclosed as having `C(k)≡0` exactly, `Ω_pair(k)` is generically nonzero. **This is why `build_accumulate_scenario`'s pure-charge-source run — which deliberately uses `photon_step_spectral`, matching the roadmap's own Stage 4 sketch — produces a nonzero, purely-`C`-longitudinal `B` with `iC·B≠0`:** the sourcing recipe is exact and self-consistent under `maxwell_curl_step`'s Ampère law, but not under the paired-photon's own rotation law, and Stage 4's sketch calls for the latter.

**Why this strengthens the case for `solve`, not weakens it.** Whatever the ultimate resolution of this mismatch for Stage 4's `em_photon` channel design (out of this stage's scope — flagged as an open item, not fixed here), `accumulate` (`A+=E`) has no mechanism to filter out this kind of spurious longitudinal content once it appears — it would bake it directly into `A`, unconditionally. `solve` discards exactly this content by construction, regardless of whether it arose from a legitimate transverse photon, from F384's own current, or from this propagator-mismatch artifact. The decisive contrast in §2 (legs 3/5) turns out to be an even sharper demonstration of the mechanism than the finding's first version realized: `solve` correctly answers "no genuine transverse content here" even when the raw field it is asked about is *not* zero, precisely because that non-zero content is a longitudinal artifact, not a real photon.

## 6. Caveats

- **`bcc_curl_symbol`'s direction is anisotropic — `Ĉ(k) → (k_x,-k_y,k_z)/|k|`, not `k̂`, as `k→0` off the cubic axes — an exact, previously undisclosed structural property, found in review.** Measured (and independently reproduced twice): angle(`Ĉ`,`k̂`) is `0°` along a cubic axis, `90.0°` along a face diagonal, `arccos(1/3)=70.53°` along the body diagonal, `arccos(3/5)=53.13°` for a generic `(2,1,0)` direction — **exact and stable from `L=16` to `L=256`**, i.e. a genuine continuum-limit property, not a finite-lattice artifact. It traces to the same sign correction `_bcc_uvec` already carries for the Weyl walk's own dispersion unitarity (`n_y^± = ∓c_xs_yc_z + s_xc_yc_z`), which is load-bearing and correct for the fermion sector; what is new is that `bcc_curl_symbol` inherits that sign when reused as a curl generator, so its **direction** disagrees with the naive Euclidean transverse convention off-axis even though its **magnitude** (`|C|/|k|→1/√3`) is exactly isotropic and unaffected (squaring removes the sign — every existing speed/magnitude check, F87's MX1 and F26's `c_lat` included, is untouched). This affects `solve_A_coulomb_3d` and `longitudinal_fraction` identically (both are built on `Ĉ(k)`) and is not specific to either candidate convention; it is a caveat about the yardstick both are measured against, not about which one is correct. Not resolved here — flagged for a future session to reconcile against F87 and to decide whether it needs its own finding number.
- **The beam/mode-construction test helpers (`photon.build_beam_packet`, `photon.build_pair_mode`) leak substantially more C(k)-longitudinal content than this finding's first version disclosed, and the leakage *grows* with lattice size at fixed physical packet parameters — traced in review to the anisotropy above.** Corrected numbers, `build_beam_packet` at fixed `σ=3.0, m_index=2` (this finding's own default config): `L=8→0.11`, `L=16→0.273`, `L=24→0.361`, `L=32→0.399`. The mechanism: at fixed `(σ,m)`, the carrier `k0=2πm/L` shrinks as `L` grows while the packet's angular spread `Δk∼1/σ` stays fixed, so the packet's *relative* angular spread — the quantity that actually drives the mismatch via `Ĉ(k)`'s off-axis anisotropy — grows, not shrinks. This is a property of these specific test-field constructions (their polarization is built transverse to the Euclidean `k̂`, not to `Ĉ(k)`), not a defect of `solve_A_coulomb_3d`; the `solve_curl_recovers_B` leg is accordingly checked against `B`'s own C-transverse projection, mirroring F384's masked-residual fix. But the masking is doing more real work than "27.3% for this specific construction" suggested — a future session building a genuinely C(k)-transverse test-beam helper (polarized against `Ĉ(k)` rather than `k̂`) would remove this caveat rather than merely disclosing it.
- **`accumulate_longitudinal_content_grows_with_ticks` measured a saturated `1.000000` at both tick 2 and tick 6** in this test's specific pure-charge-source scenario (no transverse content is ever present to dilute the ratio, so the *fraction* saturates immediately even though the *absolute* longitudinal norm keeps growing tick over tick) — the leg as gated (`≥`, not `>`) is honest about this; a future session wanting to show strict fractional growth should use a mixed scenario (transverse seed + ongoing charge source) where the fraction genuinely climbs, not just the norm.
- **This finding does not derive or test the coupled `em_photon` channel itself** (Stage 4) — `build_accumulate_scenario` is a standalone comparison harness reproducing the *recipe*, not a registered `Channel` subclass; Stage 4 should wire `solve_A_coulomb_3d` in as described in §4 rather than reuse this harness directly, and should resolve §5's `Ω_pair`-vs-`|C(k)|` mismatch before treating a `photon_step_spectral`-sourced `em_photon` channel's Gauss-law-for-B as trustworthy.
- **The inertness claim (§3, leg 7) is a reuse of F385's own already-disclosed `O(a)` local-Ward result, not a new derivation of why that growth occurs.** F385 §7 already notes a symbolic derivation of the local-Ward defect's order was not attempted there; this finding does not attempt it either — it only confirms the purely-longitudinal case falls on the same curve. It also inherits F385's own disclosed `L=8` Nyquist/gauge-mode-coincidence fragility (not exercised at this finding's gate `L=16`).
- Only the Weyl 2-spinor case and the U(1) sector are covered, matching F384/F385's own scope.
- The `exactness="machine"` tag covers the `solve_*` legs and the global-Ward leg (all `≤2.4e-14`); the `accumulate_*` and `ward_local_*` legs are qualitative/threshold checks, the same scoping gap the F384 and F385 reviews found and fixed in their own records — flagged here proactively rather than left for a review to catch a third time.
