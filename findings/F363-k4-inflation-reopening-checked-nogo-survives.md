Three of those consumers are recent enough to be worth naming explicitly because each is an independent live check that the constant still behaves as F282/F283 assumed: F357/CL297 (graviton/photon band-top energy, $E_\text{max}/M_\text{Pl}=\sqrt{\pi\sqrt3/8}$), F359/CL298 (graviton-pair collapse threshold, $\sqrt{s_\text{max}}/M_\text{rem}=\sqrt\pi$ exactly, $A^2=8\pi\sqrt3$ cancelling completely), and F362/CL300 (block-spin eigenvalue, unrelated sector but same registry). ## 2. Second step — is "compact CA direction" still the only inflaton candidate space?

F282 §3's compactness argument is deliberately **content-independent**: it does not derive a bound for the $E_g$ clock or the $E_g$ radial mode specifically and then hope it generalises — it derives $r=M_\text{Pl}^2/f^2\ge\sqrt3/J$ for *any* field whose per-cell state space is bounded, with $J=O(1)$ read off the model's entire computed coupling range ($[0.16,2.4]$, F118). That is why finding a *new* compact candidate cannot, by itself, reopen K4: any new compact direction inherits the same bound unless its own $J$ happens to be three orders of magnitude larger than every coupling the model has ever computed (F282's own falsifier 1).

A sweep of every finding filed since F282 (F283–F362) for a new scalar, angular, or order-parameter degree of freedom turns up three candidates, none of which changes the picture:

- **The T$_{2g}$ lepton-mixing channel** (F236, pre-dating F282 but not previously checked against it). This is a genuinely *free* second-shell channel — but it is a fixed low-energy texture parameter fit to PMNS data (a constant tensor of couplings), not a dynamical field with a time-dependent expectation value; nothing in F236 or its followers (F254, F294, F353) gives it a potential or a kinetic term to roll on. Not a candidate.
- **The block-spin eigenvalue $\gamma$** (F310, F362). This is an RG *exponent* attached to the confinement operator, not a field — it is a number characterising how an existing operator scales under coarse-graining, and F282 §7 already used the same block-spin machinery (F130) to show the operator spectrum has an $O(1)$ gap around marginality. F362's negative result (the eigenvalue stays exactly integer under fluctuation corrections) is, if anything, corroborating: it is one more confirmation that this model's RG spectrum does not produce a nearly-marginal scalar. Not a candidate, and not a reopening.
- **Torsion** (F63, pre-dating F282, checked here for the first time against it). Discussed on its own in §3 below because it is the one candidate worth a real check rather than a one-line dismissal.

**No new compact-direction candidate with anomalously large $J$ exists in the post-F282 ledger.**

## 3. Two escape-route classes from the literature, checked and closed

F282 §6 closed three named escape routes (Starobinsky $R^2$, N-flation, periodic-$n_s$ direction) — this session does not re-attack any of them, per the prompt's explicit instruction. The literature search (external search terms from the prompt, plus a standard-cosmology survey of "how do large-field models get around a sub-Planckian decay constant") turns up two *mechanism classes* that are not the same as any of F282's three and that specifically exist to get around exactly the obstruction F282 proves: a compact field with $f\lesssim\Lambda_\text{UV}$.

### 3a. Axion monodromy — closed (needs field content the model does not have)

Axion monodromy (Silverstein–Westphal 2008 and the McAllister–Silverstein–Westphal line that followed) evades a sub-Planckian decay constant *without* enlarging $f$ or adding fields: it makes the potential **non-periodic**. A flux or brane wrapping the compact direction adds a term (typically $\propto|\delta+2\pi n|$ or a power law in the winding) that grows secularly as the field winds through many periods, so the *effective* field range is large even though the fundamental period $2\pi f$ stays sub-Planckian. This is a genuinely different mechanism from F282 §6's "periodic direction" exclusion, because that exclusion's bound $n_s\le1-p^2r$ is derived *from* the assumption $V\propto\cos(p\delta)$ — a monodromy potential is not of that form by construction, so the bound does not apply to it mechanically.

**Why it does not reopen K4 here.** Monodromy requires an explicit symmetry-breaking coupling external to the compact field's own potential — a background flux, a wrapped brane charge, a discrete gauge field threading the cycle. The model's $E_g$ clock potential $V(\delta)=\lambda_6e^6\cos^23\delta$ is *fully derived* from the $O_h$ point-group symmetry and the F175/F234 weight-quantization chain — there is no flux, brane, or extra discrete charge anywhere in the CA construction that could source a term breaking that symmetry. Positing one would not be finding a candidate the model already contains; it would be adding new, undetermined physics with no derivation behind it, which is precisely what CLAUDE.md's Practices section rules out ("always attempt to derive new elements... before introducing new physics") and what the model's zero-free-parameter ethos (Core Design Decision 6) is built to avoid.

**This has to be checked against, not just asserted around, the one genuine discrete structure the ledger already derives on this shell.** [[F253-weight-as-phase-scale-nogo]] computes the BCC 2nd shell's $E_g$ holonomy directly (its check W5) and finds exactly one: the body-diagonal $C_3$ rotation induces $2\pi/3$ in the $E_g$ plane, "the origin of the $\cos3\delta$ invariant" (F253 §3) — and nothing else; F253's own quantized-phase sweep confirms no other rational holonomy exists on this shell. That is precisely the *wrong* kind of discrete structure for a monodromy escape: a $2\pi/3$ holonomy is what **sets the potential's period** (it is why $V(\delta)\propto\cos3\delta$ is 3-fold periodic in the first place, F175/F234), not an unwinding charge that could make the field's *effective* range secularly exceed one period. Monodromy needs the opposite kind of object — something that grows *with* winding number rather than being generated *by* a finite rotation group element — and $O_h$'s holonomy group is finite by construction, so there is no element of it that could play that role. **So this is not merely "no flux or brane exists" by omission; the one candidate discrete structure the model does contain has been checked directly and is the wrong shape for the job.**

**Considered and closed for the same reason F282 §11 gives for treating $J=O(1)$ as safe: nothing in the model supplies the needed ingredient, and adding it by hand would be a different model.**

### 3b. Torsion/kinetic-enhancement ("twisting") and DBI/k-inflation — closed, but for a more specific reason than 3a

A 2025 mechanism (arXiv:2507.10696) achieves large-field inflation from a sub-Planckian axion a third way: not by breaking periodicity, but by coupling the axion to the Einstein–Cartan **Nieh–Yan** torsion invariant, which remaps the *kinetic* term ($f^2\to f^2(1+6M^4/M_\text{Pl}^2f^2)$) while leaving the cosine potential exactly periodic. This is a genuinely third class again — it evades F282's bound by making the canonical-normalization step $\phi=f\chi$ (F282 §3) give a much larger effective $f$ than the naive $\sqrt J/a$ estimate, without touching $J$ or the potential at all. DBI/k-inflation (Silverstein–Tong 2004) achieves a related effect by a different route — a non-canonical kinetic Lagrangian $P(\phi,X)$ with a small effective sound speed, so the *potential* slow-roll parameters can be $O(1)$ while the Hubble slow-roll parameters stay small.

**Why the torsion route does not reopen K4 here.** It needs a **dynamical, propagating** torsion field carrying a topological coupling to the compact direction's kinetic term. This model already has a torsion sector — [[F63-spin-torsion-magnitude-estimate]] — and it is the wrong kind: F63's torsion is algebraically fixed by fermion spin density (the Einstein–Cartan connection equation eliminates it in favour of a four-fermion contact term, per the project's deliberate torsion-free construction, CLAUDE.md Core Design Decisions and F50/F52/F62), not an independent propagating field, and it carries no coupling whatsoever to the $E_g$ doublet or its clock angle. Recovering the kind of Nieh–Yan pseudoscalar–torsion coupling the mechanism needs would mean reversing the project's own torsion-free decision and adding a coupling F63 shows the model has no reason to contain. Same verdict as 3a: closed because it requires new undetermined physics, not because the mechanism is wrong in general.

**Why the DBI/k-inflation route is not cleanly closed the same way — see §4.**

### 3c. "Inflation without an Inflaton" (Bhattacharya et al. 2024/2025-2026 series) — checked, does not apply

A third, more recent literature line answers K4's own framing ("inflation or a declared substitute") more literally than 3a/3b: Bhattacharya, Kumar Kim, Kumar Ramkumar, et al., *Inflation without an Inflaton*, Phys. Rev. Research **7**, 032010 (2025), arXiv:2412.14265, with sequels on observational predictions (arXiv:2511.11808) and non-Gaussian signatures (arXiv:2603.15036) carrying it into 2026. It generates the primordial **scalar** curvature spectrum from second-order effects of **tensor** (graviton) vacuum fluctuations in a de Sitter background, with no scalar inflaton field sourcing it at all.

**Why it does not reopen K4 here.** It is a mechanism for generating the *scalar* spectrum given that a quasi-de-Sitter, $\sim55$-e-fold expanding phase is already underway — it does not explain why such a phase exists or is sustained; the de Sitter background is an input to the calculation, not an output of it. F282's actual content is precisely the question this mechanism does not touch: no field in this model can sustain $\dot H\approx0$ for $\sim55$ e-folds (every candidate fails slow roll by $O(10)$), and F284 independently shows the rigid substrate offers no alternative process that could. A mechanism for turning an already-sustained near-de-Sitter phase into a scalar spectrum is not a substitute for having such a phase in the first place, so it does not bear on K4 as this model would need it to. Recorded because a K4 literature search should surface and close it explicitly, not because it comes close to working.

## 4. The one open thread: is the compact directions' kinetic term actually canonical?

F282 §3 writes every candidate field as $\phi=f\chi$ with $\chi$ a bounded lattice variable and $f=\sqrt J/a$ — i.e. it assumes a **canonical** (quadratic, field-independent-normalization) kinetic term for $\delta$ and $e$. This is stated in the module's own docstring (`cosmology_primordial.py`, "every candidate's canonical decay constant is $f=\sqrt J/a$") as a working assumption, not something independently derived from a second-shell kinetic Lagrangian for the $E_g$ doublet. Nowhere in [[F118-self-consistent-Wvc-and-C-Eg-self-interaction]] (which derives the *potential*, $V(e,\delta)$) or elsewhere in the ledger is the kinetic term for $\delta$ or $e$ derived from the underlying lattice action and checked for non-canonical (field-dependent-metric or higher-derivative) corrections.

This matters because it is exactly the gap the DBI/k-inflation class (§3b) would need filled in its favour, and unlike axion monodromy or the torsion-kinetic route, it does **not** obviously require importing new field content — a non-canonical kinetic term for a compact order parameter, if one is present, would come from the model's *own* already-derived second-shell dynamics (higher-order terms in whatever expansion gives the F118 hat potential), not from a new coupling grafted on. This session did not attempt that derivation — it is a genuine second-shell kinetic-term calculation, not a literature check, and is out of scope for a re-examination pass. It is named here as the sharpest concrete thread a future attempt should pull, sharper than F282's own falsifier 1 ($J\ge780$) or falsifier 2 (a non-compact direction), because it does not require the model to contain anything it does not already have — only for an existing derivation (the $E_g$ kinetic term) to have a feature nobody has checked for.

**This is explicitly not a reopening.** No non-canonical term has been found; the default (canonical, per the module's own stated assumption) is what F282's exclusion already uses, and there is no positive indication in the ledger that the second-shell kinetic term is anything but canonical. It is flagged as an unexamined assumption, which is a different and much weaker thing than an unexamined candidate.

## 6. Falsifiers (additions to F282 §9, not replacements)

1. **(unchanged from F282)** A scalar direction with $J\ge780$.
2. **(unchanged from F282)** A non-compact field direction.
3. **(new, this finding)** A derivation of the $E_g$ doublet's second-shell kinetic term from the lattice action that turns up a non-canonical (field-dependent-normalization or higher-derivative) structure large enough to push the effective $f$ above $\sim20\,M_\text{Pl}$ without changing $J$ or the potential. This is the one route in §3–4 not closed by "the model doesn't contain the ingredient" — it would use only content the model already has, checked for a feature nobody has checked for yet.
4. **A discovered discrete flux, brane-like charge, or dynamical torsion coupling** anywhere in the CA construction that the project has not already classified as torsion-free/absent (F63, F50/F52/F62) or as the finite, period-setting $2\pi/3$ holonomy F253 already found and ruled the wrong shape for monodromy (§3a) — would reopen monodromy or torsion-kinetic-enhancement respectively. Given the project's explicit torsion-free design decision and F253's own exhaustive holonomy sweep, this would itself be a significant, independently-motivated finding before it could be used here.
