# F265 — The gauge sectors were BCC in the propagator and cubic in the action; the composite plaquette is blind to 1/3 of the curvature

**Date:** 2026-07-29 - 14:20
**Status:** established (12/12 checks). Supersedes the composite-SC field strength in `ca_wmu.plaquette_field_strength` and `ca_gluon.plaquette_field_strength_su3_bcc`.
**Test:** `tests/findings/test_F265_bcc_gauge_geometry.py` → `test-results/F265_bcc_gauge_geometry.json`
**Modules:** `ca-simulation/ca_bcc_gauge.py` (new), `ca-simulation/ca_lattice.py` (BCC geometry section)

## 1. The audit claim, corrected

The audit reported "the gauge channels are cubic only and matter is BCC only." The
split is real but it does not run between gauge and matter — it runs between
**propagator and action**, inside each gauge sector.

Every gauge *propagator* was already BCC: γ (`ca_photon_pair`), W
(`ca_wmu._f26_rotation_step`), Z (`ca_z_field`) and the gluon
(`gluon_rotation_step_spectral_bcc`) all evaluate
$\omega^\pm$ from `ca_bcc.bcc_dispersion(k/2,\pm)$. F91/F166/F250 rest on this and
are untouched.

Every gauge *action* was simple-cubic. Field strength was built by first
collapsing the eight BCC links into three straight $\pm x,\pm y,\pm z$ composites
of length 2 — `ca_wmu.py` said so in a comment, `# Build 6 effective SC links
from BCC pairs` — and then taking ordinary square plaquettes in the three
coordinate planes with `a2 = 4.0`. `ca_gluon._bcc_eff_links_su3` mirrored it.

Four further cubic layers were found and are **not** closed by this finding
(§7): the 4D hypercubic Wilson LPT chain (`ca_lpt_*`), the BCC-propagator-on-SC-BZ
fold in `ca_bgfield_loop`, the Monte-Carlo actions (F94 4D cubic, F146 3D cubic),
and `ca_hypercharge`, which is 2D despite its docstring.

## 2. The lattice, fixed by convention

Take the conventional cubic cell at side 2. Then the nearest-neighbour hop *is*
the integer vector $d\in\{\pm1\}^3$ and `np.roll` by $d$ is an exact lattice
translation — no fractional shift needed on the link side.

| | result | status |
|---|---|---|
| Lattice generated by the 8 hops | $\{n\in\mathbb Z^3 : n_x\equiv n_y\equiv n_z \pmod 2\}$ | exact (brute-force set identity) |
| Index in $\mathbb Z^3$ | 4 | exact ($\lvert\det\rvert$ of a basis) |
| Fraction of a cubic array that is lattice sites | **1/4** | exact, all $L$ |
| Reciprocal lattice | **fcc**, generated by $\pi(1,1,0),\pi(1,0,1),\pi(0,1,1)$ | exact |
| $V_\text{BZ}$ | $2\pi^3=(2\pi)^3/4$ | exact |
| Copies of the BZ in the cube $[-\pi,\pi)^3$ | **4** | exact |
| `bcc_bz_mask` count | $N/4$ for $L=4,6,8,12,16$ | exact |
| fcc shifts are periods of the integer-hop walk | $1.1\times10^{-15}$ | machine precision |

The BZ result reproduces **F264 row 326** from an independent starting point (the
hop set rather than the Weyl-point census), which is why F264's "8 doublers per
branch" turned out to be a fourfold cube-census artefact.

Consequence for the code: only 1/4 of a cubic array's entries are lattice sites,
and **any BZ integral for the BCC walk must either restrict to `bcc_bz_mask` or
divide by 4.** Integrating over the full cube and calling it one BZ over-counts by
exactly 4.

## 3. The minimal loop is a 4-bond rhombus, and there are 6 of them

Three vectors with all-odd components cannot sum to zero, so **there are no
3-bond closed loops** (verified: 0 of $8^3$). The minimal gauge loop is therefore
a 4-bond rhombus with sides $(d_1,d_2)$, $d_2\neq\pm d_1$ — path
$x\to x{+}d_1\to x{+}d_1{+}d_2\to x{+}d_2\to x$.

| | result |
|---|---|
| Non-antipodal side pairs at a site | 24 |
| Orientations modulo translation | **6** (= 24/4 corner relabellings) |
| Area $\lvert d_1\times d_2\rvert$ | $2\sqrt2$, the same for all |
| Normal direction | $\langle110\rangle$ — a face-diagonal axis, for all |
| Distinct normal axes | 6 |
| $O_h$ orbit | single orbit of size 6 |

So the BCC gauge action has **6 plaquette orientations with $\langle110\rangle$
normals**, not 3 with $\langle100\rangle$ normals. Note these $\langle110\rangle$
axes are a third set, distinct from F141's 7 Wigner–Seitz facet axes
($4\,\langle111\rangle + 3\,\langle100\rangle$); no identification is claimed here.

**Reconstruction.** With $m_p:=(d_1\times d_2)/2$ and
$f^a:=(F^a_{23},-F^a_{13},F^a_{12})$, each loop phase is
$\Phi^a_p = F^a_{\mu\nu}d_1^\mu d_2^\nu = 2\,m_p\cdot f^a$. Because

$$\sum_{p=1}^{6} m_p m_p^{\mathsf T} = 4\,\mathbb{I}\qquad\text{(exact)}$$

the Cartesian field strength comes back in closed form with no pseudo-inverse and
no conditioning issue:

$$f^a=\tfrac18\sum_p m_p\,\Phi^a_p .$$

Verified against a constant-$F$ abelian embedding: recovered to $4.5\times10^{-8}$
(the $O((g\Phi)^2/6)$ of $\sin(g\Phi)/g$, not a geometry error) with
site-to-site spread $4.8\times10^{-17}$.

## 4. Both actions have the *same* continuum limit — which is why this hid

Summing over orientations (sympy, exact):

$$\sum_{p=1}^{6}\big(F_{\mu\nu}d_1^\mu d_2^\nu\big)^2 = 8\,F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\sum_{\mu<\nu}\big(4F_{\mu\nu}\big)^2 = 8\,F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}.$$

The difference is **identically zero**. The composite-SC action with `a2 = 4` and
the genuine BCC action with 6 rhombi of area $2\sqrt2$ have the *identical*
classical continuum limit and the identical isotropic normalisation; the U(1)
Wilson density tends to $4\varepsilon^2F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$ either way
(measured 3.99975 at $\varepsilon=0.01$).

So no weak-field normalisation check, and no $g_\text{lat}$ calibration, could
ever have detected the defect. **The migration does not move the continuum limit
and does not renormalise $g_\text{lat}$.**

## 5. §K — The no-go: the composite plaquette has an exact kernel

Setting the three composite links to the identity *identically* requires only

$$U_{(-1,1,1)}(y)=A(y-(2,0,0)),\quad U_{(1,-1,1)}(y)=A(y-(0,2,0)),\quad
U_{(1,1,-1)}(y)=A(y-(0,0,2)),$$

with $A:=U_{(1,1,1)}$ **completely free**. One of the four axis fields is
invisible to the composite construction. On such a configuration:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| max deviation of every composite plaquette from $\mathbb I$ | $1.1\times10^{-15}$ |
| composite-SC Wilson density | $2.4\times10^{-16}$ |
| max deviation of the 6 BCC plaquettes from $\mathbb I$ | **1.92** |
| BCC Wilson density | **0.999** |
| fraction of BCC plaquettes non-trivial | 100% |

Both actions annihilate pure gauge ($-6.1\times10^{-17}$, $-1.3\times10^{-16}$),
so both are legitimate gauge-invariant actions; the composite one is simply
blind. And the blindness is **not** a strong-field artefact — scanning the
amplitude with one fixed generator field:

| $\varepsilon$ | $S_\text{SC}$ | $S_\text{BCC}$ | $S_\text{BCC}/\varepsilon^2$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | $-4.0\times10^{-16}$ | 1.056 | 1.056 |
| 0.3 | $-6.7\times10^{-17}$ | 0.1963 | 2.181 |
| 0.1 | $6.0\times10^{-17}$ | 0.02326 | 2.326 |
| 0.03 | $1.1\times10^{-16}$ | 0.002109 | 2.344 |
| 0.01 | $1.2\times10^{-16}$ | 0.0002345 | **2.345** |

$S_\text{SC}\equiv0$ to round-off at every amplitude while $S_\text{BCC}\to
2.345\,\varepsilon^2$. The composite field strength misses **$O(\varepsilon)$
field strength**, i.e. it is wrong already at the perturbative order that lattice
perturbation theory works at.

## 6. §R — Rank count: exactly 1/3 of the curvature content

At quadratic order about $U=\mathbb I$ the colour index decouples and each
action's Hessian is $B^{\mathsf T}B$ with $B$ the signed loop-incidence matrix,
so $\mathrm{rank}=\dim G\cdot\mathrm{rank}(B)$. Per colour component, over $N_s$
BCC sites (verified $L=4,6,8$; formulae exact, not fits):

| | count |
|---|---|
| link variables | $4N_s$ |
| gauge directions | $N_s-1$ |
| $\mathrm{rank}$(BCC rhombic action) | $\mathbf{3N_s-3}$ |
| $\mathrm{rank}$(composite-SC action) | $\mathbf{2N_s-4}$ |
| torus holonomies (flat, physical) | 4 |
| **directions the composite cannot see** | $\mathbf{N_s+1}$ |

| $L$ | $N_s$ | blind / link space | blind / curvature content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 16 | 0.2656 | 0.3778 |
| 6 | 54 | 0.2546 | 0.3459 |
| 8 | 128 | 0.2520 | 0.3386 |
| $\to\infty$ | | **1/4** | **1/3** |

The composite construction resolves $2N_s$ of $3N_s$ curvature-carrying
directions. **One third of the field strength was missing**, at every amplitude.
The lost sector is one of the four $\langle111\rangle$ link axes: the composite
construction keeps only the 3 $\langle100\rangle$ channels it manufactures and
discards the 4th body-diagonal channel.

## 7. §B — Bianchi: the composite's good behaviour *was* the symptom

`ca_wmu.bianchi_residual` is the naive Cartesian curl
$\partial_xF_{yz}+\partial_yF_{zx}+\partial_zF_{xy}$ taken spectrally. On a
smooth periodic connection, scanning $k a=2\pi/L$ at fixed $g$:

| $L$ | $ka$ | rel. residual (BCC) | slope | rel. residual (composite) | slope |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 0.393 | $2.11\times10^{-2}$ | — | $1.33\times10^{-4}$ | — |
| 24 | 0.262 | $9.39\times10^{-3}$ | 2.00 | $4.48\times10^{-5}$ | 2.69 |
| 32 | 0.196 | $5.32\times10^{-3}$ | 1.97 | $1.94\times10^{-5}$ | 2.90 |
| 48 | 0.131 | $2.37\times10^{-3}$ | 1.99 | $5.93\times10^{-6}$ | 2.93 |
| 64 | 0.098 | $1.34\times10^{-3}$ | 1.99 | $2.52\times10^{-6}$ | 2.98 |

The BCC construction converges at the generic $O((ka)^2)$. The composite is
anomalously *better*, $O((ka)^3)$ — because its three planes all live on one
simple-cubic sublattice, where the product of a cube's six faces is an **exact
lattice identity**.

This resolves the open roadmap item `docs/roadmaps/next-steps.md:76` ("if the
composite BCC plaquette has structural $O(\epsilon)$ cross-direction
contributions, what transform reduces the Bianchi residual to zero?"). The answer
is that **no transform should**: the cross-direction terms are the physical
content of the 4th link axis, and the composite plaquette's small Bianchi
residual is the *same* restriction to 3 of 6 orientations that makes it blind.
The naive Cartesian-curl diagnostic is an SC-specific test that rewards the
defect. A BCC-native replacement (closed-surface plaquette product on the
Wigner–Seitz cell) is scoped, not built.

## 9. Scope — what this does and does not touch

**Safe.** The continuum limit and $g_\text{lat}$ are unmoved (§4), so nothing
calibrated through the classical normalisation shifts. The gauge propagators were
already BCC, so F91/F166/F250/F69/F105 are unaffected. F141's 14-facet/7-axis
Voronoi lemma and the exact algebra $m_W^2:m_Z^2=7:9$ are geometry-derived, not
action-derived.

**Needs re-derivation, not yet done.** Anything that measured a quantity *off the
composite action or an SC/hypercubic action*: F43/F33 (`a2=4`, three planes),
F94 (4D Wilson $\beta\leftrightarrow\sigma$, $\langle P\rangle=0.594$),
F146 ($\sigma a^2=0.322$), F101/F102/F110 (rotor stiffness, $\sigma_\text{lat}=0.2015$),
F86/F88 (measured $v=0.713$) and hence F124/F235 ($\sqrt\sigma/f_\pi$), and the
$d_1$ chain F144/F151/F152/F154/F155/F162/F163/F239. None of these are
*invalidated* by §4 — they are re-scoped as "measured on an action that is blind
to 1/3 of the curvature."

**Still cubic, explicitly open.**
1. `ca_lpt_*` — the entire one-loop chain is 4D hypercubic Wilson
   ($\hat k_\mu=2\sin(k_\mu/2)$, `range(4)`). F155/F239 already name the BCC-BZ
   vertex computation as the one remaining production-grade job; §2's factor 4
   says the BZ measure is also wrong there.
2. `ca_bgfield_loop` — folds a BCC propagator against continuum vertices on the
   **cubic** BZ with a `mod 2π` wrap. Per §2 this over-counts the spatial measure
   by 4 and samples the wrong zone. `make_kgrid_bcc` now supplies the fix; the
   correction has not been applied or re-run.
3. `ca_hypercharge` — docstring claims a BCC kinetic step; the code calls
   `ca_dirac._weyl_half_step_2c`, which builds only `KX, KY`. U(1)$_Y$ is on the
   2D $1/\sqrt2$ lattice. Promotion to 3D BCC is a physics job, not a rename.
4. Monte-Carlo actions (F94 4D cubic, F146 3D cubic) — to be rebuilt on the
   rhombic action.
5. The $\sqrt3$ question. The link side is now the integer BCC lattice (hop $d$,
   `np.roll` exact); the fermion walk uses $e^{ik\cdot d/\sqrt3}$, a *fractional*
   shift, i.e. the same lattice at a different unit of length ($q=k/\sqrt3$).
   These are consistent as a rescaling, but the walk is evaluated on the cubic
   FFT cube, which per §2 is 4 BZ copies. Whether the fermion sector needs the
   same measure correction is **open** and is the natural next item.

## 10. Bearing on the F49 8/7 gap

F147/F149 found the F49 gap is exactly $8/7$ and flagged hops:axes ($8:7$) as a
candidate. F153 tested that on the *fermion loop* and got no clean count. §6
shows why the test was in the wrong place: the composite gauge action carried
only 3 of the available channels and discarded the 4th $\langle111\rangle$ axis,
so no channel census taken against it could have been complete. With the rhombic
action the count is now $4$ link axes and $6$ plaquette orientations. **This is a
newly testable route to $\sin^2\theta_W=2/9$, not a claim** — the stiffness
computation on the migrated action has not been run.
