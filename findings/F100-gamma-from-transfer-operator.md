## 1. What this closes

F99 derived $\sigma$ as the centre-twist Lagrange multiplier and reduced confinement's remaining freedom to a single number — the centre-weight disorder $\gamma$ in $p(n)\propto e^{\gamma\cos(2\pi n/N)}$ — but characterised the map $\gamma(\Omega)$ from the unitary rotation tick to the Euclidean centre weight **only by its limits** (F99 §5). This finding gives the map in closed form, derived through the free rule's **transfer operator**.

## 2. The derivation — the rule is an oscillator, and its vacuum sets the disorder

**T1 — the rotation tick is a harmonic-oscillator phase rotation (machine-exact).** The rule rotates each conjugate $(E,B)$ mode by the angle $\Omega(k)$ per tick: $R(\Omega)$ is an orthogonal, $\det=1$ symplectic phase-space rotation (residual $2\times10^{-17}$), i.e. the evolution generated by the oscillator number operator, one-tick unitary $e^{-i\Omega(\hat N+\frac12)}$. **Wick-rotating** $t\to-i\tau$ turns this into the positive transfer operator $T=e^{-\Omega(\hat N+\frac12)}$, whose ground state is Gaussian with the standard per-mode plaquette-flux moment

$$\langle\phi^2\rangle_\text{mode}=\frac{1}{2\Omega}\qquad\text{(verified to }3\times10^{-16}\text{ by number-basis diagonalisation of }H=\tfrac12(p^2+\Omega^2q^2)).$$

So the rule's own rotation rate $\Omega$ fixes the quantum fluctuation of the flux: a faster (stiffer) rotation freezes the flux; a slower (softer) one lets it wander.

**T2 — the vacuum flux variance is a BZ average of $1/\Omega$ (convergent, from the rule).** The plaquette flux sums the magnetic modes, so the vacuum variance is the Brillouin-zone average of the rule's inverse dispersion,

$$\sigma_\phi^2=\frac1V\sum_{k\neq0}\frac{1}{2\Omega(k)}=\Big\langle\frac{1}{2\Omega(k)}\Big\rangle_\text{BZ},$$

computed on the **actual** `rotation_omega_2d` ($\Omega(k)=2\omega_{2D}(k/2)$, $c_\text{lat}=1/\sqrt2$). It converges (successive lattice refinements shrink geometrically, ratio $0.499$) to

$$\sigma_\phi^2\to0.40297,\qquad \sigma_1=\frac{\sigma_\phi^2}{2}=\frac14\Big\langle\frac1{\Omega}\Big\rangle_\text{BZ}=0.20148.$$

The IR is finite in 2D ($\Omega\sim|k|/\sqrt2$, $1/\Omega$ integrable; the $k=0$ pure-gauge mode is excluded).

**T3 — the map $\gamma(\Omega)$ (exact inversion).** The centre order parameter of the Gaussian vacuum is $s_1=e^{-\sigma_\phi^2/2}$; $\gamma$ is fixed by matching the F99 $\mathbb{Z}_3$ clock weight, $s_1^\text{clock}(\gamma)=s_1$ with $s_1^\text{clock}(\gamma)=\frac{1-e^{-3\gamma/2}}{1+2e^{-3\gamma/2}}$. Hence the requested map — a closed-form functional of the rule's whole dispersion:

$$\boxed{\;\gamma(\Omega)=\big(s_1^\text{clock}\big)^{-1}\!\Big(\exp\!\big[-\tfrac12\langle1/(2\Omega)\rangle_\text{BZ}\big]\Big)\;}$$

For the actual 2D rule: $s_1=0.81752$, $\gamma^\star=1.780$, clock round-trip exact ($10^{-16}$). $\gamma$ is monotone in dispersion stiffness — scaling $\Omega\to2\Omega$ raises $\gamma$ ($1.30\to1.78\to2.25$ for scale $\tfrac12,1,2$).

**T4 — the F99 D4 limits, now from the rule.** Tuning the rotation rate reproduces F99's hand-set limits: a stiff rule ($\Omega$ large) gives $\sigma_\phi^2\to0$, $\gamma\to\infty$, $\sigma\to0$ (deconfined); a soft rule ($\Omega\to0$) gives $\sigma_\phi^2\to\infty$, $\gamma\to0$, $\sigma\to\infty$ (confining). The disorder parameter is no longer free — it is set by the dispersion.

## 3. The headline

The string tension is one quarter the Brillouin-zone average of the inverse rotation rate of the update rule:

$$\sigma_1=\tfrac14\,\langle 1/\Omega(k)\rangle_\text{BZ}\;\;(\text{spin-wave / Gaussian regime}),\qquad \sigma_1^{\,2D}=0.2015.$$

Confinement's scale and the propagation speed are two faces of the same dispersion: $c_\text{lat}=d\Omega/d|k|$ at $k\to0$ (Finding 26) is the *slope* of $\Omega$, while $\sigma_1=\tfrac14\langle1/\Omega\rangle$ is its *inverse average* over the zone. A rule that propagates fast (stiff $\Omega$) confines weakly; a slow rule confines strongly.

## 4. Reconciliation (T5 — consistency, **not** an identity)

$\sigma_1\approx0.20$ is the same order of magnitude, and shares the BZ-inverse-dispersion-average origin, as F70's large-$\beta$ tension floor ($\sigma(\beta{=}20)=0.219$) and F95's $I_2=\langle\cot\omega_\text{kin}\rangle_\text{BCC}=0.2202$. This is flagged as a **consistency observation, not an identity**: F70's $\sigma(\beta)$ has no fixed floor — it keeps falling ($\sigma(60)=0.069<\sigma(20)$) — so the numerical proximity reflects a shared structure (an inverse-dispersion zone average), not an equality.

## 5. Honest scope

- **Gaussian / spin-wave (weak-fluctuation) regime.** $s_1=e^{-\sigma_\phi^2/2}$ is the free-rule (quadratic) vacuum result; the compact/non-perturbative corrections (the full Mathieu rotor ground state) and the genuinely strong-coupling tension are beyond it. This is exactly the regime in which the centre weight $\gamma$ is well-defined — the regime F99 §5 asked to fill.
- **Single-plaquette → area law is 2D.** $\sigma_1=\sigma_\phi^2/2$ as a *string tension* uses the F70 independent-plaquette mechanism (2D-exact); in higher $D$ it is the strong-coupling/centre-projected statement. The transfer-operator identity $\langle\phi^2\rangle=1/(2\Omega)$ and the map $\gamma(\Omega)$ themselves are dimension-independent.
- **Magnetic form factor.** The plaquette-flux smearing of $B$ is taken at its long-wavelength ($\to1$) normalisation; an $O(1)$ lattice form factor would rescale the absolute $\sigma_1$ (not the $\gamma(\Omega)$ structure or the limits).
- **A-vs-C $k$-dependence** persists exactly as in F98/F99 (Abelian $\sigma_2=2\sigma_1$ vs Casimir $\sigma_2=\sigma_1$).
