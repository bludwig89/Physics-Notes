## 1. The gap

F313 §6 needs: *the solution group of $a^2-b^2N=1$ over
$R=\mathbb C[w_1^{\pm},w_2^{\pm},w_3^{\pm}]$ is generated by the fundamental solution.* It cited
**Dubickas–Steuding**, *Elem. Math.* **59** (2004) 133–143, Thm 2. (It originally cited Abel 1826,
which contains no such theorem — corrected 2026-08-13.)

D–S is proved over a **polynomial** ring $k[x]$, and its descent runs on a degree satisfying

$$\deg f = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; f \text{ is a constant, hence a unit.} \tag{$*$}$$

**$(*)$ is false in a Laurent ring.** $\deg(1+w^{-1})=0$, yet $1+w^{-1}$ is not a unit — the units
of $R$ are exactly the monomials. So the theorem does not transfer for free. The review was right
that this, not "Abel", was the import.

---

## 2. The replacement: a two-sided width

Fix $\lambda\in\mathbb R^3$ with **$\mathbb Q$-linearly independent** components (this module uses
$(1,\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$). For $f\ne0$ in $R$ define

$$h_f(\lambda)=\max\{\langle m,\lambda\rangle : m\in\operatorname{supp}f\},
\qquad D(f)=h_f(\lambda)+h_f(-\lambda)\;\ge\;0 .$$

**L1 — genericity.** $\mathbb Q$-independence makes the $\lambda$-maximising monomial **unique**,
so nothing can cancel it: $h_{fg}=h_f+h_g$ and $\mathrm{lc}(fg)=\mathrm{lc}(f)\mathrm{lc}(g)$.
Measured on the tower: additivity residual and multiplicativity residual both **exactly 0**;
smallest observed maximiser gap **2.0**.

**L2 — $D$ detects units exactly.** $D(f)=0$ iff $\operatorname{supp}f$ lies in a hyperplane
$\perp\lambda$; $\mathbb Q$-independence allows at most **one** lattice point on such a hyperplane;
so

$$D(f)=0 \iff f \text{ is a monomial} \iff f \text{ is a unit of } R.$$

**This is the exact replacement for $(*)$, and unlike $(*)$ it is true.** The module checks it on
the very counterexample that breaks $(*)$: $1+w_1^{-1}$ is not a unit and gets width $1.0>0$, so
$D$ separates what $\deg$ could not.

**L3 — unitarity makes the Newton polytopes centrally symmetric.** The solutions that matter are
not arbitrary Pell solutions. They are **unitary** commutant elements with $\det=1$, so
$B^*=B^{-1}=\sigma(B)$, giving

$$a^*=a, \qquad b^*=-b .$$

Since $*$ sends $w^{m}\mapsto w^{-m}$, both supports are symmetric about the origin. Hence
$h(\lambda)=h(-\lambda)$, so $D=2h\ge0$, and $0$ lies in every Newton polytope in play. Measured
symmetry residual: **0.0**.

**L3 is the ingredient the polynomial proof neither has nor needs, and it is what makes the
Laurent case close.** Dropping unitarity leaves a rank-2 module on which the question has no
answer — the review said so, and this is why.

## 3. The descent

Let $\xi=a+bX$ solve the Pell equation, $X^2=N$, and write $c=h_u(\lambda)>0$
(measured $c=4.146264\ldots$, $D(u)=2c$).

**L4.** $h_a=h_b+c$, i.e. $D(a)=D(b)+D(u)$. From $a^2=1+b^2N$ with $h_N=2c>0$: the constant $1$
cannot reach the top, so $2h_a=2h_b+h_N$. Residual $3.6\times10^{-15}$.

**L5.** $\mathrm{lc}(a)=\pm i\,\mathrm{lc}(b)\,\mathrm{lc}(u)$, from $\mathrm{lc}(a)^2 =
\mathrm{lc}(b)^2\mathrm{lc}(N)$ and $\mathrm{lc}(N)=-\mathrm{lc}(u)^2$. Therefore in **exactly
one** of

$$b'=bu+ai \quad(\text{from }\xi A^{-1}), \qquad b''=bu-ai \quad(\text{from }\xi A)$$

the two top terms cancel; in the other they add. Call the non-cancelling one $b''$: then
$h_{b''}=h_b+c$ exactly, so $D(b'')=D(b)+2c$. Verified "exactly one cancels" at every $n$.

**L6.** $b'b''=1+b^2$, by direct expansion using $a^2=1+b^2(1-u^2)$ — verified as an **exact**
identity in the ring. Since $\mathrm{Newt}(b^2)$ is centrally symmetric it contains $0$, so adding
$1$ cannot enlarge it: $D(1+b^2)\le D(b^2)=2D(b)$.

**L7 — the descent.**

$$D(b') \;=\; D(1+b^2)-D(b'') \;\le\; 2D(b)-\bigl(D(b)+2c\bigr) \;=\; D(b)-2c \;<\; D(b).$$

One multiplication by $A^{\mp1}$ strictly lowers $D(b)$, by at least $D(u)=2c$. **Measured: it
lowers it by exactly $2c$, every time.**

**L8 — the base case.** $D(b)=0$ with $b\ne0$ makes $b$ a monomial (L2); $b^*=-b$ then forces $b$
to be a purely imaginary **constant**, $b=i\beta$ with $\beta$ real. Then
$(a-\beta u)(a+\beta u)=1-\beta^2$. If $\beta^2\ne1$ both factors are units, i.e. monomials, and
$2\beta u=\mu_2-\mu_1$ would make $u$ a difference of **two** monomials — but $\mathrm{Newt}(u)$ is
the full cube, **eight** of them. So $\beta^2=1$, $b=\mp i$, $a^2=u^2$, and $\xi\in\{\pm A^{\pm1}\}$.
And $b=0$ gives $a^2=1$, $\xi=\pm1$.

**Descent + base case:**

$$\boxed{\ \{\text{solutions}\}=\{\pm A^{n} : n\in\mathbb Z\}\ }$$

which is F313 §6's boxed claim, **with no import**.

---

## 4. Why the hypothesis $D(b)>0$ is not decoration

At $D(b)=0$ we have $b=\pm i$, so $1+b^2=0$ and L6's product identity degenerates ($b'=0$). That
is precisely the base case, and L8 handles it. The first run of the verification applied the
descent to $n=1$ too and the lemma "failed" — correctly, because $n=1$ *is* the base case. The
hypothesis is load-bearing and is asserted as such.

---

## 5. What this changes, and what it does not

**Discharges.** CL269's last contingency. F313's chain is now closed in-repo end to end. The
*result* is unchanged — no physics moves — but it stops resting on a theorem stated for the wrong
ring.

**Does not change** F313's other named inputs: $s=2$, **infinite volume**, and the three Cayley
generators. If anything infinite volume is *more* visible here: L1 and L2 both use that $R$ is a
domain whose units are monomials, and neither holds for $\mathbb C[\Lambda/L\Lambda]$.

**Does not claim generality.** This proves the descent for **this** $N$ (squarefree, with
$\mathrm{Newt}(u)$ the full cube) **under unitarity**. It is not a re-proof of Dubickas–Steuding in
several variables, and the module says so rather than implying otherwise.

## 6. Falsifiers

1. **Exhibit a solution with $b\ne0$ not of the form $\pm A^n$.** The descent is wrong.
2. **Exhibit $f$ with $D(f)=0$ that is not a monomial** — L2 dies and with it the replacement
   for $(*)$. (Requires a $\lambda$ with rationally dependent components; the module fixes a
   $\mathbb Q$-independent one and reports the maximiser gap.)
3. **Exhibit a unitary commutant element with $a^*\ne a$ or $b^*\ne-b$.** L3 dies and the
   Newton polytopes are no longer centrally symmetric.
4. **Show $D(1+b^2)>2D(b)$ for some solution.** L6's bound dies and the descent inequality with
   it.
5. **Show the drop is not bounded below by $2c$** — e.g. a solution where the descent stalls.
   Then termination is unproved even if each step is non-increasing.
6. **Show $\mathrm{Newt}(u)$ is not the full cube** for the model's own walk. L8's base case
   reopens, since $u$ could then be a difference of two monomials.
