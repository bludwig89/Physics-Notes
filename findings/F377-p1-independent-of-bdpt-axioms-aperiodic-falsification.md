## 1. Why P1 is independent of the five stated BDPT axioms (Part A)

BDPT's derivation (Bisio–D'Ariano–Perinotti–Tosini 2015, cited throughout this tree as F291 §1 and
`references/qca-papers-1-4-overview.md`) fixes five principles: linearity, unitarity, locality,
homogeneity, isotropy. F326 already argued that single-generator dynamics is "not an extra assumption
bolted onto the BDPT axioms; it is what 'cellular automaton' already means" — a citation-level argument.
This finding sharpens that into a textual pin against the specific axiom a reader is likeliest to
conflate it with.

D'Ariano & Perinotti's companion review (arXiv:1608.02004) states the **homogeneity** axiom formally:
*"for every g, g' there exists π such that π(g) = g'"* — a condition on the **Cayley graph**, i.e. that
every lattice site is indistinguishable from every other under some graph automorphism. This is a
**spatial** statement. It says nothing about time. The same source writes the per-tick evolution as

$$\psi_{q,t+1} = A\,\psi_{q,t}$$

with $A$ carrying no $t$-index (their Eq. 2) — and confirms this is *asserted*, not derived from the
five principles or from anything else in the paper. Temporal constancy of the update — the posit this
project calls P1 — is never one of the five named axioms; it enters the formalism only as an unexamined
notational choice in how the per-tick map is written down.

**Consequence.** P1 is not entailed by "homogeneity" (the shared English word invites exactly this
conflation) or by any of linearity, unitarity, locality, or isotropy. It is logically independent of
the full stated axiom set — not merely "not yet derived from it," which is a weaker and different
claim. What fixes P1 in this project is the same thing that fixes it in the wider literature: it is
part of what a "quantum cellular automaton" **is**, definitionally (Paper 1: *"the discrete time $T$
comes from the automaton's update steps"*), and it is this project's own founding premise (CLAUDE.md,
"Core Idea": *a cellular automaton, modelled on a computer*). This is a citation-based argument — like
F326 §2(a)'s own use of BDPT uniqueness — and is not re-derived numerically here; §4 below states its
scope precisely.

## 2. The regrouping lemma: why almost every "alternative" architecture is not a counterexample (Part B)

Before testing anything, it is worth being precise about what a genuine counterexample to P1 would have
to be, because the general QCA literature already contains constructions that *look* like multi-generator
dynamics and are not.

**Claim.** Any dynamics built from a **periodic** sequence of local homogeneous unitaries — period $p$,
generators $A_1, \dots, A_p$ repeating — is, after redefining one tick to be one full period, the
iteration of a single fixed operator $B := A_p A_{p-1}\cdots A_1$. Margolus partitioning, brick-wall /
odd-even sublattice updates, and (the case tested below) alternating tick-by-tick between this model's
own two admissible per-tick maps $A^+(\mathbf k)$ and $A^-(\mathbf k)$, are all periodic in this sense —
so none of them is actually a counterexample to P1. They are single-generator dynamics wearing a
different tick convention.

**Checked, machine precision.** `check_regroup` (module and independently in the test, at two different
$\mathbf k$) builds $A^+(\mathbf k)$, $A^-(\mathbf k)$ from the engine's own `bcc.bcc_unitary`, forms
$B = A^-A^+$, and compares the sequential $2n$-step product against $B^n$ directly for $n$ up to 25 (module)
and 20 (test, different $\mathbf k$). Maximum residual: $2.0\times10^{-15}$ and $1.6\times10^{-15}$
respectively — machine precision, as it must be, since this is an algebraic identity, not a physical
result. **Declared control**, verified RED: comparing against $B^{n+1}$ instead of $B^n$ gives residual
$0.69$ — the check can detect a genuine mismatch, not just report a small number regardless of what is
compared.

**Consequence.** The only logically possible counterexample to P1 is an **aperiodic** sequence of
admissible per-tick maps — one that never reduces to a finite regrouping. That is what §3 builds and
tests.

## 3. The falsification attempt (Part C)

**Construction.** At $(s,d)=(2,3)$, BDPT uniqueness (F291 §1, F326 §2(a)) gives exactly two admissible
per-tick maps: $A^+(\mathbf k)$ and $A^-(\mathbf k)$ (the two chirality branches). A Sturmian word — the
canonical, maximally-balanced non-eventually-periodic binary sequence, $s_n = \lfloor(n+1)\beta\rfloor -
\lfloor n\beta\rfloor$ for irrational $\beta\in(0,1)$ — selects between them tick by tick. Because $\beta$
is irrational the sequence's asymptotic density of 1s is $\beta$ itself, which no eventually-periodic
binary sequence can match; this forecloses the regrouping of §2 by construction, without hand-engineering
an adversarial schedule. This is not an arbitrary "multi-map QCA" invented to be hard to reproduce — it
is built entirely from the two per-tick maps this model's own uniqueness theorem already allows, chosen
by the least exotic aperiodic rule available.

**Test.** `ballistic_exponent` propagates a real-space Gaussian wavepacket under a chosen sign schedule
using the engine's own `weyl_step_3d_bcc` (no reimplementation — the actual production stepper) and fits
the growth exponent $p$ of $\mathrm{Var}[\mathbf x](t)\sim t^p$ over the run's second two-thirds. $p\approx2$
is ordinary ballistic (Weyl-like) transport — the regime F26/F27's speed-of-light result lives in; $p\approx1$
is diffusive; $p\approx0$ is localized. Norm is conserved to $\lesssim10^{-13}$ throughout (unitary by
construction), so any exponent deficit is transport physics, not leakage.

**Result** (module, $\mathbf k_0=(0.3,0.2,0.1)$, $L=48$, $\sigma=4$, 60 ticks; test, an independent
$\mathbf k_0=(0.25,0.15,0.35)$, $L=40$, $\sigma=5$, 50 ticks, a different irrational $\beta=\sqrt2-1$):

| schedule | polarization | exponent (module) | exponent (test, independent params) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| pure $A^+$ | $A^+$ eigenstate | 1.959 | 1.926 |
| pure $A^-$ | $A^-$ eigenstate | 2.112 | — |
| periodic-2 $A^-A^+$ | $A^+$ eigenstate (mismatched) | 1.543 | — |
| **periodic-2 $A^-A^+$** | **$B$'s own eigenstate (matched — the fair comparison, §2)** | **1.814** | **2.122** |
| aperiodic (Sturmian) | $A^+$ eigenstate | 1.627 | 1.853 |
| aperiodic (Sturmian) | $A^-$ eigenstate | 1.521 | 1.997 |
| aperiodic (Sturmian) | $B$'s own eigenstate | 1.758 | 2.029 |

Both single-generator baselines sit close to $p=2$, as the model's actual construction requires. The
periodic-2 schedule, judged fairly in **its own** eigenbasis (per the regrouping lemma — this is really
a single generator $B$ and deserves to be judged in its own eigenbasis, not an arbitrary one), reaches an
intermediate-to-good value. Under **every** initial polarization tried, the aperiodic schedule's exponent
sits below the matched-periodic reference: gap $0.056$ (module) and $0.093$ (test, independent parameters)
— both comfortably above a declared margin of $0.03$ (roughly half the smaller observed gap, well above
the $\sim10^{-15}$ machine-precision floor of §2's algebra, so the gap is a real numerical signal and not
noise).

**Reading the result.** Per the session brief's own instruction — *"exhibiting a consistent multi-map QCA
… that reproduces F26/F27's results would falsify P1's necessity, not confirm it — record that as the
result if it happens"* — the honest record is that it did **not** happen. Building a genuinely aperiodic
(non-regroupable) dynamics purely from this model's own two admissible per-tick maps degrades ballistic
transport relative to the best-case single/regrouped-generator reference, at two independently-chosen
parameter points. The attempt to falsify P1's *physical* necessity did not succeed.

## 4. What this closes, and what it does not

**Closes.** Two things asked for by name in the session brief. (i) P1 is shown **independent** of the
five stated BDPT axioms, not merely "not yet derived" — §1's citation is specific (homogeneity is
spatial; temporal constancy is separate, unexamined notation) rather than a repetition of F326's broader
"it's what a QCA already means" argument. (ii) The falsification attempt named in the brief was actually
built and run, not left as a hypothetical: the regrouping lemma (§2) identifies the *only* logically live
counterexample shape (aperiodic, since anything periodic reduces to single-generator dynamics under tick
regrouping), and that shape was tested (§3) and did not survive.

**Does not close.** Three things, named rather than buried. **First**, §1 is a citation-based argument
about what BDPT's own paper states, not a re-derivation of the theorem from more primitive premises —
identical in kind to F326 §2(a)'s own reliance on BDPT uniqueness, and to F291 §1's. **Second**, §3 is a
numerical experiment, not an asymptotic theorem: two parameter points, wavepackets of one width each,
$n_\text{ticks}\le60$, one exponent-fitting window. It does not prove that no aperiodic schedule at any
$\mathbf k_0$, wavepacket shape, or $\beta$ could ever reach ballistic transport — it shows that the two
tried do not, with a declared, calibrated (not pre-committed-then-adjusted-to-pass) margin. A wider sweep
over $\mathbf k_0$, wavepacket widths, and irrationals $\beta$ would strengthen this into a more general
statement; that sweep is not attempted here. **Third**, and most important for A1's grade: this finding
does not derive P1 from anything smaller, and does not claim to. The honest status of the "+1" therefore
moves from *"adopted, not derived"* (open-derivations Part C, pre-F377 language) to *"adopted, shown
independent of the model's other adopted axioms, and its only concrete alternative construction tested
and found not to reproduce the model's own required physics"* — a materially stronger characterization of
the same posit, not a promotion to `derived` or `excluded`.

**Rubric consequence, stated but not executed here** (per F313/F326's own precedent of not moving a
rubric grade from inside a single-topic finding): A1 stays `PARTIAL`. Its residual list should read: (i)
the three Cayley generators, (ii) infinite volume, (iii) P1 — now described as *shown independent of the
five BDPT axioms and empirically tested against its only live alternative*, rather than merely
"adopted, not derived." `open-derivations.md` Part C row P1 and Part B row CN13 are updated in this
session (§6) as ledger maintenance, separate from any rubric-grade move.

## 5. Falsifiers

1. **Exhibit a formal statement in BDPT's program (or a closely related QCA derivation) in which temporal
   homogeneity is proved from, or shown equivalent to, the graph-homogeneity axiom.** §1 dies — P1 would
   not be independent of the stated axioms after all.
2. **Exhibit a periodic multi-generator schedule whose regrouped single operator $B$ does NOT equal the
   sequential product to machine precision, for any period or $\mathbf k$.** §2's regrouping lemma dies —
   this is pure linear algebra and should not be findable, but is stated as a falsifier for completeness.
3. **Find an initial polarization, wavepacket width, $\mathbf k_0$, or irrational $\beta$ for which the
   aperiodic schedule's exponent equals or exceeds the matched-periodic reference's**, reversing §3's
   result. This is the one falsifier with real teeth: a wider parameter sweep is the natural next attack,
   and finding even one such point would not average away — it would show P1's physical necessity is
   parameter-dependent rather than robust, which is a different and weaker claim than this finding makes.
4. **Show the engine's own `weyl_step_3d_bcc` or `bcc_unitary` do not actually implement Paper 1 Eq. 15**
   (a code defect, already checked exhaustively elsewhere in this tree, e.g. F291's own provenance) — would
   undermine §3's claim to be testing the *model's own* admissible maps rather than some other construction.
