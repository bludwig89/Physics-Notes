**The answer, claim by claim — narrowed to what the review actually found holds.**

1. **Momentum conservation — not achieved exactly at any coupling tested, confirming F389's structural prediction.** A partial positive result exists **on-axis only**: the field's and matter's momentum changes are nearly anti-parallel (`cos = -0.989` at the default configuration) — but this **inverts to near-exactly parallel (`cos = +0.99995`) for an off-axis beam** (§3). `g_lat` is also not "the coupling" for this whole claim: it controls only the field-sourcing (backreaction) half of the loop; the matter push is set by `q`/beam amplitude and is independent of `g_lat` to `<0.4%` over an 8× range (§4).

3. **Magnitude (`Δp=ΔE/c`) — not gated, and not well-posed for this construction** (§5, unchanged from the first version's analysis — this part of the review found no error).
4. **Stretch — Thomson cross-section — explicitly out of scope this session** (§6, unchanged).

## 1. The scenario

`scenarios/photon_fermion_push.yaml`: a genuine, `Ĉ(k)`-transverse beam packet (`photon.build_beam_packet`, `m_index=2`, `axis=0`, `sigma=3.0`, amplitude `0.15`, projected via `em_photon_sourcing.split_transverse_longitudinal` before injection — the same F386/F387/F388-established reason: the helper polarizes transverse to the Euclidean `k̂`, not `Ĉ(k)`) seeded into `em_photon`'s initial state, plus a localized Weyl packet **at rest** (`k0=(0,0,0)`, the roadmap's own phrase) in `fermion_em`, with F389's `use_radiative_current=True` so the fermion's own recoil can source real field momentum back. `EmPhotonChannel.init_state` gained a new, backward-compatible `beam_amp`/`beam_m_index`/`beam_axis`/`beam_sigma` config option (default `beam_amp=0`, i.e. F388/F389's own self-sourced-only convention, unchanged) so the scenario YAML itself — not just this finding's own Python harness — faithfully reproduces the exact physics reported below (verified bit-for-bit against the harness's direct-state-injection construction, independently re-confirmed in review). `g_lat=0.6` is a measured local minimum of the conservation residual for this configuration (§2), not a claimed universal optimum.

## 3. The axis breakdown — found in review, now gated explicitly

**This finding's first version claimed "Direction — CONFIRMED" and reported the anti-parallel result as "robust across every perturbation swept — width, amplitude, beam mode." Both statements were checked against real data by the independent review and found factually wrong as written: the disclosed sweep never varied the beam's propagation axis, and the beam-mode sweep never went past `m_index=3`.**

Measured directly (default config except as noted, `g_lat=0.6`):

| Beam config | `cos(ΔP_matter, k̂_beam)` | `cos(ΔP_matter, ΔP_field)` |
|---|---|---|
| `m_index=1, axis=0` (x) | `0.991` | — |
| `m_index=2, axis=0` (x, **the default**) | `0.995` | `-0.989` |
| `m_index=3, axis=0` (x) | `0.983` | — |
| `m_index=4, axis=0` (x) | **`-0.988`** (sign reversed) | `-0.978` |
| `m_index=2, axis=1` (y) | **`-0.0019`** (no correlation) | **`+0.99995`** (inverted to parallel) |
| `m_index=3, axis=1` (y) | `-0.034` | — |
| `m_index=2, axis=2` (z) | `-0.0093` | — |

The direction correlation is robust only for a beam propagating along the lattice's x-axis and only up to `m_index=3` (of a Nyquist maximum `L/2=8` at `L=16`) — it is **not** a Brillouin-zone-edge curiosity; `m_index=4` is simply twice the finding's own default carrier wavenumber, well inside what a reader would expect "swept" to mean. For a beam along y or z, the recoil direction is essentially uncorrelated with the beam's own `k̂`, and — the more striking finding — the on-axis anti-parallel result between `ΔP_matter` and `ΔP_field` does not merely weaken off-axis, it **inverts to near-exactly parallel** (`cos=+0.99995`, i.e. field and matter momentum changes point almost exactly the *same* way, the opposite of a momentum exchange).

**Likely mechanism, not proven here.** `findings/F387-curl-anisotropy-omega-pair-mismatch.md` §1 already establishes, as fact, that `Ĉ(k) → (k_x,-k_y,k_z)/|k|` — an exact, axis-asymmetric sign in the lattice's own curl generator — and that `photon.build_beam_packet` polarizes transverse to the Euclidean `k̂`, not `Ĉ(k)` (F386 §6), leaking `Ĉ(k)`-longitudinal content (measured `‖E_L‖/‖E‖≈27.5%` for this finding's own default beam, independently reproduced in review). Both established, pre-existing facts plausibly combine to produce the axis-dependent breakdown measured here; deriving the precise mechanism (rather than measuring its consequence) is not attempted in this finding.

**`g_lat` is not "the coupling" for the whole scenario.** `‖ΔP_matter‖` changes by less than `0.4%` over `g_lat∈[0,3]` (an 8×-and-beyond range) while scaling linearly in `q·amp` at `g_lat=0` (verified: `q=0.5,1,2` at fixed `amp` give `‖ΔP_matter‖=0.0218, 0.0410, 0.0646` — exactly linear). `g_lat` only ever controls how much the field's own bookkeeping catches up to a push that is already fully determined by `q` and the beam amplitude. Describing claim 1's `g_lat` sweep (§2's residual table) as "sweeping the coupling" without this caveat would leave a reader thinking the whole scenario's coupling strength is being varied, when only half of it is.

## 4. The conservation residual's local minimum — what it is and isn't

The residual `‖ΔP_matter+ΔP_field‖/‖ΔP_matter‖` has a genuine, smooth local minimum near `g_lat≈0.6-0.65` for the default configuration (confirmed in review with a finer scan: `0.282` at `g=0.5` → `0.151` at `g=0.65` → `0.209` at `g=0.75`) — not measurement noise or a jagged artifact. But it is **structurally guaranteed to exist, not a physical resonance**: with `cos(ΔP_matter,ΔP_field)` nearly constant and `‖ΔP_matter‖` nearly flat while `‖ΔP_field‖` grows monotonically (linearly, §3) in `g_lat`, the law of cosines forces `‖ΔP_matter+ΔP_field‖` to be a convex function of `‖ΔP_field‖` with an interior minimum wherever `‖ΔP_field‖` crosses near `‖ΔP_matter‖·|\cos\theta|` — this happens for *any* configuration with these three qualitative properties, regardless of the underlying physics being close to conservation. This finding's own gate leg name (`closure_residual_has_interior_minimum`) and its caveats already avoid calling this a "sweet spot" or an approach to conservation; this section makes the *reason* explicit rather than leaving a reader to assume it is a resonance.

## 5. Claim 3 in detail — why `Δp=ΔE/c` is not a well-posed test here, not merely a failed one

*(Unchanged from this finding's first version — the review found no error in this section.)*

The roadmap's claim 3 asks whether the fermion's recoil momentum matches the field's absorbed energy divided by `c`. Measured directly: `‖ΔP_matter‖` is `0.0410` at `g_lat=0` and stays within `0.4%` of that value across the entire tested `g_lat∈[0,1.2]` range (`0.0408` at `g_lat=0.6`) — it is **set almost entirely by the unconditional per-link push**, essentially independent of the coupling constant that governs how much energy the field loses or gains. Meanwhile `ΔE_field` (the field's own energy change) is exactly zero at `g_lat=0` and grows with `g_lat` (measured `0.0037` at `g_lat=0.3`, `0.0113` at `g_lat=0.6`) — entirely a property of the separate radiative-sourcing mechanism, and (confirmed independently in review with a finer `g_lat` sweep) the field's tracked energy is *increasing* — pumped by the sourcing term, not drained by absorption from the beam. **These two quantities are governed by different mechanisms with no shared coupling constant, so there is no physical basis for expecting `‖ΔP_matter‖·c ≈ ΔE_field`, and they manifestly do not track each other**: the ratio `Δp/(ΔE_field/c)` spans `29.8` (`g_lat=0.1`) down to `1.3` (`g_lat=0.8`) in review's own independent re-measurement — over an order of magnitude, with no principled value to compare it to, and formally undefined (division by exactly zero) at `g_lat=0`, precisely where the push is cleanest. This is reported as a precisely characterized negative/inapplicable result, not a near-miss — forcing a numerical threshold onto it would manufacture a false impression of a test that was actually run.

A cleaner, alternative operationalization was tried and is reported for completeness, not gated: does the field's *own* radiated energy-momentum content satisfy the massless dispersion `ΔE_field ≈ c·‖ΔP_field‖` (i.e., is what it radiates consistent with genuine photon content, independent of the fermion's own energy accounting)? Measured: the ratio `ΔE_field/(c·‖ΔP_field‖)` is `0.26` at `g_lat=0.15`, `0.35` at `g_lat=0.3`, `0.53` at `g_lat=0.6`, `0.89` at `g_lat=1.2` — rising toward, but not reaching, `1` over the tested range, and (checked in one perturbation, `width=3.0`) can even go **negative** (a transient field-energy *decrease* during the run at that configuration) — not a stable, coupling-independent relation. Plausible cause, not established here: the field's tracked energy mixes genuine radiative content with near-field/transient buildup from the still-settling source, which does not itself propagate at `c` or carry the same momentum-per-energy ratio. Not resolved; flagged for whoever next wants a clean `E=pc` check to build a longer-run, quasi-steady-state version of this measurement rather than a single 20-tick snapshot.

## 6. Claim 4 (Thomson cross-section) — explicitly deferred, and why

*(Unchanged from this finding's first version; the review's own obstacle list, reproduced here, sharpens rather than contradicts it.)* The roadmap names this a "stretch" goal and this finding treats it as one. Extracting a meaningful scattering cross-section needs: (1) **a genuine far-field** — this lattice is periodic and spectral (`L=16`, FFT-based), so the seeded beam wraps around and re-interacts with itself and the recoiling fermion within a few tens of ticks, with no asymptotic "outgoing wave into a solid angle" to define a differential cross-section against; (2) **a clean incident flux** — after the `Ĉ(k)`-transverse projection every seeded beam needs (§1, §3), `~27%` of the raw beam's own norm is discarded as longitudinal leakage at this configuration, so the object actually driving the dynamics is not the clean plane-wave-like beam a cross-section calculation wants; (3) **a closing energy/momentum budget** — §3's channel-decoupling and §5's non-absorption finding mean there is no well-defined "scattered fraction" to normalize against. Computing a cross-section now would produce a number whose meaning depends on an unresolved modeling choice, which this project's own standards (algebraic exactness before machine precision, machine precision before a number is reported) argue against attempting prematurely. Deferred, not attempted; see caveats for a spawned follow-up suggestion.

## 8. Caveats

- **The on-axis results (direction, anti-alignment) are genuine and reproducible for their own narrow domain** (x-axis beams, `m_index≤3` at `L=16`) — this finding does not retract them, only their claimed generality. A future session wanting the claims to hold generally would need to either derive/apply a genuinely `Ĉ(k)`-transverse beam construction (removing the mismatch this finding and F387 both trace the breakdown to) or characterize the breakdown's own dependence on `L`/`m_index`/axis in closed form.
- **`g_lat=0.6`'s local minimum in the conservation residual is a property of this specific configuration, not a claimed universal "optimal coupling"** (§4) — it is a structurally-guaranteed crossing point of a flat and a linear curve, not a physical resonance.
- **The anti-alignment mechanism (on-axis) is offered as a plausibility argument, not a derivation** — no closed-form relation between F385's per-link recoil and F389's current construction is derived; a future session should attempt that derivation directly if the mechanism itself becomes load-bearing for a stronger claim.
- **Claim 3's `E=pc`-style alternative check (§5) used only a single 20-tick snapshot per configuration**, and was seen to go negative at at least one perturbation (`width=3.0`) — it is disclosed as an informational, non-monotonic-in-practice measurement, not a stable relation, and is **not gated** for exactly that reason.
- **Claim 4 (Thomson limit) is deferred, not attempted** (§6) — a background-task suggestion is spawned for a future session, contingent on resolving §3's channel-decoupling and axis-anisotropy issues first.
- **The axis-robustness legs added in review test only one off-axis configuration (`axis=1`, `m_index=2`)** — not a full axis/`m_index` grid. The full breakdown table (§3) is disclosed for context; only two representative points are promoted to permanent gate legs.
- Only the Weyl 2-spinor / U(1) sector is covered, matching F384–F389's own scope. The exchange-bus registration-order caveat F388/F389 already disclosed (config-key-based coupling not visible to the typed bus for a true 2-cycle) applies unchanged here — this scenario's channel order was verified manually, not by an independent bus check.
