## The question

The quantum-computing cross-test (chat, 2026-07-01) exposed a fault line in the model. Its **implementation** is a unitary QCA that reproduces every small-scale quantum phenomenon (CHSH at Tsirelson $2\sqrt2$ to $4.4\times10^{-16}$, two-slit, Zeno, Born rule). Its **many-body sectors**, however, are all *mean-field / first-quantised*:

- `ca_manybody.py` — Hartree self-consistent electrons + a single-Gaussian variational nuclear cluster (a single product / Slater determinant),
- the QM-1 CHSH lattice test — builds its "singlet" as a **product** `A_up · B_down`, i.e. the entanglement is *inserted by hand*, never generated.

A mean-field state lives in a Hilbert space of dimension $\sim 2N$ ($N$ = number of cells), not $2^N$, and carries **zero** entanglement entropy by construction. Quantum computing's advantage requires exactly the $2^N$ many-body entanglement a mean-field substrate cannot represent. So the sharp, decidable question is:

> Built from the model's **own** primitives, does a genuine many-body lattice register **generate** entanglement entropy from a product input — the resource quantum computing needs — or does it stay mean-field?

## What was built (both gates native to the model)

A dense $2^n$ tensor-product register (`Register`) whose amplitude array has $2^n$ independent entries, so it *can* carry arbitrary entanglement. It is only ever **initialised** from single-cell product kets; any entanglement present later was generated by the dynamics, not supplied.

- **Single-qubit gate** = the model's per-cell SU(2) rotor $R(\theta,\hat n)=\cos\theta\,I-i\sin\theta\,(\hat n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)=e^{-i\theta\,\hat n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}$ — identical in form to the `ca_dirac` F26 mass/coupling rotation $\cos\theta\,I\pm i\sin\theta\,A$ with $A=\hat n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$, $A^2=I$.
- **Two-qubit gate** = the nearest-neighbour spinor **exchange** interaction $H_\text{exch}=\boldsymbol\sigma_A\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma_B$, $U_\text{exch}(\theta)=e^{-i\theta\,\boldsymbol\sigma_A\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma_B}$. Two adjacent identical Weyl cells coupled by the lattice hopping term (kinetic coefficient $n=\sqrt{1-m^2}$, `ca_dirac`) develop a Heisenberg super-exchange $J\,\boldsymbol\sigma_A\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma_B$ at second order in the hop; this is the lattice-native origin of the gate. Its magnitude sets the *rate*; the entanglement generated depends only on the accumulated angle $\theta=\int\!J\,dt$.

No Bell, GHZ, or any entangled amplitude is written anywhere in the engine.

## The crux — entanglement is an *output*, not an *input*

The initial state is a genuine tensor product of single-cell kets, verified to have $S_0=0$ to machine precision. The entangler is $e^{-i\theta\,\boldsymbol\sigma_A\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma_B}$, built only from Pauli operators the model already uses. Evolving the product $\lvert\!\uparrow\downarrow\rangle$ gives

$$\lvert\!\uparrow\downarrow\rangle\;\xrightarrow{\,U_\text{exch}(\theta)\,}\;e^{i\theta}\cos 2\theta\,\lvert\!\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-i\,e^{i\theta}\sin 2\theta\,\lvert\!\downarrow\uparrow\rangle,$$

so the reduced density matrix has eigenvalues $\{\cos^2 2\theta,\ \sin^2 2\theta\}$ and $S(\theta)=-p\ln p-(1-p)\ln(1-p)$, $p=\cos^2 2\theta$. At $\theta=\pi/8$ this is exactly $\ln 2$ — a maximally entangled (Bell) state produced from a product input. $U_\text{exch}(\pi/8)$ is therefore a **perfect entangler**; with the model's full single-qubit rotor set it is **universal** for quantum computation (Bremner et al. 2002). The substrate can, in principle, run any quantum algorithm.

## The decisive contrast — the mean-field substrate provably cannot host it

By the Schmidt decomposition, the largest fidelity **any** single product state $\lvert a\rangle\otimes\lvert b\rangle$ can have with the Bell output is the largest squared Schmidt coefficient, computed here as $\lambda_\text{max}=\tfrac12$ exactly. An explicit time-dependent Hartree evolution — projecting back onto the best product form after each infinitesimal exchange step, exactly what a substrate with no $2^n$ amplitude array is forced to do — keeps $S=2.2\times10^{-16}$ throughout and reaches at most $50\%$ fidelity to the true state. The entanglement entropy the exact dynamics generate, $\ln 2$, is precisely the quantity `ca_manybody.py`'s ansatz discards. This is the mechanised statement of why the current many-body sectors, and the hand-inserted CHSH "singlet," are not yet quantum-computational.

## Interpretation — which reading of the model QC lives in

The cross-test framed two readings:

- **Genuine-QCA reading** — the lattice is a true unitary quantum system with a $2^n$ tensor-product Hilbert space. This finding **realises** that reading: entanglement is generated dynamically from the model's own gates, and the substrate is universal. Under this reading the model **supports quantum computing** and is consistent with all 2026 hardware results (fault-tolerant scaling, certified randomness, error correction), because at bottom it *is* standard unitary QM.
- **Mean-field / hand-inserted reading** — the only reading the *previously shipped* sectors implement — is falsified as a description of quantum computation: it cannot represent the generated entropy (T3).

The finding does **not** claim the model derives a *classical-resource* shortcut to $\text{BQP}$; on the contrary, it shows the substrate must carry the full exponential state to do quantum computing. This tightens the tension flagged in the CLAUDE.md philosophy: the "elegant, simple, deterministic universe-in-a-bottle" is honest only at the single-cell **rule** level, not at the computational-**resource** level — the $2^n$ Hilbert space is irreducibly present. In 't Hooft-CAI terms (`t-hooft-2015-cai-summary.md` §5.4), this register is the **template** view; whether an underlying ontic classical CA reproduces it *with polynomial resources* remains the open problem, and scaling QC hardware is the experiment deciding it.

## What remains open

1. **Derive the super-exchange coupling $J$** from the `ca_dirac` two-cell hopping at second order (fixes the *rate* of entanglement generation; the entropy result is $J$-independent).
2. **Embed the register in the live lattice** — currently the $2^n$ register is an abstract tensor product; wiring genuine multi-cell entanglement into `casim`'s field evolution (beyond the mean-field `ca_manybody.py`) is the natural next module.
3. **Resource question** — quantify how the required amplitude count grows for a model "algorithm," making explicit where the mean-field sectors would have to be abandoned.
