## 1. What was being tested, and why

`docs/status/open-derivations.md` row **d1** (= E3 = Q1 = Q2) is the model's single largest open number. F337 (2026-08-30) closed ledger row L6 (which object is the rule's gauge action; is the redundant link-axis mode dynamical) and ran the native $n=28$–$40$ sweep the ledger called for, finding a genuinely slow convergence rate ($\sim n^{-1.1}$ to $n^{-1.35}$, against the Wilson side's own $\sim n^{-2.5}$ to $n^{-2.8}$) and naming a candidate mechanism: the sharp Wigner–Seitz-cell domain mask's $O(1/n)$-class staircase discretisation error. F350 (2026-09-02) built and verified an anti-aliased cut-cell replacement mask, re-ran the identical computation, and measured **no improvement** — the fitted exponent and extrapolated $\Lambda_{\overline{\rm MS}}/\Lambda_\text{rule}$ are statistically unchanged from F337's own numbers. F350 §5 states plainly that F337's own exhaustion condition for the "non-convergence, not falsification" reading now fires, and closes with two named, untested candidates for the *actual* mechanism (§5, §7):

> candidates not investigated here: the vertex form factors' own behaviour approaching the BZ boundary; some other feature of the `phys`-branch projection; a genuine, physical breakdown of the $g_s=\tfrac12$/branch-B monotonicity assumption itself

This finding attacks the first of those — a structural property of the vertex construction and the WS-cell projection — in the sharpest testable form available: does the argument that justifies restricting the loop sum to the WS quarter-cube (G7's exact propagator invariance under the BCC reciprocal lattice) actually hold for the *vertex*-derived piece of the same integrand, and if not, does the obvious remedy (integrate over more of the cube) help?

## 2. G7's argument, and what it does and does not cover

F305's G7 states: "the cube $[-\pi,\pi)^3$ holds EXACTLY 4 copies of the BCC Brillouin zone... and the action's quadratic form is exactly periodic under the reciprocal lattice." This is a statement about `lpt_bcc_vertex.quadratic_form` — the propagator sector. It is what license F305/F280/F337 to treat the WS cell (one quarter of the cube, `lpt_bcc_vertex.ws_mask`) as the correct, non-redundant integration domain for `pi_loop`'s full one-loop sum — vertex and propagator terms together, since `pi_loop`'s integrand is `M / den` with `den` built from `quadratic_form` at both `k` and `k+q`.

G7's proof is a primitive-cell volume argument about the *lattice's own mode density* — a fact about how many independent link-field Fourier modes exist, established once, geometrically, and it does not depend on which quantity (propagator or vertex) is later integrated over that mode set. What it does *not* establish is that a fixed *function* of $k$ built from the vertex expansion — the numerator $M$ in `pi_loop`'s integrand — takes the *same value* at the 4 cube-points that alias to the same physical propagator mode. Those are different claims. This finding measures the second one directly, since (per F305 §7.4's own stated method, reused again here) "the machinery is action-agnostic... costs one function" to test empirically rather than assume.

## 3. Measurement 1 — the vertex kernel is not $G$-periodic

`vertex_g_periodicity` evaluates the exact per-point integrand `pi_loop` sums (`M/den`, gauge-fixed and `phys`-projected, duplicated into a new single-point helper `_axis_kernel_at_point` rather than touching the tested `pi_loop` path) at 4 fixed, deterministic, generic momenta (chosen to avoid accidental symmetry), then shifts the spatial part of the loop momentum by each of the 6 shortest BCC reciprocal-lattice vectors (`lpt_bcc_vertex._recip_vectors(rng=1)`, the same "first shell" F350 §2 independently established is the complete boundary-defining set) and compares:

| quantity shifted | worst relative deviation across all (point, $G$) pairs tested |
|---|---:|
| `quadratic_form` alone (the propagator; `control_use_propagator=True`) | $1.27\times10^{-15}$ |
| the full `M/den` kernel (`pi_loop`'s actual integrand, `phys` branch) | $2.22$ (i.e. $\sim220\%$) |

The propagator-only control reproduces G7's exact invariance to numerical noise, confirming the measurement methodology discriminates correctly rather than reporting "violated" unconditionally. The vertex kernel does not come close: an identical shift that leaves the denominator bit-for-bit unchanged changes the numerator by an $O(1)$ fraction of its own size. This is a genuine, reproducible, previously unmeasured fact about the module — not a bug in the sense of an error against a known-correct target (the module's own docstring already discloses that the underlying vertex form factors have $4\pi$, not $2\pi$, periodicity in the ordinary Cartesian sense, and F308 §3 independently found the *alternative* $\Omega_\text{even}$ propagator candidate aperiodic under exactly this class of shift), but it had not previously been checked for **this** kernel against **this specific** reciprocal lattice, the one the WS-cell domain restriction actually uses.

## 4. Measurement 2 — does integrating over the full cube fix it? No — it makes it worse

If the 4 cube-copies related by these $G$-shifts carry genuinely different vertex information (not a redundant relabelling of the same physical mode, as they are for the propagator), a natural hypothesis is that restricting the sum to just one WS quarter discards real physics the vertex sector needs, and that summing the full cube instead should recover it. `domain_choice_divergence` tests this directly: identical loop integral, only the domain weight changed from `'ws'` to `'cube'` (`lpt_d1_action_consistent`'s existing `domain` parameter — no new physics path, just an existing knob), $n=8$–$16$, both `phys` and `raw` branches:

| $n$ | $b_0$ recovery, `phys`/ws | $b_0$ recovery, `phys`/cube | $b_0$ recovery, `raw`/ws | $b_0$ recovery, `raw`/cube |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 6 | 0.2087 | 0.4159 | 0.2617 | 0.4820 |
| 8 | 0.3097 | 0.8004 | 0.3839 | 0.9169 |
| 10 | 0.3779 | 1.2299 | 0.4719 | 1.3985 |
| 12 | 0.4134 | 1.7117 | 0.5245 | 1.9383 |
| 14 | 0.4410 | 2.2590 | 0.5704 | 2.5515 |
| 16 | 0.4579 | 2.8801 | 0.6056 | 3.2477 |
| 18 | 0.4765 | 3.5792 | 0.6450 | 4.0317 |
| 20 | 0.4911 | 4.3587 | 0.6813 | 4.9060 |

(range extended to $n=6$–$20$ on review, §"Reviewed & corrected" — the original $n=8$–$16$ sweep supported the same conclusion but with a weaker "no turnover" hedge.)

`domain='cube'` does not approach 1 more cleanly than `domain='ws'` — its own $b_0$ recovery is **strictly monotonically increasing at every consecutive $n$ from 6 through 20, in both branches**, climbing straight through 1 (between $n=8$ and $n=10$) and continuing unbounded, with no sign of turning over across the tested range. This is the same qualitative divergence signature F337 measured for the `raw` (unprojected) branch under the correctly-chosen `domain='ws'` (L6 leg 2) — a clean, monotone, accelerating departure from 1, not a slower-but-eventual convergence. **Measurement, not argument, answers the question this section opened with: no, the WS-cell restriction is not dropping vertex information the full cube would recover.** Whatever the vertex's $G$-non-invariance means physically, treating it as license to expand the integration domain produces a worse estimator, not a better one.

**A note on how "diverges" is measured (added on review).** $|1-b_0\text{ recovery}|$ — the natural deviation-from-target metric, and the one F337/F350 use elsewhere in this row — is *not* monotonic through the sign crossing `domain='cube'` passes through between $n=8$ and $n=10$: it dips before climbing again, so a naive endpoint comparison of this metric over a short range starting near the crossing can misread that dip as convergence. `domain_choice_divergence`'s pass criterion therefore checks monotonicity of $b_0$ recovery itself (not its deviation from 1), which has no such ambiguity and is verified strictly increasing at every one of the 8 tested $n$ values, in both branches — see §"Reviewed & corrected".

**Control (D9).** `domain_choice_divergence(swap_control=True)` asserts the reversed claim — that `cube` is the domain required to stay bounded near 1 and `ws` the one required to diverge — and this fails on both of its own sub-conditions (`good_bounded=False`, `bad_diverges=False`), because a real measurement cannot be forced to report the wrong domain as the well-behaved one. Verified directly (§ code, `test_control_swapping_which_domain_is_asserted_good_reddens_the_leg`).

## 5. Interpretation, and what this does *not* do

**This finding does not reopen, re-decide, or extend L6.** F337's own text defines L6 as exactly two litigated legs — which object is the propagator (leg 1), and is the redundant link-axis mode dynamical (leg 2) — and domain choice (WS quarter vs. full cube) was never one of them; it is an inherited default that follows from F305's G7 rather than a question L6 adjudicated. What this finding adds is a *separate* divergence measurement, on a *separate* axis (domain, not propagator scheme or branch), that happens to land on the same side as L6's two: every alternative this session and F337 have now tested — the wrong propagator (F337 leg 1), the wrong branch (F337 leg 2), and now the wrong domain (this finding) — diverges, and the combination F337 actually decided (`domain='ws'`, `branch='phys'`, own quadratic form) is the only one that (slowly) converges. That is corroboration of the decided combination from an independent direction, phrased carefully: it is not a claim that L6 itself grew a third leg.

**This finding does not execute F337/F350's named falsification call.** F350 §5 already observed, of its *own* result, that F337's stated exhaustion condition for "non-convergence, not falsification" had fired — and stopped short of declaring the two-sided falsifier (F280 §7) triggered, instead naming the two candidates this finding and a future one should test first. This finding tests one of those two candidates and excludes it. It does not, on its own, newly trigger anything F350 had not already flagged; it removes one item from F350's list of untested explanations without adding a new open question to the ledger beyond the one that was already there ("the actual mechanism is still unidentified", F350 §6).

**What is left, per F350 §5/§7, unchanged by this finding:** the phys-branch projection's own possible role (a narrower question than "which domain" — e.g. whether the projector $P=I-\hat n\hat n^{\mathsf T}$ interacts with the vertex's $G$-non-invariance in some way not tested here) and the $g_s=\tfrac12$ monotonicity assumption itself (F350's candidate (b), a physics question, not a numerics one, and out of scope for a domain/periodicity check).

## 6. Honest scope

- **The vertex's non-$G$-periodicity is measured at 4 fixed points with one external momentum ($Q=0.9$) and the first reciprocal shell (6 of the 26 shortest vectors, the ones actually tested).** It is not a proof that no $(k,G)$ pair exists where the kernel happens to be invariant (some do — off-diagonal cancellations are generic in a finite sum), only that the invariance is not generic, in flat contradiction to what the WS-domain argument would need if it depended on vertex-sector invariance (it does not: G7's own proof never needed it, §2).
- **The `domain='cube'` divergence measurement covers $n=6$–$20$** (extended from $n=8$–$16$ on review) — cheap, deliberately, since the point was a decisive directional test, not a second native sweep. A turnover at much larger $n$ cannot be excluded by 8 points, but the signature (strictly monotone at every consecutive $n$ tested, accelerating, matching the already-characterised `raw`-branch divergence class from F337's 11-point $n=8$–$40$ ladder) makes a late turnover implausible on the same grounds F337 §3 used for `raw`. **Named falsifiability condition:** if a future sweep at $n>20$ measures `domain='cube'`'s $b_0$ recovery turning over (ceasing to increase monotonically) and settling to a deviation from 1 below `domain='ws'`'s own, that overturns this finding's exclusion of the domain-choice candidate.
- **This finding does not identify what the vertex's $G$-non-invariance *means* physically** (e.g. whether it reflects a genuine additional discrete symmetry the link-axis parametrisation carries that the Cartesian mode-counting of G7 does not see, or is simply an expected feature of a non-Bravais/multi-branch lattice PT construction with no further content) — only that it exists, is large, and does not explain d1 leg 3's slow convergence via the "wrong domain" route.
- **`make gate` was not run in full this session** (this machine is a native macOS Python 3.14 environment with system numpy/scipy, not the Linux/cp310 `.vendor` sandbox CLAUDE.md's operational notes describe — the vendored wheels do not load here). Verified individually instead: `casim index` (clean, F382 the new max), `python3 -m pytest tests/findings/test_F382_vertex_domain_periodicity.py` (7/7 PASS) and the neighbouring F337/F350/F305/F307 finding tests (no regressions — this session's only production-code change is additive, new functions only, `pi_loop`/`pi_loop_chunked`/`transverse_B_chunked` untouched).
