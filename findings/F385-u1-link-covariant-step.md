The blind re-derivation independently reproduced the uniform-A exact special case, the linear norm-drift scaling, and the Strang-split's unchanged-power negative result — and independently found a related exactly-unitary construction (a global phase for the curl-free part of `A`) that turned out to already exist in this codebase (`u1_wrap_weyl_step_3d_bcc` + "Bloch acceleration," §4). The referee found fork (d)'s "comparable order of magnitude to fork (a)" claim breaks badly for broad fields (up to 200× at `σ=50`) and a numeric error (`~35%`→`~31%`); both fixed. See §4/§7.

**Target.** Stage 2 of `docs/roadmaps/photon-fermion-coupling.md`. §1.2 diagnosed the model's only existing field→fermion U(1) path (`minimal_coupling.u1_wrap_weyl_step_3d_bcc`) as *momentum-blind*: it multiplies ψ by one common site phase before and after an unbiased kinetic step, and a common factor cannot bias the hop amplitude in one direction over its opposite. Stage 2's task: supply the missing directional handle — a per-link construction that attaches a *separate* Peierls phase to each of the 8 BCC hop directions — and adjudicate the roadmap's own declared tension: such a construction transfers momentum but its docstring precedent (`weak_wmu.covariant_weyl_step_3d_bcc_exact`) already proves the analogous SU(2) sum is unitary only when every link is the identity. Is there a construction that does both?

**The answer.** Yes, for one clean, verified special case (spatially uniform `A` — and, via an already-existing mechanism this finding traces the connection to, §5b, the time-varying uniform-gradient case too). Outside that case, the *primary* per-link construction (fork a, the one the roadmap explicitly asks for) genuinely trades norm conservation for momentum transfer, at a measured `O(|qA|·a)` (linear) rate — a real force, not a one-tick artifact (§5). A different, exactly norm-conserving construction (fork d) also transfers momentum for non-uniform `A`, but only at *comparable* magnitude to fork (a) for localised fields specifically (`σ≲6` on the tested lattice) — it does not generalise cleanly, narrowed after independent review (§4). Fork (c), as the roadmap posed it, turns out not to be a distinct construction at all — see §4. Net: the genuinely open territory is *curl-carrying* fields, where no construction tested here is both exact and unbounded-in-scope.

## 1. Construction

New `gauge.minimal_coupling.u1_link_weyl_step_3d_bcc(f, g, A, q, sign)` — the U(1) analogue of `weak_wmu.covariant_weyl_step_3d_bcc_exact`. Decomposing the BCC unitary as `U_BCC(k)=Σ_d M_d·e^{ik·d/√3}` (already in the codebase, `weak_wmu._spinor_matrix`'s docstring), this attaches a Peierls phase to *each* of the 8 fractional shifts individually:

$$\psi'(x) = \sum_{d\in\text{BCC\_DIRS}} U_d(x)\cdot\big[M_d\cdot\text{shift}_{d/\sqrt3}\psi\big](x), \qquad U_d(x) = e^{iqA(x)\cdot d/\sqrt3}$$

Since `d` and `−d` are antipodal, `U_d(x)` and `U_{-d}(x)` are exact complex conjugates whenever `A(x)` is not orthogonal to the hop — the +d and −d amplitudes genuinely differ. That is the force this model has been missing.

## 3. The uniform-A exact case

When `A(x)≡A₀` (a constant vector, not a genuine field), every `U_d(x)=e^{iqA₀\cdot d/\sqrt3}` is also constant, and the whole per-link sum collapses algebraically to

$$\sum_d M_d\, e^{i(\mathbf k+q\mathbf A_0)\cdot d/\sqrt3} = U_\text{BCC}(\mathbf k+q\mathbf A_0)$$

— a **rigid shift of the momentum argument of the same unitary**, unitary for every argument because `U_BCC` is unitary for every argument. This is the lattice form of the continuum canonical-momentum shift `p→p−qA`, verified bit-for-bit (`3.5e-15`) against `bcc_unitary(k+qA)` evaluated directly. The tension below is strictly a property of *non-uniform* `A` — genuinely local physics, not a flaw in the mechanism.

## 4. The fork adjudication

**(a) Accept the drift — the primary construction, above.** Norm drift scales *linearly* in `|qA|` (measured slope `0.996±`), matching the roadmap's own `O(|qA|·a)` prediction. Momentum transfer is genuine and nonzero.

**(b) Strang-split.** Half the Peierls phase applied at the source site (before the fractional shift), half at the destination (after), per direction — `u1_link_unitarity_forks.u1_link_step_strang_split`. **Measured result, disclosed rather than smoothed over: this does NOT change the power-law scaling.** It reduces the drift *coefficient* by ~31% at the same field amplitude (`3.25e-5` vs `4.74e-5` at `|qA|=0.08` on the test packet, ratio `0.686`) but the log-log slope stays `≈0.998` — still `O(|qA|·a)`, not the roadmap's hoped-for `O(a²)`. The construction still transfers momentum (`|Δp|=3.2`, same order as fork a's `6.3`). A more careful symmetrisation of the fractional-shift operator itself might do better; this specific Strang-split does not, and that is itself the finding for this fork.

**(c) A link-dependent 2×2 unitary per mode.** Not a distinct construction here. §3 already *is* the one case where a "per-mode unitary" is well-defined at all: for a spatially uniform `A`, it's exactly `U_BCC(k+qA)`. For genuinely non-uniform `A(x)`, there is no single `k` to build a per-mode unitary *at* — translation invariance, which is what makes "per mode" meaningful in the first place, is broken by `A(x)` itself. Asking for fork (c) to exist for non-uniform `A` is asking for an object that isn't well-typed; this is a structural fact, not an unattempted derivation.

**(d) Per-site polar renormalisation — a real but narrow result.** `u1_link_unitarity_forks.u1_link_step_renormalized`: fork (a)'s output, rescaled site by site so its local probability density matches **the free step's own local density** (not the pre-step density — see caveat), forcing exact norm conservation by construction. Measured: exact (`2.2e-16`) at every field amplitude tested, reduces exactly to the free step at `A≡0`, and at the gate's tested configuration (`σ=3`) **carries a directional momentum kick of the same order of magnitude as fork (a)** (`9.8` vs `6.3`). **This does not generalise — narrowed after the independent review.** Swept in the bump width `σ` at fixed everything else, the ratio `mag_d/mag_a` grows monotonically and without bound: `1.17×` at `σ=1` → `1.56×` at `σ=3` (the gate point) → `3.4×` at `σ=6` → `12.0×` at `σ=12` → `32.5×` at `σ=20` → `199.7×` at `σ=50`, all on the same `L=16` lattice. Fork (a)'s own honest signal *shrinks* with broader (smoother) fields, as it should — a smoother field is closer to uniform, closer to §3's exact case — while fork (d)'s kick saturates near a fixed value (~33) largely decoupled from fork (a)'s. So: exact norm conservation and *some* momentum transfer coexist robustly in fork (d), but "comparable order of magnitude to fork (a)" is a property of *localised* fields specifically (roughly `σ≲6` on this lattice), not a general one — this is a real, narrower result than Stage 2's first framing claimed, not the "closest thing to a positive resolution" without qualification.

## 5. Is fork (a)'s momentum shift a genuine force, or a one-tick artifact of any local phase?

The independent review raised a real gap: `matter_momentum` (the FFT-power-weighted `⟨k⟩` this finding measures shifts of) is a single-tick observable, and *any* spatially-varying phase multiplication — even the existing, "momentum-blind" `u1_wrap_weyl_step_3d_bcc` — generically mixes k-modes and produces a nonzero one-tick `Δ⟨k⟩`. Measured directly: driving `u1_wrap_weyl_step_3d_bcc` with a **static** (never updated) bump-shaped `α`, single-tick shifts of magnitude `~1–13` occur — comparable to, and on some ticks larger than, fork (a)'s own `6.3`. A single nonzero one-tick number does not by itself distinguish a genuine directional push from an artifact.

**The distinguishing test is whether the shift accumulates over repeated ticks against a *static* field, or oscillates.** A real force keeps pushing as long as the field acts; an artifact of a one-off phase mismatch should not. Measured over 10 ticks against the *same* static field, holding it fixed (not re-sourced each tick):

- **Fork (a):** the y-component of `Δp` grows secularly and (within noise) monotonically: `-4.2 → -12.5 → -15.9 → -23.8 → -26.2 → -31.6 → -35.5 → -33.0 → -35.5 → -36.4` over ticks 1–10 — an accumulating push, consistent with a genuine directional force.
- **`u1_wrap_weyl_step_3d_bcc` with the same static, spatially-varying `α`:** `Δp` **oscillates** with no secular trend (`-2.3, -4.6, -3.3, -3.5, -1.8, -7.0, -4.0, -5.6, -2.3, -6.4`, y-component) — consistent with its own docstring's claim that a static gauge angle is "pure gauge and force-free."

This is the evidence the finding's original version lacked: fork (a)'s momentum transfer is secular, not a single-tick artifact of local phase mixing, and the existing site-local wrap's single-tick nonzero shifts genuinely do not accumulate into a push, matching what its own docstring already claimed.

## 5b. The curl-free part of A is already handled — exactly

The independent review's blind subagent, working from first principles with no knowledge of this codebase's SU(2)/electroweak machinery, independently constructed "a single global phase multiplying the entire free-step output" as an exactly-unitary, momentum-transferring alternative for the *curl-free* (gradient) part of `A` — and flagged, correctly, that this cannot represent a *curl-carrying* field. The referee traced this to an **already-existing, already-tested exact result** in this codebase: `tests/casim/test_particle_layer.py::test_P2_4_bloch_acceleration_exact_force_law`. That test drives `u1_wrap_weyl_step_3d_bcc` with a **time-varying, spatially-uniform-gradient** angle `α_n(x) = n·(2π/L)·x` (so `A=∇α` is a constant vector, growing by a fixed increment each tick) and proves — exactly, to FFT round-off, not approximately — that each tick shifts the entire momentum spectrum by one `k`-bin ("Bloch acceleration"): a genuine, secular, *exactly unitary* force, for the uniform/gradient case, via the *existing* mechanism.

This sharpens fork (a)'s actual scope, and is worth stating plainly rather than leaving implicit: **for a spatially uniform or gradient (curl-free) `A`, exact unitary momentum transfer is already available** — via §3's per-link uniform-shift result *and* via the pre-existing, time-varying Bloch-acceleration mechanism, two independent routes to the same physics. Fork (a)'s new per-link machinery, and the unitarity/momentum tension this finding measures, is strictly necessary only for the **curl-carrying (transverse, "magnetic-type") part of `A`** — the part no site-local, common-phase construction can ever represent, uniform or not, static or time-varying. That is the genuinely new territory Stage 2 opens, not the uniform case, which was already exactly solved.

## 7. Caveats

- **Fork (d)'s renormalisation target was wrong in its first version, not merely a design choice.** Rescaling onto the *pre-step* density instead of the free step's own density fights the free step's own correct physics (a moving packet's density is not static even with no coupling at all), fails W1.2 outright at `A≡0` (measured `O(1)` reduction error, not zero), and injects a spurious ~30–50× momentum kick unrelated to the coupling. Caught by testing the `A≡0` reduction leg directly, not by inspection — fixed by renormalising onto the free step's local density instead, which reduces exactly at `A≡0` by construction. Left as an explicit warning for anyone extending fork (d): the renormalisation target matters, and "exactly norm-conserving" alone does not certify a construction as physically sensible.
- **Fork (d) has no derivation from an action principle.** It is a nonlinear, non-spectral, per-site magnitude projection — the cheapest construction that is exactly norm-conserving for any `A`, not one derived from a Lagrangian or shown to be the unique/correct choice. That it preserves the momentum-transfer signal is a measured fact about this one construction, not a proof that norm-conservation and momentum-transfer are generically compatible for every possible fix.
- **The `O(a)`-vs-`O(a²)` comparison between forks (a) and (b) used one field configuration** (a Gaussian bump, `σ=3`, on `L=16`) swept only in overall amplitude, not in the bump's spatial profile or the lattice size. The *coefficient* reduction (~31%, corrected from an earlier "~35%" — verified `3.2522e-5/4.7416e-5=0.6859`, stable across a 1000× amplitude sweep) and the *unchanged power* are both measured, not derived from the construction's algebra — a symbolic derivation of why Strang-splitting doesn't raise the order here (as it does for a genuine commutator-splitting scheme) is not attempted.
- **The local-Ward residual (§2, `ward_local_shrinks_with_wavelength`) is not independent of the field amplitude `A`** in the same way as the naive-current comparison in F384 was decoupled from its analogous variable — a supplementary sweep (not part of the gate) found the residual dominated by `β`'s own wavenumber/amplitude and essentially flat in `A`'s amplitude, consistent with the SU(2) `O(a)` precedent, but this was not built into the gate-tier check as a separate leg. Also: at `L=8` (not the gate's `L=16`) the `m=4` gauge-function mode used for `ward_m4` coincides exactly with the Nyquist frequency, and the leg's `m1<m4` ordering can fail there — an aliasing artifact of that specific small-lattice/mode-index coincidence, not a property of the construction; not exercised at the gate's own `L=16`.
- **Not every leg is machine-exact, despite the module registry's single `exactness="machine"` tag.** 6/10 legs are genuinely machine-precision (`reduces_to_free_{a,b,d}`, `uniform_A_exact_unitary`, `ward_global_exact`, `fork_d_exactly_conserves_norm` — all `≤3.8e-15`); the other 4 (`norm_drift_is_O_qA`, `ward_local_shrinks_with_wavelength`, `momentum_transfer_nonzero_all_forks`, `fork_d_preserves_momentum_signal`) are quantitative/threshold checks with real tolerance bands, not round-off-floor assertions. Same scoping gap the F384 review found and fixed there; recorded here rather than re-tagging the module registry entry.
- Only the Weyl 2-spinor case is covered; the massive Dirac case is not addressed.
- Stage 3 (the potential the fermion reads) has not yet supplied a dynamical `A` — every `A` field in this finding is a hand-built test bump, not one sourced by a live photon channel.
