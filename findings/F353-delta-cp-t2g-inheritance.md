## 2. $\delta_{CP}$ in terms of the three $T_{2g}$ amplitudes

F236/F254 build the light-neutrino Majorana mass matrix as
$$m_\nu \;=\; \mathrm{diag}(E_g\text{ texture}) \;+\; T_{2g},\qquad T_{2g}=\begin{pmatrix}0&t_{xy}&t_{zx}\\t_{xy}&0&t_{yz}\\t_{zx}&t_{yz}&0\end{pmatrix},$$
diagonalised by the Autonne–Takagi factorisation $m_\nu = U\,\mathrm{diag}(m_1,m_2,m_3)\,U^\top$, $U$ unitary, giving $U_\mathrm{PMNS}=U$ (charged leptons diagonal in this basis, F236's convention). F254 fit $(t_{xy},t_{yz},t_{zx})\in\mathbb R^3$ and read the three **angles** off $|U_{ij}|$ in the standard PDG moduli parametrisation. The **Dirac CP phase** is the remaining rephasing-invariant content of $U$ once $(t_{xy},t_{yz},t_{zx})\to$ complex: in the standard PDG convention it is carried by $U_{e3}=s_{13}e^{-i\delta_{CP}}$ (up to Majorana-phase convention choices that do not affect it), and is extracted basis-independently via the Jarlskog invariant
$$J \;=\; \operatorname{Im}\!\big(U_{e1}U_{\mu2}U_{e2}^{*}U_{\mu1}^{*}\big) \;=\; s_{12}c_{12}\,s_{13}c_{13}^2\,s_{23}c_{23}\,\sin\delta_{CP},$$
which is invariant under $U\mapsto U\,\mathrm{diag}(\text{phases})$ (any Majorana-phase-type column rephasing) and vanishes identically whenever $U$ is real. Since $(t_{xy},t_{yz},t_{zx})$ are exactly the inputs that fix $U$, $\delta_{CP}$ — via $J$ — is a specific, computable, rephasing-invariant function of those same three amplitudes' **phases** (their magnitudes alone, as F254 showed, already exhaust the angle content with zero slack). This is the "$\delta_{CP}$ explicitly in terms of the three $T_{2g}$ amplitudes" the task asks for: not a closed algebraic formula (the map $T_{2g}\to J$ goes through a $3\times3$ Takagi diagonalisation, not a one-line substitution), but a well-defined, numerically evaluable one, implemented in `casim.engine.particles.majorana.jarlskog_invariant` / `sin_delta_cp` (new, this finding).

## 3. Part A — the group-theoretic argument transfers verbatim (T1)

F254's whole argument for the angles rests on one fact: $D_{2h}=\{\mathrm{diag}(s_x,s_y,s_z):s_a=\pm1\}$ acts on $T_{2g}$ by $T\mapsto S T S^\top$, so each entry picks up the real sign $s_as_b$ — three distinct, zero-sum characters, hence three inequivalent 1-d irreps ($B_{1g},B_{2g},B_{3g}$), hence no residual symmetry relates the three amplitudes (T1), and the democratic point's stabiliser is only order 2 of 8, so F92 equipartition (which needs a *degenerate multiplet* to equalise within) cannot apply (T2).

**Nothing in that argument reads $t_{ab}\in\mathbb R$.** $S$ is a real orthogonal matrix; $(S T S^\top)_{ab}=s_as_b\,t_{ab}$ holds identically for $t_{ab}\in\mathbb C$ — multiplying a complex number by $\pm1$ flips its sign (a phase shift of exactly $0$ or $\pi$) and never rotates it to any other value. This finding's `check_complex_amplitude_transport` (T1 below) verifies this directly: for 200 random complex $(t_{xy},t_{yz},t_{zx})$ and all 8 elements of $D_{2h}$, the transported entry equals the predicted sign times the original **to machine precision** ($<10^{-10}$), and the phase shift deviates from $\{0,\pi\}$ by at most $8\times10^{-17}$.

Consequence: F254's T1 (three inequivalent irreps) and T2 (democracy unprotected; equipartition inapplicable) hold for the **phase** of each $T_{2g}$ amplitude exactly as they hold for its magnitude — no new group-theoretic work is needed, because the group never sees the phase at all. This is the formal core of the inheritance the D4 row asked to confirm.

## 4. Part B — the phase content is generic and unprotected (T2–T4)

Formal non-relation is necessary but not sufficient — F254 also needed the numerical no-go (T3: single-parameter ansätze miss the data) to rule out an accidental selector. The analogous check here: is there any special value of the $T_{2g}$ phases that the construction is secretly pinned to, making $\delta_{CP}$ not actually free in practice?

**Baseline (T2).** F254's own NuFIT-fit magnitudes $(t_{xy},t_{yz},t_{zx})=(0.848,3.431,-2.158)$ at $\delta_\nu=17.18°$ give, with those magnitudes taken as literally real (F254's own construction), $J=0$ to $3.7\times10^{-27}$ — exact machine-precision zero. This is $\delta_{CP}\in\{0,\pi\}$: CP-conserving. **This is not a prediction of the model** — it is the direct algebraic consequence of $U$ being real whenever every input is real (any real symmetric matrix has a real orthogonal eigenbasis), so it says nothing about whether CP conservation is *protected*.

**Is it protected? No (T3).** Holding those exact magnitudes fixed and drawing 1000 independent random phase triples $(\varphi_{xy},\varphi_{yz},\varphi_{zx})\sim U(0,2\pi)^3$ gives $J\in[-0.0488,+0.0487]$, std $0.029$, with **zero** of the 1000 draws landing back at $J\approx0$ (threshold $10^{-8}$). $J$ is a smooth, generic, continuously-populated function of the phases — nothing in the construction pulls it back toward the real-only value.

**Is the democratic point protected? No (T4).** The phase-analogue of F254's democracy check — $t_{xy}=t_{yz}=t_{zx}=re^{i\varphi}$, scanning $(r,\varphi)$ over 300 draws — gives $\max|J|=0.059$, generically nonzero. The order-2-of-8 stabiliser that failed to protect magnitude-democracy in F254 T2 (only $\{+\mathbb1,-\mathbb1\}$ fix the point) is the *same* stabiliser here, and it fails identically for phase-democracy.

Taken together: nothing in the $D_{2h}$ stabiliser, and no symmetric ansatz tried, singles out $\delta_{CP}=0$ (or any other value) as protected. The real-only construction sits at a CP-conserving point only because F254 chose real inputs, not because the model forces it there — confirming, not merely asserting, that $\delta_{CP}$ is free in exactly F254's sense.

## 5. Bonus — two real defects found and fixed while building this check (T5, T6)

Computing $J$ correctly for a genuinely complex $m_\nu$ required exercising `takagi_light_masses`'s complex-symmetric branch for the first time under this model's own physical mass scale, and doing so surfaced two independent, real defects — neither of which affects any existing finding, since F236 and F254 are both exclusively real and only ever use the *other* (real-symmetric) branch.

**T5 (severe — the one that matters operationally).** The pre-existing branch test read `if np.allclose(m_nu.imag, 0.0):`, called with **no explicit tolerance**, i.e. numpy's default `atol=1e-8`. This model's light-$\nu$ mass matrix entries are $\sim10^{-9}$–$10^{-20}$ in these units (GeV) — far below $10^{-8}$ in absolute terms, regardless of how large the imaginary part is *relative to the matrix's own scale*. So the test silently classified **every** physically-scaled complex $m_\nu$ as real and took the real-only branch, discarding the phase content entirely. `check_T5_branch_selection_bug` reproduces the exact pre-fix code against 50 random-phase draws at F254's own magnitudes: **all 50** take the `REAL_BRANCH`, and the resulting $J$ is exactly $0$ for every one — a spurious, input-independent null, not a genuine one. **This means any naive attempt at exactly this D4 check, before today, would have "confirmed" $\delta_{CP}=0$ always, for the wrong reason.** Fixed by comparing $\max|\operatorname{Im}(m_\nu)|$ to the matrix's own scale (`1e-10 * max|m_nu|`) rather than an absolute constant.

**T6 (minor — a hygiene fix, not a masked result).** Once T5 is fixed, the pre-existing complex branch computed $U$ as the raw eigenvectors of $m_\nu^\dagger m_\nu$ (via `eigh`), used directly. This is a mathematically incomplete Takagi factorisation: those eigenvectors equal $\overline{U_\text{true}}$ (the true Takagi $U$'s entrywise complex conjugate), a fact `check_T6_sign_convention_only` verifies directly ($J_\text{raw}=-J_\text{fixed}$ to $4\times10^{-16}$ over 30 random-phase draws). Complex-conjugating a unitary matrix flips the sign of any purely-imaginary invariant, so this reproduces every $|U_{ij}|$ (hence every PMNS angle — F236/F254 unaffected) but returns $\delta_{CP}$ with an `eigh`-implementation-dependent overall sign, and never checks $m_\nu=U\,\mathrm{diag}(m)\,U^\top$ at all. Fixed with a proper SVD-based Autonne–Takagi construction (`W,S,V^\dagger=\mathrm{svd}(m_\nu)$; $\Phi=V^\top W$ is diagonal unitary for non-degenerate $S$ — checked, not assumed; $U=W\cdot\Phi^{-1/2}$ columnwise), whose reconstruction is verified to $<10^{-11}$ (residual folded into the function's return value) rather than merely checking an auxiliary Hermitian eigenproblem.

Both fixes are confined to `majorana.py`'s complex branch; the real branch (F236/F254's only path) is byte-identical to before, and both findings' own regression tests (`test_F236_three_generation_seesaw.py`, `test_F254_t2g_pmns_selector.py`) still pass 6/6 unchanged.
