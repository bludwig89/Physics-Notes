## 1. What K7/F228/F238 left open

F223 settled the geon's **binding** ($J{=}2$ graviton–graviton D-wave, virial mass $\mu\simeq\sqrt2\,M_\text{Pl}$). F228 settled its **stability and mechanism**: the light perturbative geon is not the stable state; the stable object is the F190/F107 **one-cell Planck-mass black-hole remnant**, $M_\text{rem}=(\sqrt3/2)^{1/2}M_\text{Pl}\approx0.9306\,M_\text{Pl}\approx1.136\times10^{19}$ GeV $\approx2.03\times10^{-5}$ g, produced as an evaporation endpoint (every field-theoretic production channel is exponentially forbidden; PBH remnants are the sole route). F238 proved the resulting abundance $\Omega_\text{DM}h^2$ is **not derivable**: it requires a primordial-black-hole formation fraction $\beta(M_\text{form})$ that traces back to a small-scale curvature amplitude $\sigma(k_\text{PBH})\sim0.06$–$0.08$ — about $2\times10^6$ above the measured CMB amplitude — and the model has no inflaton/primordial-spectrum sector to fix it. K7's completeness-report residual (docs/status/completeness-2026-08-20-prompts.md) asks specifically: is this candidate's mass window already constrained or excluded by current data, and can its free abundance input be sharpened into something quantitatively falsifiable rather than merely "free"?

**On the first question: no.** $M_\text{rem}\approx2\times10^{-5}$ g sits roughly 22 orders of magnitude below the "asteroid mass window" ($10^{17}$–$10^{23}$ g) that current PBH-dark-matter reviews (e.g. arXiv:2406.03114) describe as the presently unconstrained compact-object mass range, and it is a **fixed relic mass**, not a macroscopic compact object — direct/indirect detection is already noted as "hopeless" (F223 Sec. 5) because the number density is astronomically large ($n\approx1.7\times10^{-20}\,\text{cm}^{-3}$, one particle per $(40\,\text{km})^3$) and the coupling is purely gravitational. No microlensing, dynamical, or Hawking-evaporation-gamma-ray constraint plot reaches down to a fixed $10^{-5}$ g relic; those plots bound the *progenitor* PBH mass function, not the *relic* mass. So the geon's identity is not falsified by any mass-window plot — the falsifiable structure, if any, has to come from constraints on **how** the relic is produced, i.e. on $M_\text{form}$ and the perturbation spectrum that seeds $\beta$. That reframes the search: not "is $M_\text{rem}$ excluded" but "is the F228/F238 production/abundance mechanism, taken on its own terms, consistent with current data." Two 2025 papers address exactly that mechanism and had not previously been checked against this thread.

## 2. The two external papers

**[DLS/PVL] Cheek, Ghoshal & Heurtier, arXiv:2506.16154** compiles gravitational-wave bounds on ultralight PBHs ($M_\text{form}\lesssim10^{15}$ g, i.e. evaporating before BBN) that leave Planck-mass relics. Three results are load-bearing here:

- **Eq. 2.3** — the initial collapse fraction required for relics to be all of dark matter: $\beta_{\text{relic},i}\approx5.65\times10^{-22}\,(M_\text{form}/\text{g})^{3/2}\,f_\text{PBH}$.
- **Eq. 2.6 (PVL bound)** — $\beta_i\lesssim1.4\times10^{-4}\,(M_\text{form}/10^9\,\text{g})^{-1/4}$, from the Papanikolaou–Vennin–Langlois constraint on the scalar-induced GW background sourced during an early PBH-dominated era.
- **Eq. 2.8 (DLS bound)** — $\beta_i\lesssim1.1\times10^{-6}\,(M_\text{form}/10^4\,\text{g})^{-17/24}$, from the Domènech–Lin–Sasaki bound on the GW background from PBH binary mergers before full evaporation.

Both bounds constrain the *same* initial collapse fraction $\beta_i$ that must simultaneously satisfy Eq. 2.3 for the relic scenario to supply all of $\Omega_\text{DM}$: wherever the Eq. 2.3 curve exceeds a bound curve, that $M_\text{form}$ cannot deliver $f_\text{PBH}=1$ without conflicting with an already-measured GW background limit.

**[LIGO-PSR] arXiv:2509.20533, "Gaussian Planck Relics are Ruled-Out as Dark Matter by LIGO"** asks a different question: whatever $M_\text{form}$, the collapse fraction $\beta$ has to come from *somewhere* — a small-scale curvature power spectrum $P_\mathcal{R}$ large enough to cross the collapse threshold on the required fraction of horizon patches. If that spectrum's statistics are **Gaussian** (the default assumption absent a stated non-Gaussian mechanism), the same large-amplitude fluctuations unavoidably source a second-order scalar-induced gravitational-wave background. The paper's worked case needs $P_\mathcal{R}\sim10^{-2}$–$10^{-1}$ (for their own normalization, $\beta\sim10^{-5}$ near formation temperature $T_f\sim10^{11}$ GeV) and finds the resulting $\Omega_\text{GW}\sim10^{-7}$–$10^{-6}$ exceeds the LIGO O3 bound $\Omega_\text{GW}\lesssim5\times10^{-9}$ by **1–3 orders of magnitude**. Their stated escape clause: **non-Gaussian** statistics (lognormal, power-law-tailed) can reduce the required $P_\mathcal{R}$ enough to evade the bound.

Neither paper is new to the literature relative to F238 (both are dated 2025; F238 was written 2026-07-03) — they simply had not been checked against this project's own F228/F238 numbers before this session.

## 3. Cross-check — F228's own $\beta(M_\text{form})$ formula against the external Eq. 2.3 (S1, S2)

F228 Sec. 4 gives $\beta_\text{required}(M_\text{form})$ from an independently-built entropy-conservation argument (its own $M_\text{rem}$, $T_\text{form}(M_\text{form})$, and Friedmann-era horizon-mass relation), stating $\beta\approx6.6\times10^{-15},\,6.6\times10^{-12},\,6.6\times10^{-9}$ at $M_\text{form}=10^4,\,10^6,\,10^8$ g. Reproducing that formula verbatim from F228's own stated constants (S1) matches its three stated values to $<0.3\%$. Comparing it directly against the external Eq. 2.3 (S2):

$$\frac{\beta^{\text{F228}}_\text{required}(M_\text{form})}{\beta^{\text{Eq.2.3}}_{\text{relic},i}(M_\text{form})}=11.72\quad\text{(constant to }3\times10^{-16}\text{ relative spread across all three masses).}$$

The ratio is **exactly constant** (both formulas carry the same $M_\text{form}^{3/2}$ scaling, derived independently from the same physical mechanism — entropy-conserved comoving relic number), and the two independent order-of-magnitude estimates of the *same* physical quantity agree to a single order of magnitude (factor $11.7$, itself unremarkable given F228 declared this a genuinely order-of-magnitude tier — $O(1)$ collapse-efficiency and $g_*$ conventions routinely differ by a factor of several to a few tens between independent treatments). **This is a positive, independent literature validation of F228's own declared exactness tier**, not previously available at the time F228 was written.

## 4. A formation-mass ceiling: $M_\text{form}\lesssim1.6\times10^8$ g (S3, S4)

Combining the external Eq. 2.3 with the external DLS bound (Eq. 2.8) — both from the same paper, so the comparison is internally consistent — locates the mass at which the $\beta$ required to reach $f_\text{PBH}=1$ first exceeds what the DLS gravitational-wave bound permits. A hand-rolled bisection (no scipy; D8) on $\log_{10}M_\text{form}$ gives

$$\boxed{\ M_\text{form}^\text{DLS ceiling}\approx1.61\times10^8\ \text{g}\ }\qquad\text{(PVL crossover: }1.68\times10^{11}\text{ g — DLS is tighter throughout this range).}$$

**F228's own third worked example, $M_\text{form}=10^8$ g, sits at a margin of only $2.9\times$ below this ceiling** — allowed, but not the safely-interior illustration its Sec. 4 table presented it as. The other two worked examples remain comfortably inside: $M_\text{form}=10^4$ g has margin $\sim1.9\times10^9$, and $10^6$ g has margin $\sim7.5\times10^4$. **This sharpens, and mildly corrects, F228 Sec. 4's table**: of its three illustrative formation masses, two are safely viable and the third is marginal under a GW constraint that postdates neither paper but had not been checked. The qualitative conclusion (PBH remnants are viable, un-excluded) is unchanged; the quantitative window is now bounded on one side by a named, external, checkable number rather than being open-ended.

**Caveat carried over from the source paper (added at review, 2026-09-04).** arXiv:2506.16154 itself states that the DLS bound (Eq. 2.8) assumes a **monochromatic** PBH mass function, which it explicitly flags as a simplification that "may not reflect a realistic physical scenario," and separately notes that **early PBH mergers may weaken this constraint**. The $1.61\times10^8$ g number above is therefore an order-of-magnitude ceiling against a bound that is itself acknowledged by its own authors to be mass-function-dependent, not an unconditional, all-scenarios exclusion. This does not remove the sharpening (F228's $10^8$ g example is still marginal under the bound as stated), but it means "ceiling" should be read as "the ceiling under the DLS paper's own stated assumptions," not as a model-independent wall.

## 5. A non-Gaussianity plausibility argument (S5, S6) — corrected at review, 2026-09-04

F238 Sec. 3 already computed, independently and before either external paper was checked, the small-scale amplitude $\sigma_\text{req}(M_\text{form})$ the Press–Schechter collapse fraction requires: $0.058,\,0.066,\,0.079$ at $M_\text{form}=10^4,\,10^6,\,10^8$ g (delta_c $=0.45$). Reproducing F238's own formula verbatim (S5) matches its three stated values to $<1\%$.

**This finding originally converted these to $P_\mathcal{R}$ using only the general integral relation and claimed all three values landed "squarely inside" the LIGO-excluded band. The review-finding attack pass (2026-09-04, attack 6) found this overstated and the claim is corrected here.** arXiv:2509.20533 gives *two* transfer relations: its Eq. 9, the general integral $\sigma^2\approx(16/81)P_\mathcal{R}$ (i.e. $P_\mathcal{R}\approx(81/16)\sigma^2$), and its Eq. 10, a narrow-bump approximation $\sigma^2(R_H)\approx(4/9)P_\mathcal{R}(k_c)$ (i.e. $P_\mathcal{R}\approx(9/4)\sigma^2$) — **and the paper uses Eq. 10, not Eq. 9, to compute its own headline number** ($\sigma=0.106\Rightarrow P_\mathcal{R}(k_c)\approx5.6\times10^{-2}$). The two conventions disagree by a factor $2.25$, which is large enough at these values to move the answer across the band's stated lower edge:

$$P_\mathcal{R}^\text{Eq.9 (general)}(M_\text{form}=10^4,10^6,10^8\text{ g})\approx0.017,\ 0.022,\ 0.032\qquad(3/3\text{ inside }[10^{-2},10^{-1}])$$
$$P_\mathcal{R}^\text{Eq.10 (paper's own headline convention)}(M_\text{form}=10^4,10^6,10^8\text{ g})\approx0.0077,\ 0.0100,\ 0.0141\qquad(1/3\text{ inside }[10^{-2},10^{-1}])$$

**Under the source paper's own preferred convention, two of the three points fall at or just below the band's lower edge rather than "squarely inside" it.** The honest statement is weaker than originally written: the model's own required amplitude is in the **same order of magnitude** as the regime arXiv:2509.20533 shows is LIGO-excluded for Gaussian statistics, straddling the band's edge depending on which of the paper's own two stated conventions is used — not a clean, convention-independent placement inside it.

**A second, independent weakening (attack 5, numerology).** Press–Schechter's $\beta=\tfrac12\text{erfc}(\delta_c/\sqrt2\sigma)$ compresses an enormous dynamic range in $\beta$ (F228's three examples span $10^{-15}$ to $10^{-9}$, six orders of magnitude) into a narrow range in $\sigma$ (a factor $1.4$, from $0.058$ to $0.079$) — this is generic to $\text{erfc}^{-1}$'s behaviour, not a feature of this scenario specifically. A sweep of $\beta$ over $10^{-25}$ to $10^{-2}$ (twenty-plus orders of magnitude) keeps the resulting $P_\mathcal{R}$ inside roughly $[0.009,\,0.19]$ the *entire time* — so landing "near this band" is close to guaranteed for any sub-dominant PBH fraction computed this way, not a meaningful coincidence specific to this candidate.

**Honest scope on this step (revised).** This is a same-order-of-magnitude *plausibility placement*, not a from-first-principles exclusion and not even a convention-independent "hit" — both the transfer-factor ambiguity and the erfc compression weaken how much weight it can carry. What survives: the model's own already-derived requirement (F238, unchanged) sits in the same broad amplitude regime that a real, independent 2025 LIGO result associates with Gaussian-statistics exclusion, so **if** a future model-native primordial-spectrum sector is built and shown to be Gaussian at this scale, a genuine tension with LIGO O3 is a live possibility worth checking directly (compute $\Omega_\text{GW}(k)$ from that sector's own $P_\mathcal{R}(k)$, not from this order-of-magnitude placement) — but this finding does not establish that tension quantitatively, only that it is plausible and worth naming.
