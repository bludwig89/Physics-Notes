# F358 — E13 re-examination: the Bullet-Cluster dark-source requirement survives a live 2026 MOND/QUMOND dispute, but F194's "regardless of clump shape" framing was overclaimed

**Date:** 2026-09-03 - 19:43

## Summary

Rubric row E13 (galactic-scale consistency) is `EXCLUDED`: [[F194-emergent-gravity-bullet-falsification]] falsified the model-native emergent-gravity ("dark matter without dark matter") route using the Bullet-Cluster lensing/gas offset, concluding a dark gravitating *source* is required ([[F191-dark-matter-rotation-curves-bullet]]). The reopening condition named for this row is whether that offset measurement still discriminates baryon-only modified/emergent gravity from a dark-source picture as cleanly as F194 assumed. A literature check finds that (a) the empirical offset itself has been reconfirmed and sharpened by JWST since F194, and (b) F194's own supporting argument overstated its case: a real, live 2026 dispute inside the MOND/QUMOND literature shows the "phantom density can never migrate to the offset galaxies, regardless of $a_0$, the interpolation function, or the clump shapes" claim in F194 does not hold as a strict, shape-independent statement — a published mechanism exists by which it partially can. Despite that, the *quantitative* re-adjudication of that same mechanism against the sharpest available (JWST) data, done independently by a leading MOND theorist, finds it falls short by roughly a factor of two and still requires added collisionless, galaxy-tracking mass. **Net verdict: the no-go survives.** E13 stays `EXCLUDED`; a dark source is still required; F194's bottom line is confirmed by outside adjudication, but its "topological, clump-shape-independent" phrasing should be read as narrowed, not literal.

## Physics

F194's falsifier (§"The falsifier (E4)") rests on the QUMOND phantom-density functional

$$\rho_\text{dyn}=-\frac{1}{4\pi G}\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\big(\nu(\lvert g_N\rvert/a_0)\,g_N\big),\qquad g_N=-\nabla\Phi_N[\rho_\text{baryon}],$$

and made two logically separate claims about it:

1. **[empirical anchor]** the observed lensing/dynamical mass peak sits on the collisionless galaxies, offset from the dominant baryonic gas (Clowe et al. 2006).
2. **[model-specific, theoretical]** because $\rho_\text{dyn}$ is "a local functional of the baryons," and gas dominates the toy's baryonic budget 6:1, the predicted lensing peak "can never migrate to the offset galaxies... **regardless of $a_0$, the interpolation function, or the clump shapes**." F194 stated this as a topological, geometry-independent impossibility.

This re-examination checked both against the post-F194 (and some pre-F194 but uncited) literature.

### (1) The empirical anchor: reconfirmed and sharpened, not weakened

Rihtaršič et al. (2026, *A&A*, arXiv:2601.22245), using JWST imaging and spectroscopy, redo the Bullet-Cluster strong+weak-lensing mass reconstruction and report the sub-cluster dark/lensing halo centroid offset from the brightest cluster galaxy at $4^{+3}_{-2}$ kpc — a roughly threefold tightening of the uncertainty on the offset compared with pre-JWST analyses. The paper stays entirely inside a particle-dark-matter framework (it discusses self-interaction cross-section bounds, not modified gravity) but its relevance here is that **the raw discriminating observation F194 leans on is, if anything, better measured today than when F194 was written**, not called into question.

### (2) The "regardless of clump shapes" claim: overstated

Three mutually adversarial 2026 papers, in sequence, work the exact QUMOND functional F194 uses:

- **Hernandez (2026), "A consistent MOND modelling of the Bullet Cluster,"** arXiv:2604.10811 (12 Apr 2026). Computes the QUMOND phantom density from the *combined* gas+galaxy baryon distribution (not each component in isolation) and finds that because $\rho_\text{dyn}$ is the divergence of a strongly nonlinear vector field, the **compactness/point-like geometry of the galaxy clumps versus the diffuse, extended gas** causes a disproportionate share of the phantom-density signal — Hernandez states "almost half of the total phantom matter" — to concentrate near the galaxies, despite the galaxies holding roughly a tenth of the baryonic mass. This is presented as resolving the Bullet-Cluster tension for QUMOND without any added mass.
- **Famaey (2026), "On the residual missing mass of the Bullet Cluster,"** arXiv:2605.10022 (11 May 2026). Re-runs essentially the same class of calculation against the same updated (JWST-based) lensing map and finds the mechanism real but **quantitatively insufficient**: MOND-only convergence between/around the galaxies reaches only $\kappa\sim0.5$ against an observed $\kappa\gtrsim1$, even after doubling assumed galaxy masses. Famaey concludes a residual $\sim3.4\times10^{14}\,M_\odot$ of additional mass — comparable to the cluster's baryonic content, concentrated on the galaxies, and "mostly collisionless" — is still required. This is the same qualitative conclusion reached for MOND at the Bullet Cluster twenty years earlier (Angus, Famaey & Zhao 2007, *MNRAS* 375, 29, requiring $\sim2$ eV sterile neutrinos; Angus 2008/2009 follow-ups finding even that patch insufficient) — updated for 2026 data, not a new result in kind.
- **Zhang et al. (2026), "Baryonic mass budgets in the central regions of the Bullet Cluster and their consistency with strong lensing in MOND,"** *Phys. Rev. D* (arXiv:2606.19454, accepted; built on Cha et al.'s JWST lens model). Proposes closing Famaey's residual with *additional invisible baryonic* mass — stellar remnants (white dwarfs, neutron stars, black holes) implied by a top-heavy integrated galaxy-wide initial mass function (IGIMF) in massive cluster ellipticals — reporting this budget "encompasses" the MOND-required strong-lensing mass in the cluster core, or per the popular-press figure, could "reduce the amount of dark matter required by half, at least." Independent commentators quoted in the *Physics World* coverage (Richard Massey, Durham; Kathy Romer, Sussex) note the top-heavy-IGIMF stellar-remnant budget is an **extrapolation that cannot be independently verified in external galaxies** and is not obviously required over standard IMF assumptions.

The Hernandez mechanism is a genuine, published counterexample to F194's literal claim: **clump shape is exactly the lever it pulls**, and F194's own toy model (smooth, roughly Gaussian clumps; see F194 §"The falsifier") did not explore the compact/point-mass regime that produces the effect. The strong form of F194's claim — "regardless of... the clump shapes" — is therefore not correct as stated and should be withdrawn.

### Net effect on the discriminator

What survives is the *weaker*, quantitative form of F191/F194's conclusion, and it survives by outside adjudication rather than by this model's own toy calculation: the MOND/QUMOND community's own leading specialists, arguing with each other and using the sharpest available data, converge on **baryons-only (tuned via $a_0$, the interpolation function, *or* clump geometry) does not reproduce the observed Bullet-Cluster lensing map; some additional, collisionless, galaxy-tracking mass component is required.** Zhang's proposed fix trades an exotic particle for an extrapolated, disputed invisible *baryonic* component — a different move from "no dark source needed," and one that independent reviewers have not accepted. Ethan Siegel's contemporaneous rebuttal (Big Think, 2026) adds an independent, DM-side data point: over 80% of the reconstructed cluster mass sits in the extended weak-lensing field rather than concentrated at the galaxies, and dozens of independently studied bullet-like merging-cluster systems now show consistent offset patterns — the discriminator is no longer resting on one object even if this row's citation (Clowe et al. 2006) still names only the original.
