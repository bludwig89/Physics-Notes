## 2. Step 0 — the stability gate (this is the load-bearing result)

**A light perturbative geon is not stable as spin-2 (G0).** The $J=2$ state needs orbital $L=2$, whose lowest principal quantum number is $n=L+1=3$; the true $1/r$ ground state is $n=1$ ($L=0$, $J=0$). So the perturbative $J=2$ geon is an **excited D-wave**, not a ground state: in the light ($m\ll M_\text{Pl}$) Coulomb regime it radiatively **cascades** $n{=}3,L{=}2\to n{=}2,L{=}1\to n{=}1,L{=}0$ by graviton emission ($\Delta L=\pm1$), ending on the $J=0$ scalar. It is therefore not stable *as* spin-2 in that regime — and it is not self-bound there anyway ($E_b/mc^2=(m/M_\text{Pl})^4/36$, e.g. $2.8\times10^{-14}$ at $m=10^{-3}M_\text{Pl}$). This forces the stable dark-matter candidate to be the **Planckian** object, where the Coulomb-atom level picture is superseded by the fact that the object sits at its own Schwarzschild radius — i.e. it is a Planck-mass black hole.

**The Planckian object is stable as a one-cell remnant (G1).** A Planck-mass hole has Hawking temperature $T_H\sim M_\text{Pl}$ and lifetime $\tau\sim(M/M_\text{Pl})^3\,t_\text{Pl}\sim t_\text{Pl}$ (F183, $\tau\propto M^3$). Naively it evaporates in a Planck time — the fatal channel. **What saves it is the F190/F107 lattice structure.** A Schwarzschild horizon has area $A=16\pi(M/M_\text{Pl})^2\ell_P^2$, and F190 tiles it with F107 cells of area $a^2=8\pi\sqrt3\,\ell_P^2$, so the cell count is

$$N=\frac{A}{a^2}=\frac{2}{\sqrt3}\Big(\frac{M}{M_\text{Pl}}\Big)^2 .$$

Evaporation cannot shrink the horizon below **one** cell ($S=A/4$ requires $N\ge1$; fewer than one cell is geometrically impossible on the lattice). Setting $N=1$ fixes an absolutely-stable remnant mass in closed form,

$$\boxed{\ M_\text{rem}=\Big(\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\Big)^{1/2}M_\text{Pl}=\frac{3^{1/4}}{\sqrt2}\,M_\text{Pl}\approx0.9306\,M_\text{Pl}\approx1.14\times10^{19}\ \text{GeV}.\ }$$

Any heavier hole evaporates *down* to this one-cell remnant in $\sim t_\text{Pl}$ and then **halts** — there is no lighter horizon-bearing state to decay into, and it cannot tile fewer than one cell. **Verdict: the stability gate PASSES.** The geon is stable, not as a perturbative spin-2 atom, but as the F190/F107 one-cell Planck-mass black-hole remnant. This is exact-algebraic (it follows from the F190 area law and the F107 cell), and it decides the ontology in Step 3.
