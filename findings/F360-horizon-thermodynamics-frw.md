## 1. What the rubric row actually asks, and what F284 does not supply

Rubric row K1 grades FRW background `QUANT`, with the evidence column stating plainly: *"solved WITH the model's source, not derived FROM the lattice."* Re-reading F182/F188/F284 line by line:

- **F182/F188** take the induced Einstein equation $G_{\mu\nu}=(8\pi G/c^4)T_{\mu\nu}$ (F178, itself adopted by general covariance + neutron-star phenomenology, not re-derived from lattice dynamics in the strong/nonlinear regime) as an axiom, impose the flat FRW metric ansatz, plug in the model's own $T_{\mu\nu}$ content (full tensor, pressure-weighted), and **solve**. This is exactly what the row's evidence column names. Confirmed by reading the actual module (`src/casim/engine/interactions/cosmology.py`): `hubble()` *is* `H = sqrt((8 pi G/3) rho)` — Friedmann I is the input, not an output.
- **F284** does not change this. It re-reads *what is expanding* (the lattice is rigid; expansion is $K$'s homogeneous/conformal mode) and derives one sharp new prediction ($\dot G/G\equiv0$), but its own §7 verdict is explicit: *"FRW is reproduced exactly (F182/F188 unchanged)"* — it is an **ontological** reading of an unchanged **dynamical law**. F284 does not derive the Friedmann *equations themselves* from any lattice update rule; it explains which lattice object ($K$) the already-solved equations describe.

## 2. Why "just extend the lattice's own dynamical law nonlinearly" is not available

The model *does* have a genuinely lattice-native (not GR-imported) dynamical equation for the gravitational field: F180's

$$\Big(\nabla^2-\tfrac1{c_\text{lat}^2}\partial_t^2\Big)\ln K = -\tfrac{8\pi G}{c^4}T^{00}\qquad\text{(D-GW)},$$

derived leg-by-leg from the rotation-rate mechanics (F26), the zero-tree-stiffness theorem (F79), and the constituent-invariant structure of the induced self-energy loop — no covariant field equation posited. But D-GW is explicitly flagged (F180 §5, "Honest scope") as the **linearised, scalar/conformal-mode** reduction of the induced Einstein equation: it was obtained by linearising the induced self-energy around flat space to get a **quadratic-in-fluctuation** kinetic term. Applying it naively in its linear form to the homogeneous cosmological mode (where $\ln K$ ranges over many orders of magnitude across cosmic history, not a small fluctuation) is not licensed by its own derivation — a linear wave equation cannot, by construction, reproduce Friedmann I's *nonlinear* $H^2\propto\rho$ relation (checked directly: the naive homogeneous limit of D-GW gives a linear second-order ODE for $\ln K(t)$, not a first-order quadratic constraint). Closing K1 this way would require redoing the induced-self-energy loop **nonlinearly**, for a spatially-uniform background $K$ rather than a small fluctuation — a nonperturbative Sakharov-type calculation that is not attempted here and is named in §7 as the harder, more direct alternative to the route actually taken.

## 3. The route taken: horizon thermodynamics (Jacobson 1995 / Cai-Kim 2005)

A different, independently well-established literature route replaces "posit the covariant field equation, solve it" with three more primitive, more locally-motivated ingredients applied at the cosmological **apparent (Hubble) horizon** $\tilde r_A=1/H$ of the model's own emergent FRW metric:

1. **A horizon temperature set by local causal structure** — the Hayward–Kodama surface gravity $\kappa$, giving $T=|\kappa|/2\pi$. On this lattice the causal structure at *any* horizon is the same light cone that already governs everything else ($c_\text{grav}=c_\text{lat}$, F180) — no new input.
2. **A horizon entropy** $S=A/(4G)$, with $G$ the model's own **induced** (Sakharov-type, F79) constant — not an externally supplied Newton's constant.
3. **The Clausius relation** $\delta Q = T\,dS$, applied to the energy flux $-dE=A(\rho+p)H\tilde r_A\,dt$ crossing the horizon — the least theory-specific ingredient in the whole argument, and the one where F178's full-tensor ($\rho+p$, Tolman-weighted) source enters *as the physical heat flux*, not as an assumed field-equation source.

**This never writes down or solves $G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}$.** Jacobson's own 1995 framing is exactly this: the field equation is derived as a thermodynamic *equation of state*, not assumed and then shown to imply thermodynamics.

### The computation (sympy-exact, `verify_quasistatic()`)

With the "quasi-static" convention $\kappa\simeq-1/\tilde r_A$ (horizon radius treated as slowly varying over one Clausius step — the standard simplification in this literature), $\delta Q=T\,dS$ gives directly

$$\dot H = -4\pi G(\rho+p)\qquad\text{(Friedmann II)},$$

verified as an **exact** sympy identity (residual $=0$ after substitution). Integrating with the continuity equation $\dot\rho+3H(\rho+p)=0$ (an independent statement of local energy-momentum conservation, not itself part of the field equation) gives

$$\frac{d(H^2)}{dt}=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\dot\rho\ \Longrightarrow\ H^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho\qquad\text{(Friedmann I, exact match, boundary condition }H\to0\text{ as }\rho\to0).$$

**These are F182/F188's own equations, exactly** (`cosmology.py`'s `hubble()` and `accel_over_a()`), now obtained from horizon thermodynamics rather than posited as the field equation to solve.

## 5. What this genuinely adds

- **A second, independent derivation route to F182/F188's exact Friedmann pair**, from ingredients that are more local and more directly tied to the model's own structure (its light cone, its induced $G$, a general thermodynamic principle) than "assume the metric obeys a global covariant PDE." This is a real alternative to "solve GR with the model's source" in the specific and limited sense that the covariant field equation itself is never invoked.
- **F178's pressure-gravitates decision is now doing double duty.** The $\rho+3p$ (Tolman) weighting was previously justified in F182 by the Bianchi identity applied to an already-posited field equation. Here it appears independently, as the physical heat flux $(\rho+p)$ crossing a horizon — the *same* combination, reached by an unrelated argument. That the two routes agree is a nontrivial cross-check, not a restatement.
- **A clean, falsifiable, quantitative honesty check (Q2)** that a casual reading of this literature would skip: the "quasi-static horizon" step usually presented as an obviously-small approximation is shown here, in closed form, to be an $O(1)$ convention for ordinary matter and radiation. Anyone citing the simple Cai-Kim argument as a controlled expansion for this (or any) FRW cosmology should know this.

## 6. What this does not close — the residual, sharpened

1. **The FRW symmetry ansatz (homogeneity/isotropy) is still external input**, exactly as in F182/F188 and in essentially all of cosmology. F284 already gives this a lattice-native reading (a globally-specified initial state, §6 of that finding); this finding changes nothing here and does not claim to.
2. **The horizon-thermodynamics ingredients are themselves imported continuum semiclassical-gravity constructs.** The Hayward–Kodama surface gravity and the Unruh-type temperature it defines have not been independently re-derived from this lattice's own microphysics — the same character of import F61 flagged for the $R/4$ Lichnerowicz curvature coupling in the $G$-derivation. This finding does not close that; it inherits it, honestly, as a named limit.
3. **The entropy coefficient is exactly the live, unresolved F355 tension.** $S=A/4G$ needs a specific per-cell entropy; F190 shows what number is required ($2\pi\sqrt3$ nats), and F355 — written the same day as this finding — shows the model's own most direct lattice-native attempt to compute it (BCC vacuum entanglement entropy) disagrees with F79's induced-$G$ value by a bracketed factor $[0.79,3.18]$, hostage to an undecided fermion-doubling convention. Plugging "the model's own" numbers into this finding's derivation inherits that same unresolved tension; it is not new, and this finding does not attempt to resolve it.
4. **The fully exact treatment splits into two, and only the easier half is closed.** There are two distinct "go beyond quasi-static" routes in this literature, and this finding initially conflated them (corrected 2026-09-04, §11): (a) keep the simple energy-flux/Clausius pairing but use the **exact** Hayward–Kodama $\kappa$ (no $\kappa\simeq-1/\tilde r_A$ substitution) — this closes cleanly with a consistent sign convention and is exactly what `quantify_approximation()` now implements (§4, Q2): it reproduces Friedmann II at leading order and gives the $-3(1+w)/4$ correction as a genuine, asserted derivation, not an approximation-free "exact Friedmann" result — the quadratic-in-$\dot H$ correction is still there, it is simply now correctly computed and honestly reported rather than dropped without comment. (b) The literature's actual claim to remove the approximation **entirely** uses the harder **unified first law** (Akbar & Cai, *Phys. Rev. D* **75**, 084003 (2007)): the Misner–Sharp energy plus a work term $W=(\rho-p)/2$, replacing the simple energy-flux pairing outright. Reconstructing **that** specific (harder) route independently here produced a residual that did not reduce to Friedmann II with a consistent sign once Friedmann I was imposed on-shell, and remains unresolved. **This is reported as a genuine gap, not silently dropped**: getting the unified-first-law (Misner–Sharp + work-term) version right is named in §7 as the natural next step, and no claim is made about it here.
5. **Jacobson's original argument is stronger than the version used, and reproducing that stronger version is the real target.** Jacobson (1995) works with *local* Rindler causal horizons at every spacetime point, in every null direction, deriving the *full* Einstein equation (not the FRW-restricted, global-apparent-horizon shadow of it used here) as a local equation of state. Cai–Kim's argument is the cosmological specialisation of that idea to one particular (global, apparent, non-local) horizon. Whether the model's own light-cone/entropy machinery can support the fully *local* Jacobson construction — which would be the genuinely stronger, more clearly "derived from the lattice, not merely the metric" result — is not attempted here and is the well-posed harder problem this finding hands on.

## 7. Verdict and grade

**K1 stays at `QUANT`.** This finding does not promote it, for the reasons in §6 — most decisively, the entropy-coefficient input (§6.3) is presently *disputed by the model's own two computations of it* (F79 vs F355), so no claim about the model's own numbers closing this loop would survive that tension being named. What changes is the *shape* of the residual:

- The vague "solved with the source, not derived from the lattice" is now split into two precisely-named, independently-tractable open problems: (a) a nonperturbative/nonlinear re-derivation of the induced gravitational dynamics directly from the lattice loop (§2, extending F79/F180's own technology beyond its flagged linear/weak-field scope), or (b) a fully local (Jacobson-style, not the FRW-global-apparent-horizon shortcut) horizon-thermodynamics derivation, built on lattice-native (not continuum-imported) temperature and entropy once F355's tension is resolved (§6.5).
- A genuine, independently-verified second derivation route to F182/F188's exact equations now exists and is cross-referenced from F284's provenance table, alongside a quantified, closed-form honesty check (§4, Q2) that the standard literature's usual approximation step is not automatically small for this model's own matter content.

Future work attempting to promote K1 should start from §6.4–§6.5 (get the unified-first-law signs right; resolve F355 before trusting a numeric entropy input) rather than re-attempting the simple quasi-static shortcut, which is now closed off as a genuine promotion route (its exactness rests on a convention, not a controlled expansion, per Q2).
