## Route 1 — the vacuum is forced to condense (Nielsen–Olesen / Savvidy)

### CC1 — the exact growing mode (sympy, Tier 1)

Linearise the full Yang–Mills equation of motion $\big(\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu,a} + g\epsilon^{abc}A^b_\mu F^{\mu\nu,c} = 0\big)$ around a constant chromomagnetic background $\bar F^3_{xy} = B$ (symmetric gauge). The charged (off-diagonal) gluon with **spin aligned** along $B$ and the lowest-Landau-level profile is an exact solution that *grows*:

$$W_\mu(t,x,y) = e^{\gamma t}\, e^{-gB(x^2+y^2)/4}\,\varepsilon_\mu,\qquad \varepsilon \propto (0,1,i,0),\qquad \boxed{\;\gamma^2 = +gB\;}$$

verified symbolically: the linearised EOM closes **exactly** at $\gamma^2 = gB$ (all 12 components), fails at any other dispersion (non-vacuous), and the anti-aligned polarisation does not grow.

The control experiment isolates the cause: a charged **scalar** (minimal coupling, no spin term) on the same Landau profile has $\omega^2 = +gB > 0$ — stable. The $2gB$ difference is the anomalous chromomagnetic moment (gyromagnetic factor 2) generated by $\epsilon^{abc}$: **the instability comes from the same structure constants F43 implements.** In F26 language: $\Omega^2 = -gB < 0$ means this mode's $(\mathbf E,\mathbf B)$ rotation rate is *imaginary* — the pair does not rotate, it grows. A vacuum whose rotation rule cannot rotate is not the vacuum.

### CC2 — condensation is energetically favoured (sympy, Tier 1)

Hurwitz-zeta regularisation of the charged-vector Landau tower $M^2_{n,s} = gB(2n+1+2s)$, using $\zeta_H(-1,\tfrac32) = \zeta_H(-1,-\tfrac12) = -\tfrac{11}{24}$ (so the spin sum is $-\tfrac{11}{6}$ exactly):

$$\mathrm{Re}\,\Delta V(B) = \frac{11}{96\pi^2}\,g^2B^2\left[\ln\frac{gB}{\mu^2} - \frac12\right]\qquad\text{(SU(2), exact coefficient)}$$

The log coefficient $+\tfrac{11}{96\pi^2}$ is the asymptotic-freedom coefficient ($= \beta_0/4$ with $\beta_0 = \tfrac{11N}{48\pi^2}$, $N{=}2$). Consequently $V(B) = \tfrac{B^2}{2} + \Delta V(B)$ has the closed-form minimum

$$B_\text{min} = \frac{\mu^2}{g}\,e^{-1/(2cg^2)},\qquad V(B_\text{min}) = -\frac{c\,g^2 B_\text{min}^2}{2} < 0,\qquad c = \frac{11}{96\pi^2},$$

**for every $g>0$** — the trivial vacuum is never the ground state. The tachyonic level also contributes $\mathrm{Im}\,V = g^2B^2/16\pi > 0$ in our conventions (vacuum decay; same object as CC1's growth rate).

## Honest scope / open items

- **CC1/CC2 are continuum SU(2) statements.** The instability and the Savvidy minimum are derived in the continuum around a constant Abelian background ($\epsilon^{abc}$ = the SU(2) reduction of F43's $f^{abc}$; any SU(3) background picks out such SU(2) directions). The lattice versions (CC3/CC4) are quantitative, not exact.
- **The Savvidy state itself is not the final vacuum.** CC1's tachyon destabilises the *constant-B* state too (the known "Copenhagen spaghetti" refinement): the true vacuum is a disordered condensate of domains. F88 establishes that the trivial vacuum decays toward a condensed one — not the detailed structure of the endpoint. That endpoint is exactly what F86 parametrises as $\varepsilon_c(x)$.
- **Route 2 quantitative steps are 3D compact U(1).** Polyakov's chain ($\kappa = \beta/4\pi^2$, dilute gas $z = \rho/2$) is the standard Abelian-projected/dimensionally-reduced proxy; the full 3+1D SU(3) monopole MC (Option A machinery) is not built here. CC7 only certifies that the model's SU(3) links carry Abelian-projected monopoles at strong coupling.
- **$\mathrm{Im}\,V = g^2B^2/16\pi$ is stated in our conventions** (single charged vector pair, principal-branch treatment of the tachyonic level); literature values quoted as $g^2B^2/8\pi$ differ by counting/normalisation conventions. The sign (positive = decay) and the parametric form are the physics claims.
- **Naive Cartan projection in CC7.** $\theta_i = \arg U_{ii}$, not maximal-Abelian-gauge projection; adequate for the existence/quantisation statement, not for quantitative SU(3) monopole densities.
